[AGENT]: For the RTL path specifically, pre-allocating a DMA-safe buffer (or small pool) per RX path is the safer fix. The chip DMA-writes straight into `g_rx_buffer`, so anything you hand off upstream needs to be in stable, non-stack storage. Grabbing per-CPU heap buffers still risks ending up on a scheduler/idle stack during interrupts, and you’d have to pin them manually to keep the NIC from DMA’ing into freed memory. Instead, carve out a bounce buffer (or a few) once at init—aligned, page-backed, and large enough for the max frame—and reuse it every time the ISR wants to hand a packet to the network stack. That keeps all copies off the idle stack and avoids worrying about per-CPU heap lifetime or allocator reentrancy inside an interrupt.
