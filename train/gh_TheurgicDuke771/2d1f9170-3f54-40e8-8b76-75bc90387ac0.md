Both correct — with one useful distinction on the Terraform side. **Terraform for Airflow — yes, but it's two different layers:** 1. **Provisioning the Airflow runtime** (what you'd normally mean) — Terraform manages the infra Airflow runs *on*: - A managed service: **Azure Managed Airflow** (it's a feature inside ADF — fitting, since you're already in the Azure/ADF world), AWS MWAA, or GCP Composer. - Or self-hosted compute: AKS / Container Apps / a VM, then Helm or compose on top. 2. **Managing Airflow objects** — there's also an Airflow **REST-API Terraform provider** that manages *connections, variables, pools* inside a running Airflow. That's config-as-code, not provisioning. Usually overkill for a demo harness. So yes, Terraform covers Airflow — just be clear whether you're standing up the *engine* or configuring its *contents*. **Airflow locally via Docker — yes, and for your harness it's the easier path.** Apache ships an official `apache/airflow` image with a published quickstart `docker-compose.yaml` (webserver + scheduler + Postgres metadata DB + optional Celery workers). For a local demo that's lighter than provisioning Managed Airflow, and **Terraform isn't needed for it** — docker-compose is the norm. (Terraform's `kreuzwerker/docker` provider *can* manage local containers, but it's unnecessary friction for dev.) **A natural split for your harness, then:** - **Local / fast iteration:** Airflow via docker-compose, ADLS/Snowflake/UC pointed at real cloud accounts. - **Cloud / realistic:** Terraform stands up Azure Managed Airflow (or AKS-hosted) alongside the rest of the infra. None of this touches the DataQ repo — Airflow stays an `OrchestrationProvider` behind the abstraction (ADR 0004); DataQ neither knows nor cares whether it's Dockerized locally or Terraform-provisioned in Azure.
