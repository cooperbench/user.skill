344	## Post-v1 roadmap (v1.x)
345	
346	> v1 ships **GX-only, rule-authored, observe-and-alert**. The themes below are ordered
347	> roughly by leverage. **Theme A (Auto-monitors)** and **Theme B (Enforcement)** are the
348	> leap from "GX runner with a dashboard" to a standalone DQ platform — most real
349	> incidents are freshness/volume, not value-level, and v1 can observe but not *gate*.
350	> Nearly everything here is **additive**, because the expensive decisions are seamed in
351	> v1: `check.kind` + `metric_value` (Week 3, ADR `0012`), `ResultPublisher` + generic
352	> runner dispatch (ADR 0011), `engine: gx | dqx` (ADR 0003), orchestration abstraction
353	> (ADR 0004). Promote an item into a numbered week only when it's scheduled.
354	
355	### Theme A — Auto-monitors (rule-free coverage; the platform leap)
356	*Each is a new `check.kind` slotting into the Week-3 seam + `metric_value` column; no schema rewrite. Delivers coverage without a hand-authored expectation per table.*
357	- Freshness monitor — `kind=freshness`: last-loaded timestamp / `max(updated_at)` vs an expected interval (warehouse + UC + flat-file)

> AGENT

Both correct — with one useful distinction on the Terraform side.

**Terraform for Airflow — yes, but it's two different layers:**

1. **Provisioning the Airflow runtime** (what you'd normally mean) — Terraform manages the infra Airflow runs *on*:
   - A managed service: **Azure Managed Airflow** (it's a feature inside ADF — fitting, since you're already in the Azure/ADF world), AWS MWAA, or GCP Composer.
   - Or self-hosted compute: AKS / Container Apps / a VM, then Helm or compose on top.
2. **Managing Airflow objects** — there's also an Airflow **REST-API Terraform provider** that manages *connections, variables, pools* inside a running Airflow. That's config-as-code, not provisioning. Usually overkill for a demo harness.

So yes, Terraform covers Airflow — just be clear whether you're standing up the *engine* or configuring its *contents*.

**Airflow locally via Docker — yes, and for your harness it's the easier path.** Apache ships an official `apache/airflow` image with a published quickstart `docker-compose.yaml` (webserver + scheduler + Postgres metadata DB + optional Celery workers). For a local demo that's lighter than provisioning Managed Airflow, and **Terraform isn't needed for it** — docker-compose is the norm. (Terraform's `kreuzwerker/docker` provider *can* manage local containers, but it's unnecessary friction for dev.)

**A natural split for your harness, then:**
- **Local / fast iteration:** Airflow via docker-compose, ADLS/Snowflake/UC pointed at real cloud accounts.
- **Cloud / realistic:** Terraform stands up Azure Managed Airflow (or AKS-hosted) alongside the rest of the infra.

None of this touches the DataQ repo — Airflow stays an `OrchestrationProvider` behind the abstraction (ADR 0004); DataQ neither knows nor cares whether it's Dockerized locally or Terraform-provisioned in Azure. It all lives in the external harness per ADR 0021.
