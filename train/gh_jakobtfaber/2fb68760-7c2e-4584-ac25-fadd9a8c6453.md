14	
15	| Surface | Size | Right tool |
16	|---------|------|-----------|
17	| Batch results DB | ~24 rows (12 bursts × 2 telescopes) | hvPlot / HoloViews — tiny, interactive is free |

- **Best-guess (point estimate) rule:** 0.46 < 0.51, so most-likely in front → keep. (~81% probability it's truly foreground.)
- **Strict rule:** require even the pessimistic +1σ edge, 0.46 + 0.057 = 0.517, to be below 0.51. It isn't (0.517 > 0.51) → not confident enough → drop.

Same galaxies, different confidence bar. Point estimate keeps Wilhelm's 2; strict drops them.

**Why velocity (Q2 — applies to Zach).**
Zach's candidates are *spectroscopic* redshifts (precise, from GLADE+), at z = 0.0407 and 0.0427, just barely in front of the FRB at z = 0.043. "Barely in front" is ambiguous: a galaxy at almost the same redshift as the FRB could be a **genuine separate galaxy in front**, OR just a **neighbor of the FRB's own host** — same group, slightly different motion. Galaxies in a group swarm around with random velocities of a few hundred km/s, so a tiny redshift gap might be nothing but that swarm motion, not real foreground distance.

Converting the redshift gap to a velocity (Δv = c·Δz/(1+z)) tells you which it is:
- Zach-b: Δv = **76 km/s** → smaller than group swarm motion → probably just a host neighbor, *not* a clean foreground screen → drop.
- Zach-a: Δv = **667 km/s** → bigger than typical group motion → genuinely a separate galaxy in front → keep.

The "window" is where you draw that line. 500 km/s ≈ the edge of normal group velocity spread. So velocity is just a precise way to ask "is this really *in front* of the FRB, or basically *at* the FRB sitting next to its host?"
