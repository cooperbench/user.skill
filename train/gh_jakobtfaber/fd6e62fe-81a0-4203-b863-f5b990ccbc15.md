How does the game marginal reconstruction work? Is it just a weighting per channel that's imposed after the fit?

> AGENT

Acronyms: MAP — Maximum A Posteriori (the mode/peak of the posterior) Short answer: **during the fit it's not a weighting at all — the per-channel amplitudes are integrated out (marginalized). In my figure they're computed after, as the conditional MAP at the median shape — the model's own posterior, not an imposed fudge.** The mechanics (in `scattering/scat_analysis/burstfit_joint.py:190`, `_gain_marginal_multi_band`): **In the likelihood.** Each component *n* contributes a fixed-shape kernel `K_n(f,t)` — the scattered sub-pulse at channel *f*, set by that component's `t0_n, zeta_n` and the **shared** `τ·ν^−α` tail (`burstfit_joint.py:246`). The data model per channel is linear in unknown amplitudes: ``` d(f,t) = Σ_n g_{f,n} · K_n(f,t) + noise ``` The `g_{f,n}` (the "gains") are a free amplitude per channel per component = the burst's spectrum × scintillation × component brightness. They get a Gaussian prior `g ~ N(0, s2·I)`. Because the model is linear in `g` and noise is Gaussian, that integral is **analytic** — so at every likelihood call the gains are integrated out and you get a closed-form per-channel marginal evidence (`burstfit_joint.py:207`). The nested sampler therefore only explores the *shape* parameters (`τ, α, t0_n, zeta_n, δDM`); the amplitudes are never sampled. That's the whole point: the shared scattering law is constrained while the per-channel spectrum is free. **The `s2` and the Occam term.** `s2` is the gain-prior variance — profiled (empirical-Bayes) by default, or fixed for the clean ladder. The `−½ ln det(I + (s2/σ²)M)` term grows with component count, which is the model-selection penalty (and the lever the fixed-s2 sign-flip test probes). **In my reconstruction (the overlay/2D figures).** Marginalizing destroys the point values, so to *draw* a model I evaluate the gains at their conditional MAP given the median shape — the ridge/Wiener solution (`burstfit_joint.py:295`): ``` g_f = (M_f + (σ²/s2)·I)^−1 b_f , model(f,t) = Σ_n g_{f,n} K_n(f,t) …
