> AGENT

1. **`transition_to` before speech is a footgun.** It adds another model/tool roundtrip exactly where latency is tight, and it asks Haiku to remember sequencing under pressure. Some turns will transition without a coherent utterance; others will speak from the old node. Tool-call loops are less likely than silent non-transition or over-transition.

Use one required structured emission instead:

```json
emit_live_turn({
  "utterance": "...",
  "route": {"current_node_id": "...", "reason": "..."},
  "coverage": [{"node_id": "...", "status": "covered", "summary": "..."}],
  "state_patch": {...}
})
```

The orchestrator validates and applies this atomically before TTS. Coherence comes from one object, not procedural sequencing.

2. For sub-second Haiku, the 9-tool surface is too wide.

Haiku will use reasonably well: `transition_to`, `mark_node_covered`, `note_fact`.

It will underuse or misuse: `skip_node`, `add_dynamic_node`, `add_open_thread`, `close_open_thread`, `undo_last_coverage`. These require bookkeeping discipline Haiku will not reliably maintain in a live loop.

Merge:
- `transition_to` + `mark_node_covered` + `skip_node` into `route_update`.
- `note_fact` + `set_state_field` + open-thread changes into `state_patch`.
- Drop `undo_last_coverage` from Haiku. Let UI/admin or synthesis correct it.

3. The prep JSON is too prose-heavy for routing. `exit_hints` as English is bad agent ergonomics. Haiku needs explicit adjacency and coverage criteria.

Add per node:
- `title`
- `intent`
- `ask`
- `done_when`
- `next_node_ids`
- `priority`
- `speaker_scope`: `primary | partner | both`
- `coverage_evidence_required`: `explicit_answer | emotional_shift | concrete_decision | blocker_named`

Strip or shrink:
- Long `summary`
- Prose `exit_hints`
- Large `follow_ups`
- `parent` as primary navigation, since this is a graph

Keep the cached prep compact and route-shaped.

4. Last N transcript turns should be **6-10 turns or about 90 seconds**, whichever is smaller. More than that slows decisions and invites recency confusion.

Missing context:
- Last emitted bot utterance
- Last route decision/tool result
- Covered summaries for completed nodes
- Open threads, capped and ordered by recency
- A compact topic progress table: `must pending`, `current`, `covered`

5. Haiku will be too cautious unless biased hard. Prompt rule should be explicit:

“Mark covered when the user has substantively answered the node’s `done_when`, even if imperfect, unresolved, or emotionally messy. Do not wait for closure. Use `open_threads` for loose ends.”

To prevent eagerness, require a `coverage_evidence_quote` or short paraphrase in the structured output. No evidence, no coverage.

6. Opus prep should use structured output/function calling. Prose-then-parse is the wrong choice. Opus will otherwise invent fields, vary nesting, and produce soft invalid graph edges. Use a strict schema with enums, bounded arrays, required IDs, and validation that every `next_node_id` exists.

7. Post-session Opus should not read raw transcript only. Add an intermediate compression step for long sessions: per-node transcript slices plus coverage summaries, facts, unresolved threads, and notable quotes. For short sessions, raw transcript is fine. For anything over ~20 minutes, compress first or synthesis will overweight late transcript and miss earlier commitments.

8. The one biggest change: **replace the 9 live tools with one atomic `emit_live_turn` structured output containing utterance + routing + coverage + state patches.**

That removes sequencing bugs, cuts tool-loop latency, makes evals easier, and gives Haiku one job per turn: decide the next conversational move as a single coherent object.
