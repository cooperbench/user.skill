> AGENT

## The nuggery core The orchestrator has **one switch with two meanings glued together**: `is_live_test_worker` means both *"don't count this toward production capacity"* AND *"don't ever clean this up."* The first is correct. The second is a bug — it makes the orchestrator's zombie-reaper opt out of its own job whenever the harness owns the row, on the assumption that *some other process* will clean up. No other process does, reliably, because crashes exist. Lifecycle ownership got conflated with capacity accounting. ## The well-engineered solution Split the two concerns: - **Capacity accounting** stays excluded for live-test rows — production scaling shouldn't see them. Keep `filter_live_test_workers` at `control_loop.py:172`. - **Liveness reaping** applies to *every* pod the orchestrator can see, regardless of who owns it. Remove the `is_live_test_worker` short-circuit at `control_loop.py:1719`. The reaper's contract becomes: "if a pod has no heartbeat past its timeout, or has been `spawning` past `SPAWNING_TIMEOUT_SEC`, terminate it — full stop." That's the whole fix. One deletion plus a clarified invariant: **the orchestrator is the sole authority on pod liveness; ownership tags only affect accounting, never cleanup.** Harness teardown logic becomes a nice-to-have for fast cleanup, not a correctness requirement — crashes stop leaking GPUs by construction. Optionally tighten the contract: rename the helper to `excluded_from_capacity_control` so the next person doesn't repeat the conflation, and have the spawner stamp an explicit `max_lifetime_sec` on harness pods (e.g. 2 h) so the reaper has a clean rule for "spawned but never claimed work" cases too.
