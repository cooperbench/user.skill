The gate is the load-bearing concept of the whole system — it is what makes "task mode" mean anything — yet it is implemented as a guard clause inside the thing it guards. That is the inversion. The runner should be dumb: given an argv, run it. A gatekeeper above the runner should decide whether this invocation is allowed, advance the cursor, and append the event. Right now your runner knows about checklists, projects, and active_run; that coupling will metastasize the moment you add a second runtime or a second entry point. Every new execution path will need to remember to call the gate. Make the gate a decorator/middleware over dispatch and the rest of the design simplifies. **checklist + cursor + events: one too many.** Events is truth, cursor is a cache, checklist is the plan. Cut cursor.json; recompute on read. If recomputation is too slow, cache it in-process, not on disk.
