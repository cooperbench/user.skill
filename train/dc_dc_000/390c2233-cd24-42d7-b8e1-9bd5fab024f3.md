Here's how I'd think about it: ## The core mechanic Hunyuan-Foley generates one audio track for one video clip — it has no concept of "region." To get *spatial* Foley you have to make the spatial split yourself: **crop the video into tiles, run each tile through Foley independently, then mix the resulting tracks in the browser based on where the viewport is looking.** The Foley model only "hears" what's in the crop, so a tile of just the creature's mouth gets mouth-sounds; a tile of swirling water gets water. ## Design decisions, in order of how much they matter 1. **Tile grid.** For 3072×3840, a 2×3 (six tiles, ~1536×1280 each) is the sweet spot — small enough that each tile has a distinct subject, large enough that Foley still has semantic context. 3×4 (twelve tiles) gives finer spatial resolution but doubles cost and risks Foley generating generic ambience for tiles that are too zoomed-in to read. **Overlap the tiles by ~25%** so neighboring tracks share content and crossfades sound continuous instead of seamed. 2. **Temporal handling.** Hunyuan-Foley's per-call limit is ~10–15s; 18.8s is borderline. Either trim to 15s, or split each tile into two ~9.4s halves and concatenate. One call per tile is much simpler — I'd start by trimming. 3. **Zoom as a separate layer, not a continuum.** Don't try to interpolate Foley between zoom levels. Generate **two passes**: (a) one global Foley for the full frame, (b) the tile grid. When the user is zoomed out, you hear the global track; as they zoom past some threshold, you crossfade into the local tile mix. Trying to do 3+ zoom levels multiplies cost without proportional payoff. 4. **Browser playback.** Web Audio API. One `<audio>` (or `AudioBufferSourceNode`) per tile, all started in sync, all looping the 15s clip. A `GainNode` per tile whose value is a function of distance from viewport center to tile center (gaussian falloff works well). Pan/zoom updates gains every animation frame. The video element sits underneath with `pointer-events` driving a CSS transform for pan/zoom. 5. **Sync.** All tile clips and the video must be frame-locked.
