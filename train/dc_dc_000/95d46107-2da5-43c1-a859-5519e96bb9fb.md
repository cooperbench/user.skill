Right — and this is the missing piece in the spec. The spec only imagines Arnold running as a **resident process** (gateway connection, owns its own loop lifecycle, polls for messages, manages mid-turn coalescing). When you invoke it via CLI from inside an agentic process, the lifecycle is fundamentally different — the outer agent owns time, Arnold runs one turn per invocation as a subagent, exits, and reports back. These aren't just two transports of the same loop. They're two **execution modes**, and the spec needs to acknowledge both because half its sections (coalescing, recovery, status messages, mid-turn handling) only make sense in one of them. ## The two modes **Resident mode** (Discord, future Slack, future webhooks): - Long-lived process, persistent gateway connection. - Loop runtime *owns* the turn lifecycle: detects when to coalesce messages, when to start a turn, when to recover. - Mid-turn arrivals are real; status messages stream live; external_requests ledger is load-bearing. **Invocation mode** (CLI, programmatic call from a Claude Code session, megaplan handler, cron, HTTP one-shot): - Ephemeral. Caller invokes `megaplan arnold turn --epic X "<input>"`, Arnold loads state from the store, runs **one turn** (defined as: one Anthropic invocation expanded to tool-use completion), persists, exits with a structured result. - No coalescing — caller batched inputs before invoking. No mid-turn — the caller blocks. No live status — progress is structured events on stdout (or silent; final summary suffices). - Recovery is the caller's problem — Arnold's job is "run a turn atomically and report." The ledger still helps but is less load-bearing. The "turn" itself is the same atom in both modes: one Claude invocation that may chain through many internal tool calls until the model stops. What differs is who owns the lifecycle around it. ## How the spec should be updated A new top-level section, slotted right after **Models** and before **What an Epic Is**: > **## Execution Modes** > > Arnold runs in two modes.
