## The provocation engine

The brief fires a small set of provocations at the executor — adversarial prompts that demand an act on the work, not introspection. Three vectors:

- **Cuts** — strip / expose / kill. Example: "Cut the line you'd fight hardest to keep. If the piece is worse, you were right; if it's the same, you killed a darling."
- **Forces** — perform a transformation on the piece. Example: "Halve it. Write the line that survives."
- **Sparks** — the brief itself supplies foreign material the maker reacts to. This is the new vector vs joke mode today. Sub-types:
  - Hostile gifts (an image, line, or detail invented in the brief and asserted to belong)
  - Substitutions ("what if the protagonist were the antagonist of this same scene")
  - Inversions ("the version of this that's a confession, not a performance")
  - Forced transplants (steal a structure from a different form: tarot spread, recipe, courtroom transcript)
  - Borrowed moves (steal one specific move from a master in this form, named)
  - Hostile claims ("this piece is afraid of the thing it's actually about" — refute or absorb)
  - Generative constraints (half the words forbidden, no character speaks, must rhyme)
  - Sensory escalation (what does it smell like / sound like in the next room)
  - Dares (name the move that would get you booed; try it)

Default firing pattern: **one cut, one force, one spark per pass.** Three is plenty; five is a lecture. The prep phase chooses the specific provocations targeted at the draft state — over-explained drafts get cuts on the unsaid; safe drafts get risk/dare sparks; bloated drafts get force-halve; replaceable-feeling drafts get borrowed-move + "who could have made this."

## The stance

Every executor task output includes a stance field: in the maker's authentic voice, naming the provocation engaged, the angle taken, and what changed. Hard constraints to prevent LLM-flavor sand:

- ≤50 words
- First person
- Names the specific provocation engaged
- No hedging verbs (considered / tried / attempted / sought to)
- Must take a position someone could disagree with

A sub-provocation fires at the stance itself: "Read it aloud. If it's safe, the work is too. Rewrite the part that would embarrass you to say to a stranger."

Stance lives in per-task receipts and is bubbled into a "director's notes" sidecar artifact. NOT inline in the final piece — the artifact must stand alone.

## Robustness mapping

Creative work has a different process curve than code: it peaks and then declines into over-sanded mush. Robustness should mean *different angles, harder targets, more diverse provocateur voices* — not just more cycles.

- **Light** — single pass, one cut + one spark (skip the force), stance ≤2 sentences, no critique cycle. For sketches and throwaways. Failure mode protected against: over-process killing a quick instinct.
- **Standard** — one critique/revise cycle. Full triad on prep. Critique fires *different* provocations from a different angle (no relitigating addressed material). Revise stance must explicitly contrast the prior stance.
- **Robust** — multiple cycles with *different provocateur voices*: formalist (structure/economy), iconoclast (risk/lie/dare), audience (stakes/specificity for one named person). Review evaluates full scene canvas + stance authenticity + alignment.

## Executor stop-affordance

Let the maker signal "the next pass would damage the piece" with a stance defending why. Code mode shouldn't have this (more review = safer); creative must. Without it, robust mode reliably over-edits.
