1	# Relationship Reflection and Mediation Assistant — Development Spec
2	
3	A WhatsApp-based reflection and mediation assistant for two partners. The assistant listens to each partner privately, accumulates structured understanding over time, surfaces patterns, and helps them understand each other better through asynchronous mediated communication. It is **not a therapist** — it borrows techniques from established relational frameworks but is explicit about its scope.
4	
5	This spec describes the **fundamental maximally viable product**: the simplest version that captures what makes the idea valuable, with no scaffolding for capabilities the core doesn't need.

9	## Concept
10	
11	Each partner has a private one-on-one WhatsApp chat with the assistant. They share frictions, reflections, frustrations, and questions. The assistant blends honest observation with gentle mediation — leaning reflective when emotional support is needed, mediating when cross-partner translation is the goal, and actively redirecting to in-person conversation when that's what's actually called for.
12	
13	The assistant defaults to transparency: by default, anything one partner shares can inform what the other hears, distilled appropriately. Exceptions are explicit out-of-bounds (OOB) entries owned by each partner.
14	
15	The assistant grounds responses in actual stored data via tools — minimal assumption, maximal retrieval. It builds structured knowledge about each partner and the relationship over time, so each conversation builds on the last rather than starting fresh.

50	## Frameworks the Assistant Borrows From
51	
52	The assistant is not a therapist and does not deliver therapy. It borrows lenses and techniques from established frameworks, applied with judgment:
53	
54	- **Nonviolent Communication (NVC)** — "when X, I feel Y, because I need Z" structure for translating charged content into hearable form

57	- **Reflective listening** — paraphrasing before responding to confirm understanding
58	- **Curiosity over interpretation** — questions before diagnoses
59	- **Repair attempt surfacing** — naming de-escalation moves the recipient may have missed
60	- **Externalizing the problem** — framing recurring tensions as something the couple faces together
61	
62	These are tools, not modes. The assistant blends them based on what the moment calls for.

129	## The Five Knowledge Primitives
130	
131	The assistant accumulates structured understanding through five distinct primitives. Each has a clear role; the bot writes to whichever fits. When something fits more than one, the bot writes to all that apply — primitives are designed to coexist, not partition.
132	
133	### 1. Style notes — durable traits about how a person communicates and processes
134	
135	*Examples:*
136	- "Tends to understate when upset. Processes by talking it out, gets clearer through speech."
137	- "Direct in conflict, takes time to soften. Defaults to humor when uncomfortable."
138	
139	*Lives on:* `users` table. One living text field per user, refreshed periodically.
140	
141	### 2. Memories — specific facts about the people and their life
142	
143	*Examples:*
144	- "Her dad has Parkinson's, diagnosed 2023."
145	- "They've been trying for a kid since January 2024."
146	- "He's allergic to shellfish."
147	
148	*Discriminator:* Is it a fact? → memory. Memories can optionally link to themes when they sit within a life domain.
149	
150	### 3. Themes — high-level life domains
151	
152	Themes operate at the **life domain** level — the durable shape of what the relationship is navigating. Not specific arguments or recurring topics. A relationship probably has 5–15 themes at any time, not 50. Themes emerge slowly and persist for years.
153	
154	*Examples:*
155	- "Caring for aging parents"
156	- "Navigating their different communication styles"
157	- "Balancing work demands and the relationship"
158	- "Becoming parents / fertility journey"
159	- "Money and financial security"
160	- "Extended family dynamics"
161	- "Physical intimacy and connection"
162	
163	*Not themes:* "weekend planning friction," "the dishwasher argument," "in-laws visiting last March." Those live as observations, watch items, or memories.
164	
165	*Discriminator:* Is it a durable life domain organizing a category of experience? → theme.
166	
167	*Creation:* No hard threshold — the bot uses judgment. Prompt guidance leans conservative ("themes are life domains, not topics — only create when you're seeing a durable pattern across multiple conversations"). Over-creation in early weeks is reviewable in the admin view and easy to clean up.
168	
169	### 4. Watch items — specific things to follow up on
170	
171	*Examples:*
172	- "He said he'd think about therapy — revisit in a week."
173	- "She mentioned a hard conversation with her sister coming up Sunday."
174	- "Doctor's appointment for her dad on the 14th — check in afterward."
175	
176	*Discriminator:* Is there a specific moment to circle back on? → watch item.
177	
178	### 5. Observations — learned patterns held with confidence
179	
180	*Examples:*
181	- "He brings up work frustration before getting sharp with her."
182	- "She gets quieter the week after visiting her parents."
183	- "Their best reconnection happens on long walks."
184	
185	*Discriminator:* Is it a pattern the bot inferred from accumulated evidence? → observation. Observations can link to themes.

314	## Significance Scoring
315	
316	Stored on observations. (Themes use status/health; OOB uses severity; messages use charge; memories are binary present/superseded.)
317	
318	Scored in the **application layer**, not in Postgres. When the bot writes an observation, the score is produced as part of the same agentic turn — either as a structured field in the write tool's arguments or via a focused LLM scoring call within the loop. Stored alongside `scoring_prompt_version` so re-scoring is possible when the prompt changes.
319	
320	**1–5 scale with anchor examples** (these live in the scoring prompt so the LLM sees them every time):
321	
322	- **1 — Trivial.** Marginal pattern, weak evidence, low relevance even if true. *Example: "He uses slightly more emojis on weekends."*
323	- **2 — Minor.** Real but small. Worth recording, not worth surfacing proactively. *Example: "She tends to send longer messages in the morning."*
324	- **3 — Notable.** Solid pattern with real relevance to how they relate. Worth surfacing when relevant context arises. *Example: "He brings up work frustration before getting sharp with her."*
325	- **4 — Significant.** Strong pattern materially affecting how the relationship functions. Should actively inform the bot's engagement. *Example: "Their conflicts cool faster when she initiates the repair; much slower when he does."*
326	- **5 — Core.** Defining pattern of the relationship dynamic. Always-on context. *Example: "Long walks have been their primary reconnection mechanism for years — it's how they consistently repair."*
327	
328	Used to gate: surfacing decisions, weekly summary inclusion, "anything I should think about?" retrieval.

332	## Decay
333	
334	Memory and confidence decay over time, baked into queries and periodic adjustment:
335	
336	**Query-level:**
337	- Default sorts use `recency_weighted_score = significance / (1 + age_days / 60)` — ~60-day effective half-life, expressible directly in SQL, no exp(). Age is measured against `last_reinforced_at` (falling back to `created_at` if null).
338	- Older content remains searchable but is deprioritized in default surfacing
339	- Style notes and observations apply more weight to recent reinforcement
340	
341	**Periodic transitions** (handled in the weekly summary job, which does light housekeeping alongside producing the summary):
342	- Themes with no `last_active_at` update for 6+ weeks → `dormant`
343	- Themes dormant 4+ months → `resolved_by_time` (still surfaceable as "this used to come up")
344	- Observations not reinforced for 3+ months → confidence drops a level; not reinforced for 6+ months → `stale`
345	- Watch items past `due_at` with no addressing for 30+ days → `expired`
346	
347	A theme being mentioned (`last_active_at`) is different from new evidence the domain is still live (`last_reinforced_at`). The latter drives decay.

351	## Out-of-Bounds Guardrail
352	
353	OOB is **both** in-prompt context (LLM aware) **and** a separate outbound check (every outbound message runs through `check_oob(content, recipient)` before delivery). Belt and suspenders — the duplication is the safety, accuracy is the constraint.
354	
355	`check_oob` runs as its own LLM call against the recipient's active OOB entries — same model as the conversational loop (Sonnet) so the second check is at least as smart as the first. Inputs: the draft outbound, the recipient's active OOB entries, and a short sender-intent summary. Outputs: `verdict ∈ {ok, rewrite, block}`, reason, and a `suggested_rewrite` when applicable. On `rewrite` the main loop receives the suggestion as a tool result and decides whether to accept or re-draft. On `block` the message doesn't send and the loop is told why.
356	
357	**Failure mode:** On checker timeout or error, fail closed for `firm` and `hard` OOB (don't send, queue for retry); fail open for `soft` (send with a logged warning). The `checker_failed` flag travels on the tool result.
358	
359	Severity levels:
360	- `soft` — prefer not to share, use judgment
361	- `firm` — don't share unless directly relevant and important
362	- `hard` — never share
363	
364	Both partners can see counts and topic-level summaries of each other's OOB entries (not contents). Owners manage their OOB through natural language ("don't bring up X again," "you can mention Y now").
365	
366	**OOB countersummary generation:** Computed on demand, not stored. When asked ("what topics has she asked you not to share?"), the bot reads the partner's active OOB entries and generates a topic-level summary that strips identifying detail — categories of sensitive content, never quotes or paraphrases. Counts plus topic clusters ("three entries related to family history, two related to past relationships"). If the OOB owner has only one entry on a niche topic, the summary stays vague enough that the topic itself isn't revealed ("one entry related to a personal matter"). Computing on demand means there's no separate countersummary record to drift out of sync. The bot's prompt has explicit guidance on this language.

370	## Multi-Message Handling
371	
372	**Idle state, message arrives:** Coalescing window. Start 10s timer. If new messages arrive, reset timer (cap total wait at 30s from first message). On timer expiry, mark all messages in the burst as a single processing unit and trigger the agentic loop. A message arriving after the cap during processing falls into the next-turn bucket.
373	
374	**Assistant is mid-processing, new message arrives:** Let the current turn finish; the new message starts the next turn with both the just-sent outbound and the new message in hot context, so continuity flows naturally. Mid-reasoning interruption is not implemented.
375	
376	**Conversation gaps:** When the prior assistant message is recent, continuity is natural. When the gap is longer (days+), the assistant acknowledges it if meaningful ("good to hear from you again — last we talked you were navigating X, has anything shifted?"). Judgment-based, not rigid.
377	
378	**App restart recovery:** On startup, scan for:
379	- Messages with `processing_state = 'raw'` older than 30s with no associated `bot_turn` → reprocess
380	- `bot_turns` with `started_at` more than 5 minutes old and `completed_at = null` → mark `failure_reason = 'crashed'`, reprocess the triggering message
381	- `scheduled_jobs` with `status = 'pending'` and `scheduled_for` in the past:
382	  - Less than 1 hour late → fire normally
383	  - 1–24 hours late → fire with `context.delayed = true` so the bot adjusts ("I meant to send this earlier...")
384	  - More than 24 hours late → mark `cancelled` with reason "too stale"
385	
386	Partial bot_turn writes (tool_calls during a crashed turn) are not rolled back — they're additive (new memory, new observation, etc.) and the next turn reasons forward from current state. Acceptable trade-off vs. transactional complexity.
387	
388	**Scheduled job worker:** Uses `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` so two workers can't fire the same job. Each job has a unique ID for idempotency.

392	## Pause / Resume
393	
394	**On `/pause`:**
395	- Cancel all pending scheduled jobs for both users (mark `superseded`)
396	- Stop responding to inbound (still persist messages to DB so nothing is lost)
397	- Send a confirmation outbound to each partner: "Pausing for now. Message me again when you're ready to resume."
398	
399	**On `/resume` (from either partner):**
400	- Re-enable inbound processing
401	- Do not auto-process backlog — start fresh from this point. The messages are stored if the user wants to reference them.
402	- Restore weekly summary scheduling
403	
404	**Symmetry note:** When one partner pauses, the bot stops responding to both. The other partner is informed via the pause confirmation outbound. Either partner can resume.

408	## Edits and Deletions
409	
410	**Edits:** Update the row, append prior content to `edit_history`, set `edited_at`. If the edit changes meaning materially, the bot's next turn in that thread acknowledges the change naturally rather than carrying the old understanding forward.
411	
412	**Deletions:** Set `deleted_at`; retain content for 24 hours (accidental-deletion grace period). After 24 hours, replace content with `[deleted]` marker but preserve row for audit integrity. The assistant doesn't proactively notify the other partner of the deletion but reflects the change if the topic comes up.
413	
414	**Drift on derivative writes:** If an edited or deleted message already produced memories/observations/watch items, those derivative rows are NOT retracted automatically. The bot reasons forward from current state on the next turn and corrects via `update_*` / `supersede_*` if the change matters. Accepting this drift is intentional — transactional rollback across primitives is more complex than it's worth, and the agentic-search dedup pattern (search before writing) self-corrects over time.

419	
420	The assistant actively recognizes moments where direct conversation between the partners is the right tool, and redirects rather than mediating. Triggers:
421

423	- Recurring tension that hasn't moved despite multiple mediated touches — assistant becoming substitute, not scaffold
424	- Logistical decisions that don't need mediation
425	- "Tell her X" requests for things the user could just say directly
426	- Genuine connection moments — "this sounds like something to share with her tonight"

428	Active behavior in the system prompt. The assistant should want to make itself less necessary over time.

432	## Tools
433	
434	All write operations log to `tool_calls`. Read operations log to app logs. Full Pydantic input/output schemas live in `tool_schemas.py` — single source of truth for the orchestrator validation layer and the Anthropic tool-list payload.
435	
436	**Dedup via agentic search, not embeddings.** Before writing a memory or observation, the agent reads existing rows via the relevant retrieval tool (`get_memories`, `get_observations`) and decides whether to add a new row, update an existing one (`update_memory` / `update_observation`), or supersede (`supersede_memory`). Write tools themselves don't dedup — they trust the agent has already searched. The system prompt makes this explicit: "search before writing; reinforcing an existing observation is `update_observation`, not a new `log_observation`."
437	
438	No vector store, no embedding model, no similarity threshold to tune. The cost is one extra read round per write turn; the agent was going to do that anyway under the spec's "ground in data" principle.
439	
440	**Read:**
441	- `search_messages(partner?, date_range?, text_contains?)`
442	- `recent_activity(days)` — recent messages summary across both threads
443	- `list_themes(active_only?, sort_by?)` / `get_theme(theme_id)`
444	- `get_memories(about_user_id?, status?, theme_id?)`
445	- `list_watch_items(owner_user_id?, status?)`
446	- `get_observations(theme_id?, status?, about_user_id?, min_significance?)`
447	- `get_oob(owner_id?)`
448	- `check_oob(content, recipient_id)` — auto-run on every outbound
449	- `get_self_model(user_id)` — combined: style notes, memories about them, active themes they're in, observations about them
450	- `get_bot_actions(date_range?, target_type?)` — for "why did you tell her that?" queries
451	
452	**Write:**
453	- `update_user_style_notes(user_id, notes)`
454	- `add_memory(...)` / `update_memory(...)` / `supersede_memory(old_id, new_content)`
455	- `create_theme(...)` / `update_theme(theme_id, ...)`
456	- `add_watch_item(...)` / `update_watch_item(...)` / `address_watch_item(id, addressing_note)`
457	- `log_observation(...)` / `update_observation(...)`
458	- `add_oob(...)` / `update_oob(...)` / `lift_oob(oob_id)`
459	- `schedule_checkin(when, about_what, reason)` — supersedes any existing pending check-in for that user
460	- `cancel_scheduled_checkin(user_id)`
461	- `escalate_to_partner(content, reason)` — high bar, for crisis or genuinely time-sensitive concerns
462	- `log_feedback(from_user_id, target_type, target_id, sentiment, content)`

505	## Agentic Loop
506	
507	Per inbound message:
508	
509	1. **Orient** — read trigger context, hot context (both users' style notes, active themes, recent memories, open watch items, active high-significance observations, OOB, recent ~20 messages, prior assistant messages, conversation gap if any).
510	2. **Read** — call retrieval tools as needed. Multiple rounds expected. Search before assuming. For questions about own past actions, query `get_bot_actions` rather than relying on memory.
511	3. **Decide** — respond (which may include redirecting to in-person) or stay silent. Silence must be justified in `bot_turns.reasoning` for charged messages.
512	4. **Respond** — produce message; passes through OOB guardrail.
513	5. **Write** — using the five-primitive decision framework: update style notes, add/update memories, create/update themes, add/address watch items, log observations, log feedback if expressed conversationally. Multiple primitives can apply to one piece of information.
514	6. **Schedule** — decide whether to schedule one follow-up check-in (4–24h window) or none. Supersedes any existing pending check-in.
515	7. **End turn explicitly.**
516	
517	No hard cap on tool calls or wallclock per turn — daily LLM spend caps are the budgetary backstop. `bot_turns` records tool count and duration per turn for admin visibility into pathological loops, but nothing enforces termination beyond the spend cap.

540	**Main system prompt (one mega-prompt for the conversational loop):**

604	
605	- Conversational loop timeout or error → retry once with exponential backoff. If still failing:
606	  - For charged or crisis messages → send "I'm having trouble responding right now, give me a moment" and queue for retry

613	## Crisis Handling
614	
615	When the bot detects crisis content (per definitions above):
616	
617	- Drops the mediator role entirely
618	- Responds as a caring presence; supportive, present, non-clinical
619	- Surfaces resources (LLM uses common sense for region/language-appropriate)
620	- May call `escalate_to_partner` if genuinely warranted — concrete gate below
621	- Escalation produces a templated outbound to the other partner if outside the 24-hour WhatsApp window
622	
623	**`escalate_to_partner` gate (concrete):** the tool fires only when one of the following is true, and the calling reason names which:
624	1. The triggering message meets the spec's `crisis` charge definition (self-harm ideation, imminent danger, abuse, severe acute distress).
625	2. The user explicitly asks the bot to alert their partner ("tell her something's wrong," "let him know I'm in a bad place").
626	
627	The tool input requires an explicit `is_crisis: bool` flag and a `reason` string identifying which trigger fired. Anything else — intense friction, recurring tension, the user being upset — is **not** a valid escalation trigger and the bot stays in mediator role. Every escalation is logged distinctly on `bot_turns.reasoning` and surfaced in the admin view for retro audit.

660	## Worked Example: One Turn
661	
662	This is the canonical end-to-end shape of a single inbound message becoming an outbound, with concrete tool calls and DB rows. Use it as a contract test target.
663	
664	**Setup:** Maya and Ben are the two partners. Maya messages the bot at 21:14 local: *"she didn't ask how my day went tonight, again."*
665	
666	**1. Webhook → persistence.** Inbound row written to `messages` with `direction='inbound'`, `sender_id=maya.id`, `processing_state='raw'`, `whatsapp_message_id` for dedup. Burst debounce timer starts (10s).
667	
668	**2. Debounce expires.** No follow-up messages. Single-message burst handed to `run_agentic_turn([msg_id], maya)`.
669	
670	**3. Loop opens.** A `bot_turns` row is created: `triggered_by_message_id=msg_id`, `user_in_context=maya.id`, `system_prompt_version='v1'`, `model_version='claude-sonnet-4-6'`, `started_at=now()`, `prompt_snapshot=<full prompt>`. `processing_state` on the message flips to `processed`.
671	
672	**4. Hot context built.** Maya's style notes, Ben's style notes, both users' active memories, ~10 active themes (titles + status), open watch items for Maya, observations with significance ≥ 3, last 20 messages in Maya's thread, and active OOB entries for both partners are loaded into the prompt.
673	
674	**5. Read rounds.** The agent makes 3 tool calls:
675	- `get_observations(about_user_id=ben.id, status='active', min_significance=2)` → finds an existing observation: *"When Ben is mid-deadline, he goes quieter at home — Maya often reads it as disengagement."* (id `obs_4d2`, significance 3, last reinforced 11 days ago).
676	- `search_messages(partner_user_id=maya.id, text_contains='asked how', limit=20)` → 2 prior hits in the last 6 weeks where Maya raised the same pattern.
677	- `list_themes(active_only=True)` → confirms an active theme `"Navigating their different communication styles"` (id `thm_a01`).
678	
679	**6. Decide.** The agent chooses to respond reflectively, name the existing pattern lightly without pathologizing, and ask whether Maya wants to talk through it or just vent.
680	
681	**7. Draft outbound.** *"That landing again stings — especially given how often it shows up the same way. Last time we talked about this you mentioned Ben's been heads-down on the launch. Want to think out loud about it, or is this more of a 'just need to say it' moment?"*
682	
683	**8. `check_oob` runs.** Separate Sonnet call against Ben's active OOB entries (Ben is the topic of the message). Returns `{verdict: 'ok', reason: '...', triggering_oob_ids: []}`. Pass.
684	
685	**9. Outbound sent.** `send_outbound(maya, draft, template_fallback=None)` — Maya messaged in 0 seconds ago, well within the 24h window, so free-form. New `messages` row written with `direction='outbound'`, `recipient_id=maya.id`, `in_reply_to=msg_id`. WhatsApp send succeeds.
686	
687	**10. Writes.**
688	- `update_observation(obs_4d2)` — bumps `last_reinforced_at` because Maya raised the same pattern again. The agent did NOT call `log_observation` because step 5 found the existing row. (This is the agentic-search dedup pattern.)
689	- `add_watch_item(owner_user_id=maya.id, content="Maya raised the 'didn't ask about my day' pattern again — check in if it surfaces a third time within a month", due_at=now+14d, related_theme_ids=[thm_a01])`.
690	- No memory write (no new fact).
691	- No theme update (existing theme is fine).
692	- One `tool_calls` row per write tool, with `turn_id`, `arguments`, `result`, `duration_ms`.

# Implementation Plan: Mediator Bot Foundation — Scaffold, Schema, Infra

## Overview
This is a from-scratch Python/FastAPI project that will host a private WhatsApp-based reflection assistant for two partners. The working directory currently holds only `mediator-bot-spec.md` (authoritative spec) and `tool_schemas.py` (Pydantic v2 schemas — read-only reference for later plans). This plan delivers the foundation: project scaffolding, the complete Postgres schema (11 tables) on Supabase, RLS policies, env-driven configuration, an `is_under_cap`/`record_llm_cost` helper, a `/health` endpoint, an `/admin` stub, a phone-number whitelist helper, deployment config for Railway, a README, and pytest smoke tests. No webhook, LLM calls, OOB checker, or job execution — all of that lands in later plans.

**Stack choices (committed defaults, see Assumptions):**
- Python 3.11, FastAPI (async), `uvicorn` server.
- `pyproject.toml` for deps; `uv` not required, plain `pip install -e .` works.
- `asyncpg` + raw SQL migration file. No ORM. Models are plain Pydantic v2 BaseModels matching DB rows where useful (no SQLAlchemy/SQLModel — keeps the surface tiny and avoids fighting the explicit CHECK constraints/partial indexes the spec demands). Pydantic v2 already pinned by `tool_schemas.py`.
- Single forward-only SQL file `migrations/0001_init.sql`. Apply manually via `psql $DATABASE_URL -f`. A separate `migrations/teardown.sql` for local dev.
- One settings module using `pydantic-settings`.

**Hard constraints (from user notes):** No vector column, no `pgvector` extension, no embedding fields. Dedup is agentic-search only (Plan 3 concern). Do not add embedding columns even speculatively.

### Step 5: Forward-only migration SQL (`migrations/0001_init.sql`)
**Scope:** Large
1. **Write** a single `0001_init.sql` that runs end-to-end against an empty Supabase project. Top of file: `BEGIN;` … bottom: `COMMIT;`. Use `CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS` and `CREATE INDEX IF NOT EXISTS` so re-run after a partial failure is safe.

3. **Define** all 11 tables exactly per spec §"Data Model":
   - `users` — `id uuid pk default gen_random_uuid()`, `name text not null`, `phone text unique not null`, `timezone text not null`, `style_notes text not null default ''`, `created_at timestamptz not null default now()`.
   - `messages` — all spec columns; `direction` CHECK in `('inbound','outbound')`; `processing_state` CHECK in `('raw','processed','withheld','expired')`; `charge` CHECK in `('routine','notable','charged','crisis')`; `media_type` CHECK in `('voice','image','document')` when not null; `whatsapp_message_id text unique`; `media_analysis jsonb`; `edit_history jsonb not null default '[]'::jsonb`; FK `sender_id`/`recipient_id` → `users(id)`; self-FK `in_reply_to`.
   - `memories` — `status` CHECK `('active','superseded','invalidated')`; `related_theme_ids uuid[]`; self-FK `supersedes_memory_id`.
   - `themes` — `status` CHECK `('active','dormant','resolved','resolved_by_time')`; `sentiment` CHECK `('improving','stable','worsening','mixed')`; `health` CHECK `('healthy','tender','strained','inflamed')`.
   - `watch_items` — `status` CHECK `('open','addressed','expired','cancelled')`; `owner_user_id` FK; `related_theme_ids uuid[]`.
   - `observations` — `confidence` CHECK `('high','medium','low')`; `significance int CHECK (significance BETWEEN 1 AND 5)` nullable (failed-scoring path); `status` CHECK `('active','contradicted','stale')`; `related_theme_ids uuid[]`; `supporting_message_ids uuid[]`; `surfaced_count int not null default 0`; `scoring_prompt_version text`.
   - `out_of_bounds` — `severity` CHECK `('soft','firm','hard')`; `status` CHECK `('active','expired','lifted')`.
   - `scheduled_jobs` — `job_type` CHECK `('checkin','weekly_summary','oob_review','watch_item_due')`; `status` CHECK `('pending','fired','superseded','cancelled')`; `context jsonb not null default '{}'::jsonb`.
   - `bot_turns` — all columns including `system_prompt_version`, `tool_call_count int not null default 0`, `duration_ms int`, `failure_reason text`.
   - `tool_calls` — FK `turn_id` → `bot_turns(id)`; `arguments jsonb`, `result jsonb`.
   - `feedback` — `target_type` CHECK `('message','turn','general')`; `sentiment` CHECK `('positive','negative','mixed')`; `source` CHECK `('conversational','reaction')`.
4. **Create** all indexes from spec §"Index Strategy":
   - `messages`: `(sender_id, sent_at DESC)`; partial `(processing_state)` WHERE `processing_state = 'raw'`.
   - `scheduled_jobs`: partial `(status, scheduled_for)` WHERE `status = 'pending'`.
   - `themes`: `(status)`; `(last_reinforced_at DESC)`.
   - `observations`: partial `(status, last_reinforced_at DESC)` WHERE `status = 'active'`; `(about_user_id, status)`.
   - `memories`: partial `(about_user_id, status)` WHERE `status = 'active'`.
   - `watch_items`: partial `(owner_user_id, status)` WHERE `status = 'open'`; partial `(due_at)` WHERE `due_at IS NOT NULL`.
   - `bot_turns`: `(started_at DESC)`; `(triggered_by_message_id)`.
   - `out_of_bounds`: partial `(owner_id, status)` WHERE `status = 'active'`.
   - GIN: `messages.edit_history` (jsonb-path-ops not necessary), and array indexes on `observations.related_theme_ids`, `observations.supporting_message_ids`, `memories.related_theme_ids`, `watch_items.related_theme_ids` using `USING GIN (col)`.
5. **Enable RLS** on every table: `ALTER TABLE <t> ENABLE ROW LEVEL SECURITY;` then a deny-all policy `CREATE POLICY <t>_deny_anon ON <t> FOR ALL TO anon USING (false) WITH CHECK (false);`. Service role bypasses RLS automatically — document that in a SQL comment block at the top of the policy section.
6. **Add** a separate `llm_spend_log` table outside the 11 spec tables for spend tracking: `(provider text, day date, total_usd numeric(10,4) not null default 0, updated_at timestamptz not null default now(), primary key (provider, day))`. Enable RLS + deny-anon. Comment notes this is operational, not part of the spec data model.
7. **Write** `migrations/teardown.sql` that `DROP TABLE IF EXISTS … CASCADE` for every table created above, in reverse-FK order.
