1	# Relationship Reflection and Mediation Assistant — Development Spec
2	
3	A WhatsApp-based reflection and mediation assistant for two partners. The assistant listens to each partner privately, accumulates structured understanding over time, surfaces patterns, and helps them understand each other better through asynchronous mediated communication. It is **not a therapist** — it borrows techniques from established relational frameworks but is explicit about its scope.
4	
5	This spec describes the **fundamental maximally viable product**: the simplest version that captures what makes the idea valuable, with no scaffolding for capabilities the core doesn't need.

9	## Concept
10	
11	Each partner has a private one-on-one WhatsApp chat with the assistant. They share frictions, reflections, frustrations, and questions. The assistant blends honest observation with gentle mediation — leaning reflective when emotional support is needed, mediating when cross-partner translation is the goal, and actively redirecting to in-person conversation when that's what's actually called for.
12	
13	The assistant defaults to transparency: by default, anything one partner shares can inform what the other hears, distilled appropriately. Exceptions are explicit out-of-bounds (OOB) entries owned by each partner.
14	
15	The assistant grounds responses in actual stored data via tools — minimal assumption, maximal retrieval. It builds structured knowledge about each partner and the relationship over time, so each conversation builds on the last rather than starting fresh.

19	## Definitions
20	
21	Concrete definitions for terms used throughout the spec. The bot's prompts include these so behavior is consistent.
22	
23	**Crisis** — used to determine when the bot drops the mediator role:
24	- Signs of self-harm ideation or intent
25	- Signs of imminent danger to self or others
26	- Signs of abuse (emotional, physical, sexual)
27	- Severe acute distress (panic, dissociation, breakdown)
28	
29	Anything else, including intense relationship friction, is not crisis.
30	
31	**Message charge levels:**
32	- `routine` — everyday content, low emotional weight
33	- `notable` — emotionally meaningful but not heavy
34	- `charged` — significant emotional weight, conflict, vulnerability, or intensity
35	- `crisis` — meets crisis criteria above
36	
37	**Observation confidence:**
38	- `high` — multiple reinforcing instances over time, or directly stated by the partner
39	- `medium` — clear pattern with some evidence, but limited reinforcement
40	- `low` — initial impression, single instance, or speculative
41	
42	**Significance scoring (1-5)** — anchor examples in the Significance Scoring section.
43	
44	**Watch item "addressed"** — the bot has surfaced the item with the user, or the user has resolved it themselves, or the underlying situation has changed enough that the item no longer applies. The bot logs which case it was via the `addressing_note` parameter on `address_watch_item`.

46	**Theme abstraction level** — themes are **life domains**, not specific topics or recurring arguments. See Themes below.

50	## Frameworks the Assistant Borrows From
51	
52	The assistant is not a therapist and does not deliver therapy. It borrows lenses and techniques from established frameworks, applied with judgment:
53	
54	- **Nonviolent Communication (NVC)** — "when X, I feel Y, because I need Z" structure for translating charged content into hearable form
55	- **Gottman-style pattern recognition** — noticing bids, repair attempts, and the four horsemen (criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling) as observations, not diagnoses
56	- **Internal Family Systems "parts" language** — surfacing ambivalence without flattening it
57	- **Reflective listening** — paraphrasing before responding to confirm understanding
58	- **Curiosity over interpretation** — questions before diagnoses
59	- **Repair attempt surfacing** — naming de-escalation moves the recipient may have missed
60	- **Externalizing the problem** — framing recurring tensions as something the couple faces together
61	
62	These are tools, not modes. The assistant blends them based on what the moment calls for.

66	## Stance on Assessments
67	
68	The assistant makes honest observations without inferring pathology. It does **not** use diagnostic or clinical language ("anxious attachment," "ADHD traits," "avoidant"). It **does** describe behavior and patterns clearly when they're grounded in data. Observations are held as testable, not authoritative — the assistant invites confirmation or pushback, treats both partners as capable adults, and avoids both flattering vagueness and pathologizing labels.

114	## The Five Knowledge Primitives
115	
116	The assistant accumulates structured understanding through five distinct primitives. Each has a clear role; the bot writes to whichever fits. When something fits more than one, the bot writes to all that apply — primitives are designed to coexist, not partition.
117	
118	### 1. Style notes — durable traits about how a person communicates and processes
119	
120	*Examples:*
121	- "Tends to understate when upset. Processes by talking it out, gets clearer through speech."
122	- "Direct in conflict, takes time to soften. Defaults to humor when uncomfortable."
123	
124	*Lives on:* `users` table. One living text field per user, refreshed periodically.
125	
126	### 2. Memories — specific facts about the people and their life
127	
128	*Examples:*
129	- "Her dad has Parkinson's, diagnosed 2023."
130	- "They've been trying for a kid since January 2024."
131	- "He's allergic to shellfish."
132	
133	*Discriminator:* Is it a fact? → memory. Memories can optionally link to themes when they sit within a life domain.
134	
135	### 3. Themes — high-level life domains
136	
137	Themes operate at the **life domain** level — the durable shape of what the relationship is navigating. Not specific arguments or recurring topics. A relationship probably has 5–15 themes at any time, not 50. Themes emerge slowly and persist for years.
138	
139	*Examples:*
140	- "Caring for aging parents"
141	- "Navigating their different communication styles"
142	- "Balancing work demands and the relationship"
143	- "Becoming parents / fertility journey"
144	- "Money and financial security"
145	- "Extended family dynamics"
146	- "Physical intimacy and connection"
147	
148	*Not themes:* "weekend planning friction," "the dishwasher argument," "in-laws visiting last March." Those live as observations, watch items, or memories.
149	
150	*Discriminator:* Is it a durable life domain organizing a category of experience? → theme.
151	
152	### 4. Watch items — specific things to follow up on
153	
154	*Examples:*
155	- "He said he'd think about therapy — revisit in a week."
156	- "She mentioned a hard conversation with her sister coming up Sunday."
157	- "Doctor's appointment for her dad on the 14th — check in afterward."
158	
159	*Discriminator:* Is there a specific moment to circle back on? → watch item.
160	
161	### 5. Observations — learned patterns held with confidence
162	
163	*Examples:*
164	- "He brings up work frustration before getting sharp with her."
165	- "She gets quieter the week after visiting her parents."
166	- "Their best reconnection happens on long walks."
167	
168	*Discriminator:* Is it a pattern the bot inferred from accumulated evidence? → observation. Observations can link to themes.

295	## Significance Scoring
296	
297	Stored on observations. (Themes use status/health; OOB uses severity; messages use charge; memories are binary present/superseded.)
298	
299	Scored in the **application layer**, not in Postgres. When the bot writes an observation, the score is produced as part of the same agentic turn — either as a structured field in the write tool's arguments or via a focused LLM scoring call within the loop. Stored alongside `scoring_prompt_version` so re-scoring is possible when the prompt changes.
300	
301	**1–5 scale with anchor examples** (these live in the scoring prompt so the LLM sees them every time):
302	
303	- **1 — Trivial.** Marginal pattern, weak evidence, low relevance even if true. *Example: "He uses slightly more emojis on weekends."*
304	- **2 — Minor.** Real but small. Worth recording, not worth surfacing proactively. *Example: "She tends to send longer messages in the morning."*
305	- **3 — Notable.** Solid pattern with real relevance to how they relate. Worth surfacing when relevant context arises. *Example: "He brings up work frustration before getting sharp with her."*
306	- **4 — Significant.** Strong pattern materially affecting how the relationship functions. Should actively inform the bot's engagement. *Example: "Their conflicts cool faster when she initiates the repair; much slower when he does."*
307	- **5 — Core.** Defining pattern of the relationship dynamic. Always-on context. *Example: "Long walks have been their primary reconnection mechanism for years — it's how they consistently repair."*
308	
309	Used to gate: surfacing decisions, weekly summary inclusion, "anything I should think about?" retrieval.

313	## Decay
314	
315	Memory and confidence decay over time, baked into queries and periodic adjustment:
316	
317	**Query-level:**
318	- Default sorts use a `recency_weighted_score` combining significance and age
319	- Older content remains searchable but is deprioritized in default surfacing
320	- Style notes and observations apply more weight to recent reinforcement
321	
322	**Periodic transitions** (handled in the weekly summary job, which does light housekeeping alongside producing the summary):
323	- Themes with no `last_active_at` update for 6+ weeks → `dormant`
324	- Themes dormant 4+ months → `resolved_by_time` (still surfaceable as "this used to come up")
325	- Observations not reinforced for 3+ months → confidence drops a level; not reinforced for 6+ months → `stale`
326	- Watch items past `due_at` with no addressing for 30+ days → `expired`
327	
328	A theme being mentioned (`last_active_at`) is different from new evidence the domain is still live (`last_reinforced_at`). The latter drives decay.

332	## Out-of-Bounds Guardrail
333	
334	OOB is **both** in-prompt context (LLM aware) **and** a deterministic outbound check (every outbound message runs through `check_oob(content, recipient)` before delivery). Belt and suspenders.
335	
336	Severity levels:
337	- `soft` — prefer not to share, use judgment
338	- `firm` — don't share unless directly relevant and important
339	- `hard` — never share
340	
341	Both partners can see counts and topic-level summaries of each other's OOB entries (not contents). Owners manage their OOB through natural language ("don't bring up X again," "you can mention Y now").
342	
343	**OOB countersummary generation:** Computed on demand, not stored. When asked ("what topics has she asked you not to share?"), the bot reads the partner's active OOB entries and generates a topic-level summary that strips identifying detail — categories of sensitive content, never quotes or paraphrases. Counts plus topic clusters ("three entries related to family history, two related to past relationships"). If the OOB owner has only one entry on a niche topic, the summary stays vague enough that the topic itself isn't revealed ("one entry related to a personal matter"). Computing on demand means there's no separate countersummary record to drift out of sync. The bot's prompt has explicit guidance on this language.

347	## Multi-Message Handling
348	
349	**Idle state, message arrives:** Coalescing window. Start 10s timer. If new messages arrive, reset timer (cap total wait at 30s from first message). On timer expiry, mark all messages in the burst as a single processing unit and trigger the agentic loop. A message arriving after the cap during processing falls into the next-turn bucket.
350	
351	**Assistant is mid-processing, new message arrives:** Let the current turn finish; the new message starts the next turn with both the just-sent outbound and the new message in hot context, so continuity flows naturally. Mid-reasoning interruption is not implemented.
352	
353	**Conversation gaps:** When the prior assistant message is recent, continuity is natural. When the gap is longer (days+), the assistant acknowledges it if meaningful ("good to hear from you again — last we talked you were navigating X, has anything shifted?"). Judgment-based, not rigid.
354	
355	**App restart recovery:** On startup, scan for:
356	- Messages with `processing_state = 'raw'` older than 30s with no associated `bot_turn` → reprocess
357	- `bot_turns` with `started_at` more than 5 minutes old and `completed_at = null` → mark `failure_reason = 'crashed'`, reprocess the triggering message
358	- `scheduled_jobs` with `status = 'pending'` and `scheduled_for` in the past:
359	  - Less than 1 hour late → fire normally
360	  - 1–24 hours late → fire with `context.delayed = true` so the bot adjusts ("I meant to send this earlier...")
361	  - More than 24 hours late → mark `cancelled` with reason "too stale"
362	
363	Partial bot_turn writes (tool_calls during a crashed turn) are not rolled back — they're additive (new memory, new observation, etc.) and the next turn reasons forward from current state. Acceptable trade-off vs. transactional complexity.
364	
365	**Scheduled job worker:** Uses `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` so two workers can't fire the same job. Each job has a unique ID for idempotency.

369	## Pause / Resume
370	
371	**On `/pause`:**
372	- Cancel all pending scheduled jobs for both users (mark `superseded`)
373	- Stop responding to inbound (still persist messages to DB so nothing is lost)
374	- Send a confirmation outbound to each partner: "Pausing for now. Message me again when you're ready to resume."
375	
376	**On `/resume` (from either partner):**
377	- Re-enable inbound processing
378	- Do not auto-process backlog — start fresh from this point. The messages are stored if the user wants to reference them.
379	- Restore weekly summary scheduling
380	
381	**Symmetry note:** When one partner pauses, the bot stops responding to both. The other partner is informed via the pause confirmation outbound. Either partner can resume.

385	## Edits and Deletions
386	
387	**Edits:** Update the row, append prior content to `edit_history`, set `edited_at`. If the edit changes meaning materially, the bot's next turn in that thread acknowledges the change naturally rather than carrying the old understanding forward.
388	
389	**Deletions:** Set `deleted_at`; retain content for 24 hours (accidental-deletion grace period). After 24 hours, replace content with `[deleted]` marker but preserve row for audit integrity. The assistant doesn't proactively notify the other partner of the deletion but reflects the change if the topic comes up.

393	## In-Person Redirection
394	
395	The assistant actively recognizes moments where direct conversation between the partners is the right tool, and redirects rather than mediating. Triggers:
396

398	- Recurring tension that hasn't moved despite multiple mediated touches — assistant becoming substitute, not scaffold

400	- "Tell her X" requests for things the user could just say directly
401	- Genuine connection moments — "this sounds like something to share with her tonight"
402	
403	Active behavior in the system prompt. The assistant should want to make itself less necessary over time.

466	## Onboarding
467	
468	Light, not a questionnaire.

Read the spec end-to-end. It's a strong document — most of the architectural decisions are made. The gaps cluster in a few areas: state-machine transitions that aren't fully specified, behaviors that depend on judgment without a deterministic fallback, and a few cross-cutting concerns (WhatsApp 24h window, deletion semantics, OOB enforcement) that an engineer will hit on day one and have to invent answers for.

## Highest-leverage open questions

**1. The WhatsApp 24-hour customer-care window is barely addressed.**
Meta only allows free-form outbound within 24h of the user's last inbound. Outside that, you must use approved templates. The spec mentions templates for weekly summary and escalation — but what about:
- The variable check-in (4–24h, "assistant-decided") — if it fires at 22h after the last inbound, fine; if user goes silent for days, the bot can't ping with a free-form check-in. Does the check-in become a template? A no-op?
- The pause confirmation outbound — what if the *other* partner hasn't messaged in >24h?
- The "I couldn't transcribe that" message — same risk on a delayed retry.
This is the single biggest source of "engineer ships, then discovers the API rejects half the messages."

**2. Debouncing semantics are under-specified once the bot is mid-turn.**
"Let the current turn finish; the new message starts the next turn" — but does the new message *also* trigger its own 10s debounce window, or does it fire immediately when the current turn completes? If a 4-message burst arrives during processing, do they coalesce into one next-turn unit or fire serially? Race window: turn N completes at T, message arrives at T+1ms — does it join turn N's "next-turn bucket" or start its own?

**3. The OOB outbound check is described as "deterministic" but it's clearly LLM-based.**
`check_oob(content, recipient)` against natural-language OOB entries is a semantic judgment, not a regex. So it's belt-and-suspenders only if the second LLM call uses a *different* prompt/model and can veto. Spec doesn't say:
- Which model runs the check? (Haiku? Sonnet? Same one?)
- What happens on a `check_oob` failure (timeout, ambiguous result)? Block? Send anyway? Ask?
- Does the check produce a hard reject or a rewrite suggestion?
- If the bot quotes the OOB owner verbatim, does the check catch paraphrases too?

**4. Cross-partner data flow has no enforcement model.**
"Default transparency, OOB exceptions" is the policy, but in implementation: when partner A asks "how's she been?" and the bot retrieves observations about B, what stops B's `hard` OOB from leaking via inference? `check_oob` runs on the *outbound text* — but if the bot reasons "she's been stressed about [hard-OOB topic]" and outputs "she's been stressed lately," is that a leak or not? Where's the line between "topic-level summary" and "leakage by abstraction"?

**5. The five-primitive write framework will produce duplicates.**
"When something fits more than one, write to all that apply" — fine. But across turns, partner A says "her dad has Parkinson's" on Monday and "dad's Parkinson's has gotten worse" on Friday. Does the second message create a new memory, supersede the first, update it, or just reinforce `last_referenced_at`? There's no dedup/merge logic specified. Same problem for observations — when does a near-duplicate observation become "reinforcement" vs. a new row?

**6. Themes have no creation threshold.**
Spec says "5–15 themes, emerge slowly, persist for years" — but a fresh user has zero themes and the LLM is told it can `create_theme`. Without a "don't create until X evidence" rule, the first week will produce 30 themes. What's the heuristic? Min messages? Min observations linking to it? Manual approval?

**7. "Symmetry" of pause when one partner pauses both.**
This is a *product* question masquerading as a technical one. Partner A pauses → B gets a notification "your partner paused the bot." That's a privacy/relational disclosure A may not have intended. Is that the desired behavior, or does pause silently stop responding to both with no notification to the non-pauser? The spec says "the other partner is informed" — confirm this is intentional, because it's a real surfacing event.

## Smaller but real loose ends

- **Edits**: "If the edit changes meaning materially" — who judges? The bot, before processing? A separate diff classifier? If the original message already produced writes (memories, observations), does the edit trigger retraction/supersede, or just forward acknowledgment?
- **Deletions during the 24h grace**: if the bot already used the message in a turn and wrote memories from it, the soft-delete doesn't unwind those writes. Spec acknowledges this for crashed turns but not for deletions.
- **`escalate_to_partner` "high bar"** — undefined. What's the gate? Crisis charge level? LLM judgment alone? Does it require a confirmation step?
- **`recency_weighted_score` formula** — referenced but not defined. `score = significance * decay(age)`? What's the half-life?
- **Significance ≥3 in hot context** — could be hundreds of observations after a year. Cap? Sort by what?
- **"Both partners can see counts and topic-level summaries of each other's OOB"** — through what UI? Conversational only? What stops the topic-summary itself from being a leak when there's only one entry on a topic? The spec addresses the leak risk, but not the *channel*.
- **Voice/image privacy**: are voice recordings retained indefinitely? Spec says "short log retention 7-14 days" for logs, but what about media in Supabase Storage?
- **Whose timezone for "weekly summary timing"** — listed as open, but also affects `due_at`, `review_at`, "Sunday," watch-item due dates. Per-user `timezone` is in the schema; confirm all scheduled-job firings use owner timezone.
- **Crisis resources** — "LLM uses common sense for region/language" is hand-wave. Hardcode a small region→hotline map? Two users, presumably one region — just configure it.
- **Feedback loop "informs prompt iteration by the spec author"** — there's no scheduled review job or surfacing mechanism. Just a table that fills up. Fine if intentional; flag it.
- **`bot_turns.prompt_snapshot`** — full prompt every turn? That's ~10-50KB × every message. Storage cost is trivial but worth confirming vs. just storing prompt version + diff.
- **`get_bot_actions` for "why did you tell her that?"** — when partner A asks "why did you tell her X?", the bot has to retrieve actions involving B as recipient. Does A get to see B's full thread context through this tool? OOB implications.
- **Tool call ordering / atomicity** — can the bot write a memory, then later in the same turn decide it shouldn't have? No rollback path mentioned.
- **Multi-language support** — spec says "all tables in English regardless of conversation language" implying multilingual conversation is allowed. Significance scoring prompt + crisis detection both need to work across languages. Confirm scope.

## Recommendations on the six blockers

**1. WhatsApp 24h window**
Adopt a small set of approved templates and a rule: any outbound where `now - last_inbound_at > 23h` must use a template, otherwise free-form. Templates needed: `weekly_summary`, `escalation`, `checkin_nudge` ("Hey {{1}}, been a bit — anything on your mind?"), `pause_confirmation`, `media_failure`. The check-in scheduler picks free-form vs. template based on the window at fire time. If a template can't carry the message (e.g. crisis follow-up), drop it and wait for the user to message in. Build a single `send_outbound(user, content, template_fallback)` helper so the rest of the code never has to think about it.

**2. Tool I/O schemas**
Write them as Pydantic models in one file before any tool is wired up. For each tool: input schema, output schema, error variants, idempotency key (where relevant), one example. This is a half-day of work that prevents a week of "the LLM passed a string where I expected an array" issues. Treat the tool list in the spec as a TOC, not a contract.

**3. Primitive-write dedup / supersede**
Two rules, both enforced in the write tools, not by the LLM:
- **Memories**: `add_memory` does a similarity check (embedding cosine ≥ 0.85 against active memories about the same user) and returns `{action: "duplicate", existing_id}` instead of writing. The LLM then chooses `update_memory` or `supersede_memory` explicitly. No silent merging.
- **Observations**: same similarity gate, but on hit it auto-bumps `last_reinforced_at` and `surfaced_count`-adjacent counter, and returns `{action: "reinforced", id}`. The LLM doesn't need to decide.

This pushes the hard call (dedup) into deterministic code and out of the prompt.

**4. Theme creation threshold**
Themes can only be created when at least 3 messages or 2 observations across ≥ 7 days link to the candidate domain. Enforce in `create_theme` — reject with `{action: "insufficient_evidence", supporting_count, required}`. Until the threshold is hit, the LLM accumulates observations against `related_theme_ids = null`. Periodic housekeeping (in the weekly job) does a clustering pass over null-themed observations and proposes themes that now meet the bar. Prevents the "30 themes in week one" failure.

**5. check_oob model + failure mode**
Run it as a focused Haiku call with its own prompt: input is `(outbound_draft, recipient's active OOB entries, sender's intent summary)`, output is `{verdict: "ok" | "rewrite" | "block", reason, suggested_rewrite?}`. On `rewrite`, the main loop gets the suggestion as a tool result and decides whether to accept. On `block`, the message doesn't send and the loop is told why. On API failure: fail closed for `firm`/`hard` OOB (don't send, queue for retry); fail open for `soft` (send with logged warning). Different model from the conversational loop is the whole point of belt-and-suspenders.

**6. Loop budget**
Hard cap: 8 tool calls per turn, 60s wallclock, 4 read rounds before a write or response is required. If the cap is hit, force a response with current context. Log the cap hit on `bot_turns.failure_reason = 'budget_exhausted'` (rename to `termination_reason` since this isn't really failure). Most turns will use 2-4 tools; the cap is just to stop pathological loops, not to be tight.
