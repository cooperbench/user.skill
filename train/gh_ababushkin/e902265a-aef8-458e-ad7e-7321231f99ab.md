## Part A — The OKR and goal-setting canon

### Andy Grove — *High Output Management* (1983)

The original OKR formulation was Grove's at Intel, where he reframed Drucker's MBOs by separating two things that MBOs had collapsed: the *direction* and the *milestones*. In Grove's words, "the key result has to be measurable. But at the end you can look, and without any argument: did I do that or did I not do it? Yes? No? Simple. No judgements in it." The **Objective** is qualitative — a direction worth chasing for a defined period. The **Key Results** are the concrete, near-term, measurable milestones that, if hit, give *evidence* the objective is being achieved. Grove also introduced the discipline of grading KRs numerically (0.0–1.0) and using ~0.7 as the target for stretch KRs — full attainment means the KRs were not ambitious enough.

### John Doerr — *Measure What Matters* (2018)

Doerr brought OKRs from Intel to Google and packaged Grove's discipline for wide use. Two ideas matter for this work:

1. **Committed vs aspirational OKRs.** Committed OKRs must hit 1.0 — they are operational commitments. Aspirational ("moonshot") OKRs are expected to land at 0.6–0.7; missing them is normal and is the price of ambition. Conflating the two is the single most common organisational failure mode.
2. **CFRs — Conversations, Feedback, Recognition.** OKRs without a continuous-performance-management cadence calcify. The KRs need to be reviewed weekly, not quarterly, and the review is a conversation, not a status report.

### Christina Wodtke — *Radical Focus* (2016, 2nd ed. 2021)

Wodtke is the most useful operational guide for small teams. Her rules:

- "OKRs are a *commitment to focus*. Focus is the thing OKRs buy you." The cost of an OKR is the things you're now saying no to.
- KRs must be **hard, time-bound, and trackable weekly**. If you cannot tell on a Friday whether you've moved closer to a KR, the KR is wrong.
- **One Objective, three KRs** is her default — she explicitly argues against 5 KRs for small teams, on the grounds that "five things" is not focus.
- OKRs are a *scoreboard*, not a *to-do list*. The KR is the score; the work to move the score is separate. A KR that reads like a task is a category error.
- Confidence rating: every Monday, each KR gets a 1–10 confidence-of-hitting score. The conversation about why a confidence number went down is the actual value.

### Ben Lamorte — *The OKRs Field Book* (2022)

Lamorte (who runs OKRs.com and has rolled out OKRs at hundreds of companies) codifies the rules most often violated:

- **3–5 KRs per Objective is the working range**, with strong preference for 3.
- The "value vs activity" distinction: a KR describes a *result*, not an *activity that might produce a result*. "Publish 5 blog posts" is activity; "10% increase in organic signups from blog traffic" is value.
- Objectives are qualitative and inspirational; KRs are quantitative and verifiable. If you can't write the Objective without numbers, it's actually a KR.
- The OKR cycle is a closed loop: set → check in weekly → review and score at end → reflect on what we learned about our calibration.

### Felipe Castro — input vs output vs outcome

Castro popularised a hierarchy that's especially load-bearing for solo and small-team work:

- **Input KRs** describe what you'll do ("hold 5 customer interviews"). Almost always wrong as a KR — they measure effort, not value.
- **Output KRs** describe what you'll produce ("ship feature X to 100% of users"). Sometimes appropriate, but easily becomes a feature-factory KR.
- **Outcome KRs** describe the change in the world ("checkout completion rate ≥ 78%"). This is the level OKRs are designed for.

Castro's rule: **the right KR level is the highest level you can directly measure within the cycle**. For long causal chains (eg "product change → retention → revenue"), pick the earliest *outcome* (not output) on the chain that you can observe within the window.

### SMART goals — Doran (1981)

The SMART acronym (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound) predates OKRs by two decades and is from George T. Doran's 1981 *Management Review* piece. SMART describes the *quality* of any individual KR — a KR should pass SMART — but SMART by itself doesn't give you the objective layer or the cadence. **OKRs ⊃ SMART**: every well-formed KR is SMART; not every SMART goal is an OKR.

### North-star metrics and leading/lagging indicators

The "north-star metric" terminology comes from Sean Ellis and was popularised by Amplitude. The discipline: one top-level outcome metric per product, and every OKR cycle's KRs should be either that metric, an input to it, or a constraint on it. **Leading indicators** are early signals (sign-ups, activation rate) that predict the **lagging indicator** (retention, revenue). For solo work, the lagging indicator usually moves too slowly to be a per-cycle KR — pick a leading indicator that has known causal connection to it.

### Counter-perspectives

- **Marty Cagan** (SVPG) — Cagan's standing critique is that OKRs in feature-factory teams become outputs in disguise: "ship Feature X by end of quarter" is dressed up as a KR but is really a roadmap commitment. His fix: KRs must be *customer or business outcomes*, never team activity.
- **Itamar Gilad — GIST (Goals/Ideas/Steps/Tasks)** — Gilad argues OKRs need a discovery layer beneath them: Goals (≈ Objectives), Ideas (hypothesised ways to move the goal, ranked by ICE confidence), Steps (small validation experiments), Tasks (implementation). The KRs sit at the Goal level; everything below is treated as bets. For a solo author this prevents OKRs from collapsing into a quarterly to-do list.
- **Will Larson** (*An Elegant Puzzle*, *Staff Engineer*) — For very small teams or solo work, Larson is sceptical that the full OKR machinery is worth its weight; he prefers strategy docs that name a small number of bets and what evidence would disprove each. Worth taking seriously for a single-author portfolio: maybe the right tool is OKR-shaped artefacts (Objective + observable states), but without the corporate cadence overhead.

## Part B — Common OKR failure modes

Each named in the canon (Doerr, Wodtke, Lamorte, Castro, Cagan); each common enough to merit explicit pre-rebuttal in a skill.

- **Sandbagging.** Setting KRs you know you'll hit so the scoreboard reads green. Doerr's antidote is the committed-vs-aspirational split — aspirational KRs are *supposed* to land at 0.6–0.7, so 1.0 on every aspirational KR is a red flag, not a victory.
- **Output KRs disguised as outcomes.** "Ship X" instead of "X drives Y observable change." Cagan's specific objection. The test: if the team can hit the KR without anything changing for the user or the business, it's an output.
- **Activity KRs.** "Hold 5 user interviews," "run 3 experiments." Measures effort, not value. Castro's input-vs-outcome distinction. The fix: name what you'll *know* or what will be *true* afterwards.
- **Too many KRs.** Wodtke is explicit: more than 3–5 isn't focus, it's a checklist. Each additional KR halves the chance any one of them gets attention.
- **Stale KRs.** Set at the start of the quarter, never revisited. The Wodtke cadence (weekly check-in, confidence rating) exists exactly to prevent this. KRs without a check-in cadence rot.
- **Cross-team KRs with no owner.** Doerr: every KR has exactly one accountable owner, even if execution is shared. KRs that read "we" with no named owner default to nobody.
- **Vanity metrics.** Page views, downloads, sign-ups when the actual value is retention or correctness. Castro's note: a vanity metric goes up and to the right while the business doesn't actually improve.
- **Misuse for performance review.** Doerr is emphatic: OKRs must be decoupled from compensation. The moment your bonus depends on hitting your KRs, you sandbag, and Doerr's calibration target of 0.7 dies. (Less relevant to a solo author — but the analogue is "don't use OKRs to judge yourself," because then you stop setting honest stretch targets.)

## Part C — Project-type-specific OKR playbooks

The user's six project types each require a different KR shape because each has a different *theory of success*. A KR shape is a coherent answer to: "what does it mean for this project to have worked?"

### Type 1 — Skill pack / methodology (guidance-only)

*Example: pde-skills.*

**Objective shape.** "For [agent or human invoker], make [decision moment] happen correctly without prompting." The Objective is about *invocation quality at the right moment*, not about authoring more skills. The product's value is decisions made differently — not Markdown shipped.

**Default KR mix.** Heavily *outcome*-weighted, with *invocation-rate* KRs as the leading indicator and *decision-quality* KRs as the lagging one.

- **Invocation-rate KR** (leading): does the skill fire at the right moment? Eg "plan-review skill fires before approval in ≥80% of sampled plan-approval moments." This is testable against transcript history (eg `rtk discover`).
- **Decision-quality KR** (lagging): when the skill fires, does the outcome improve? Eg "in ≥4 of 5 sampled invocations, plan-review surfaced an issue that would otherwise have shipped."
- **Adoption KR** (output, sparingly): "skill is installed in N repos." Use only if cross-repo adoption is the actual goal — most of the time it isn't.

**Concrete worked example** (for pde-skills `initiative-shape` itself):

```
Goal:           For Anton, make initiatives properly shaped before they enter
                a cycle — so cycle planning has goal+KR-bearing initiatives
                rather than repo-aliased backlogs.
Key results:    1. initiative-shape fires (or is offered) in ≥80% of sampled
                   "new initiative" moments in the next 4 cycles
                2. in ≥4 of the last 5 created initiatives, all 4 fields
                   (goal/KRs/repos/appetite) are populated and pass the
                   verification rubric
                3. zero Linear projects created in the next 4 cycles that
                   are repo-aliased rather than outcome-named
                4. at the end of the next 2 cycles, ≥3 of the 4 retro
                   conversations cite a KR (not "did we ship X")
Affected repos: pde-skills
Appetite:       ~8 issues
```

**Anti-patterns for this type.**

- "Author 5 new skills this cycle" — output, not outcome. Authoring more skills doesn't matter if they don't fire at the right moment.
- "Update SKILL.md for X" — task, not KR. Belongs on the issue, not on the OKR.
- "Improve skill quality" — unmeasurable; Wodtke's "trackable weekly" test fails.
- "Get to 100% test coverage of skill verification rubrics" — vanity coverage metric; what matters is whether the rubric catches the failures, not whether every line ran.

**Verification rubric.** A KR is good if it could be inspected by a fresh agent in the next session: (a) the data source is named (transcript history, Linear project list, sampled invocations); (b) the sample method is named; (c) the baseline (current state) is recorded; (d) the target is a number; (e) it could in principle be 0.0–1.0 graded.

### Type 2 — Personal product (single-user, running)

*Example: nestl (Dutch housing monitor).*

**Objective shape.** "Make [recurring job] happen reliably without manual intervention for [user, ie me]." Success is the job-gets-done; growth and MAU are inapplicable.

**Default KR mix.** Heavily *correctness* and *operability* KRs (universal Principle 6 — operability is a functional requirement). No adoption KRs.

- **Correctness KR**: "zero false negatives in 30 days" / "every new listing matching criteria reaches me within 1 hour."
- **No-silent-failure KR**: "every job failure produces a visible alert within 10 minutes; zero silent failures in 30 days."
- **Availability KR**: "≥99% successful scheduled-run completion across 30 days" (SLO appropriate to a personal tool).
- **Maintenance burden KR** (occasionally): "no manual intervention required for 30 days" — useful when the project has been needy.

**Concrete worked example** (for nestl):

```
Goal:           For Anton, make Dutch-housing-listing monitoring reliable enough
                that I find out about every matching listing within 1 hour
                without checking the app.
Key results:    1. zero missed listings (false negatives) in a 30-day window,
                   measured by spot-checking source sites weekly
                2. every job failure produces a visible push notification within
                   10 minutes; zero silent failures in 30 days
                3. ≥99% of scheduled job runs complete successfully across 30 days
                4. zero manual interventions (restart, redeploy, fix) required
                   over 30 days
Affected repos: nestl
Appetite:       ~6 issues
```

**Anti-patterns for this type.**

- "Add user registration" — wrong product type; this is single-user.
- "Improve UI" — unmeasurable and probably not the bottleneck.
- "Increase MAU" — vanity metric; the user is one person.
- "Ship Rails 8 upgrade" — that's an issue (or KTLO), not an outcome.

**Verification rubric.** Every KR points at something the running system emits (logs, alerts, telemetry) or that can be spot-checked against the source of truth (the actual housing site). If the KR isn't backed by an inspectable signal, add the instrumentation before the cycle starts.

### Type 3 — Utility skill pack (reusable micro-tools)

*Example: agent-skills (resell-au, garage-sale).*

**Objective shape.** "For [me, when doing recurring task X], make the skill produce a correct, immediately-usable artefact." Success is *per-invocation utility*, not adoption breadth.

**Default KR mix.** *Output-quality* KRs (does the artefact work first-shot?) and *first-shot success rate* KRs.

- **First-shot correctness KR**: "in last 5 invocations of resell-au, generated listing required ≤1 edit before posting."
- **Coverage KR**: "for ≥X of the last Y use cases, the skill handled the task end-to-end without requiring me to drop to manual."
- **Time-to-result KR**: occasionally — "skill produces a usable artefact in <N minutes from invocation." Use sparingly; if speed isn't the bottleneck, this becomes vanity.

**Concrete worked example** (for resell-au):

```
Goal:           For Anton selling stuff in Australia, make resell-au produce a
                ready-to-post Facebook Marketplace listing that I post without
                editing — for any item I throw at it.
Key results:    1. in next 10 invocations, ≥8 listings posted without edits
                   (only acceptable edit: photo selection)
                2. in next 10 invocations, ≥9 prices are within ±10% of what the
                   item actually sold for (or got serious offers within 7 days)
                3. zero invocations where the skill fails to handle the category
                   and I have to write the listing manually
                4. for ≥3 batch-pricing runs, the skill handles a folder of 5+
                   items end-to-end with no per-item intervention
Affected repos: agent-skills
Appetite:       ~7 issues
```

**Anti-patterns for this type.**

- "Add more skills to the pack" — output, not outcome. More skills doesn't mean better.
- "Improve skill descriptions" — unmeasurable in isolation; the test is whether they trigger correctly, which is a Type-1 invocation-rate KR.
- "Run resell-au 20 times this cycle" — activity, not value. Hitting 20 with bad outputs is a failure.
- "Generate prettier listings" — aesthetic, not testable.

**Verification rubric.** Every KR has a *use record* attached — an artefact log of recent invocations with outcomes (posted / edited / failed / sold-price). The skill produces evidence for its own KRs by being used.

### Type 4 — Research + concept / thesis-driven

*Example: em-os.*

**Objective shape.** "Make [thesis or concept] understood and adopted by [audience]." Or, when not yet ready for adoption: "Make [open research question] either resolved or definitively bounded." Knowledge KRs, not output KRs.

**Default KR mix.** *Knowledge KRs* ("we now know X / can defend X / have ruled out Y") and *artefact-clarity KRs* ("the core argument fits on one page and survives critique from N reviewers"). Adoption KRs are valid but later-stage.

- **Knowledge KR**: "we can answer [open question 1, 2, 3] with sourced evidence and a stated confidence." Borrows from Gilad's Confidence Meter — going from "opinion" (0.1) to "data" (≥0.5) is the move.
- **Position KR**: "the thesis has a 1-page statement that 3 informed readers can summarise back accurately without reading the long version."
- **Survives-critique KR**: "the thesis has been challenged by N domain reviewers and either updated or successfully defended on each point."
- **Adoption KR** (later): "the thesis is cited or referenced by ≥N external sources" — only if external adoption is actually the goal.

**Concrete worked example** (for em-os, hypothetically):

```
Goal:           For people building agentic systems, make the em-os thesis on
                [core claim] clear, defensible, and adoptable.
Key results:    1. one-page thesis statement exists that 3 informed readers
                   can summarise back accurately without reading the long form
                2. the 5 most-likely critiques are pre-rebutted in writing,
                   each citing ≥1 named counter-argument
                3. ≥3 demo artefacts exist that instantiate the thesis and
                   show concretely what it implies (not just describes)
                4. at least 1 external reader has engaged substantively
                   (debate, citation, application) — not just acknowledged
Affected repos: em-os
Appetite:       ~9 issues
```

**Anti-patterns for this type.**

- "Publish 5 blog posts about em-os" — activity, not knowledge change.
- "Write the full long-form thesis" — output, not adoption. A clear short-form usually beats a sprawling long-form.
- "Get to 10,000 readers" — vanity unless reach is genuinely the objective; usually the goal is depth of one reader, not breadth.
- "Iterate the thesis until it's perfect" — no exit condition; Wodtke's "trackable weekly" test fails.

**Verification rubric.** A research KR is good if it names what would *change* about the world's (or the author's) state of knowledge when the KR is hit. "Wrote 3 essays" doesn't change knowledge state; "the 5 critiques are pre-rebutted with sourced counters" does. Test: can a fresh agent six months from now look at the artefacts and tell whether the KR was hit?

### Type 5 — Equity research tooling

*Example: stock-screen, stock-signal, stock-model, stock-timing.*

This is the type where the OKR bar is most different from product work. The discipline borrows directly from Philip Tetlock's *Superforecasting* and *Expert Political Judgment* — forecasting accuracy is what matters, not throughput of analyses.

**Objective shape.** "Make [forecast / decision] calibrated, pre-registered, and reviewed." Success is *forecasting accuracy improves over time*, not *more reports generated*.

**Default KR mix.** *Calibration KRs* (forecasts land in range), *pre-registration KRs* (predictions were written down before outcomes were known), *decision-quality KRs* (did the decision derived from the forecast turn out well), and *brier-score-style accuracy KRs*.

- **Calibration KR** (lagging, the most important): "for ≥5 of 7 covered tickers, the 12-month price range produced by `/stock-model` bracketed the actual price 12 months later."
- **Pre-registration KR**: "every BUY/WATCH/AVOID call from `/stock-signal` was logged to a dated file at the moment it was made; zero post-hoc rationalisation."
- **Hit-rate KR**: "of the BUY calls made 12+ months ago, ≥X% are now above the entry price by ≥Y%; of AVOID calls, ≥Z% are flat or down."
- **Decision-quality KR**: "for each of the last N decisions, a written postmortem exists comparing model output, action taken, and outcome."
- **Process KR** (input, sparingly): "every covered ticker has an updated `/stock-screen` and `/stock-signal` cached within the last 30 days." Acceptable here because process freshness is a precondition for calibration — but only if it doesn't displace the lagging KRs.

**Concrete worked example** (for the equity research portfolio):

```
Goal:           For Anton, make the 7-ticker equity-research portfolio produce
                forecasts and decisions whose calibration improves cycle over
                cycle — not just more reports.
Key results:    1. for ≥5 of 7 covered tickers, the 12-month price band from
                   the most recent /stock-model run bracketed the actual price
                   at the 12-month mark
                2. every BUY/WATCH/AVOID call from /stock-signal is logged to
                   a dated, immutable reports/ file at the moment of call;
                   zero retroactive edits in 90 days
                3. of BUY calls made ≥12 months ago, ≥60% are above entry
                   by ≥10%; of AVOID calls, ≥70% are flat or down
                4. a calibration-postmortem exists for ≥3 closed positions
                   this cycle — comparing model output, decision, outcome,
                   and what the model would need to change to have been right
Affected repos: stock-review
Appetite:       ~10 issues
```

**Anti-patterns for this type.**

- "Run /stock-screen on 20 new tickers" — throughput vanity. More tickers covered = more attention diluted, not more calibration.
- "Update /stock-model with new financial data" — KTLO masquerading as outcome. Belongs on the issue, not on the OKR.
- "BUY calls outperform the S&P" — un-pre-registered, easy to cherry-pick on. The pre-registration KR (KR2 above) is what makes this honest.
- "Improve model accuracy" — unmeasurable without a calibration target and a time window.

**Verification rubric.** Every KR points at the structured outputs the equity skills already produce (`reports/TICKER_YYYYMMDD.json`). The data source is the cached report file, dated and immutable. A KR is good if a fresh agent could grade it 12 months later by reading the cached reports — without any narration from the original analyst. This is the Tetlock standard: forecasting calibration is only meaningful when the predictions are dated and unchangeable.

### Type 6 — Production / customer-facing product (anticipated)

**Objective shape.** "For [customer segment], make [valuable behaviour] happen more, retain longer, or convert at higher rate." Standard product-OKR territory.

**Default KR mix.** Standard product-OKR mix — activation, retention, conversion, NPS — with strong preference for *retention* (the lagging indicator most predictive of product-market-fit) and explicit Cagan-style *outcome* framing.

- **Activation KR**: "≥X% of new users complete [activation event] within first session."
- **Retention KR** (the north star): "Day-30 retention ≥X%, up from baseline Y%."
- **Conversion KR**: "[funnel step] converts at ≥X%, up from baseline Y%."
- **Quality KR** (paired, to prevent vanity gains): "NPS ≥X among activated users" or "p95 latency ≤Xms."

**Worked example** — deferred until a real customer-facing product exists in the portfolio. The OKR template is well-trodden territory once the product type is present; flagging it here for completeness.

**Anti-patterns for this type.**

- "Ship feature X to 100% of users" — Cagan's exact critique: output, not outcome.
- "Increase MAU" — vanity unless paired with retention.
- "Improve user satisfaction" — unmeasurable without an instrument (NPS, CSAT, behavioural proxy).
- "Get to product-market-fit" — Objective, not KR; needs the leading indicator underneath it.

**Verification rubric.** Every KR has a baseline number from production telemetry, a target, and a time window. The telemetry instrument exists *before* the cycle begins — if you can't measure the baseline today, the cycle's first issue is to instrument it, not to ship the feature.

## Part D — Cross-cutting playbook rules

Universal rules that apply across all six project types. These should appear in `initiative-shape` regardless of project type.

1. **Every KR is inspectable in the system, not felt by the team.** A fresh agent in the next session must be able to grade the KR by looking at an artefact (file, log, telemetry, cached report, Linear state). "We feel like we made progress" is not a KR. (Wodtke's "trackable weekly"; agentic Principle 7 — memory lives in artefacts.)
2. **Every KR specifies baseline AND target.** "Reduce X" without naming the current X is unscoreable. If the baseline isn't known, the first issue in the initiative is to measure it.
3. **Every KR has a time window.** Even for personal projects: "in the next 30 days," "by end of cycle," "across the next 4 cycles." Without a window, a KR cannot be closed.
4. **Distinguish what we'll KNOW vs what we'll SHIP vs what will HAPPEN.** Research projects KNOW; build projects SHIP; outcome-bearing projects HAVE THINGS HAPPEN. Mixing these is the single most common KR-shape error in solo work.
5. **The Objective is qualitative; the KRs do the measuring.** If the Objective sentence has numbers in it, you've collapsed Objective into KRs and lost the directional layer. (Lamorte.)
6. **3 KRs is the default; 5 is the cap.** More than 5 = no focus (Wodtke). Fewer than 3 risks collapsing to a single arbitrary threshold.
7. **Define what "killing" means for this initiative.** When do you stop? For Type-1 (skill pack): "if the skill doesn't fire correctly after 2 iteration cycles, retire it." For Type-2 (personal product): "if the underlying need disappears." For Type-4 (research): "if the thesis is decisively falsified or the field moves past it." For Type-5 (equity): "if calibration doesn't improve over 4 cycles, the model is wrong, not just unlucky." A KR-bearing initiative without a kill condition becomes a zombie.
8. **Pre-register the KRs.** Write them down, dated, before the work begins (Wodtke step 1; Tetlock for research-type work). Retroactively adjusting KRs to match outcomes is the loudest possible signal of self-deception — and in a solo single-author portfolio, no one else will catch it.
9. **One owner per KR — even if the owner is always you.** For solo work this looks redundant, but naming "owner: Anton" makes it explicit that there is no diffusion of responsibility — no "someone will get to it." (Doerr.)
10. **Weekly confidence rating, even solo.** Wodtke's Friday-confidence-score practice translates to solo work as a `confidence: 0–10` field on each KR, updated at the end of each cycle. When confidence drops without an event explaining it, the KR is probably wrong.

## Part E — Mapping OKRs to the four-field initiative format

The user's `linear-workflow.md` already has the four-field initiative format (goal / key results / affected repos / appetite). The question is how heavyweight the OKR machinery should sit on top of it given:

- single-author portfolio
- 5–15 issue appetites (one cycle slot ≈ 9–12 issues)
- 3–4 working day cycles
- no team alignment problem (the canonical reason for OKRs)

**Recommendation 1 — Per-initiative OKRs, not quarterly.** The Doerr/Wodtke quarterly cadence assumes team-level alignment across many initiatives. For solo work with 3 initiatives per ~5-day cycle, **each initiative carries its own OKR**, and the OKR's time window matches the initiative's lifecycle (often: this cycle + the next, for outcome KRs that need observation time). Quarterly OKRs would add a second layer of bookkeeping for marginal value.

**Recommendation 2 — 3 KRs is the right default, 5 the cap, 2 acceptable only with explicit justification.** Wodtke's 3-KR default fits small-appetite work better than Lamorte's 3–5 range. For 5–8 issue ("small") initiatives, 2 KRs may be appropriate — but only if the second KR is genuinely orthogonal to the first (eg one correctness + one operability) rather than a softer restatement.

**Recommendation 3 — Mix committed and aspirational on a per-KR basis, not per-Objective.** Doerr's split assumed organisational scale; for solo work the cleaner pattern is to label KRs individually:

- **Committed KRs** must hit 1.0 (operability, no-silent-failure, pre-registration, baseline tracking).
- **Aspirational KRs** land at 0.6–0.7 (outcome KRs, behaviour change, forecast calibration).

The user's existing "appetite" is closer to a **scope cap** than to an OKR stretch/commit signal — it answers "how much will we spend" not "how hard are we trying." Keep appetite as is; layer commit/aspirational labelling on individual KRs.

**Recommendation 4 — Initiative-shape should branch by project type.** The skill currently asks one question: "What 3–5 observable states would tell you it worked?" This is too generic. The right shape is: (a) ask the project type first, (b) load the type-specific KR mix and anti-patterns from this report, (c) probe the KR types appropriate to the project type, (d) verify against the type-specific rubric. Same four-field output, but the prose and the probes change by type. This is the single highest-leverage change to the skill.

**Recommendation 5 — Add baseline-and-window fields to each KR.** The current template ("observable state — binary pass/fail, fitness function firing, or measurable delta") allows degenerate KRs that name a state without a baseline or a window. Tighten to: `KR: [observable state] | baseline: [current value/state] | target: [target value/state] | window: [time frame] | type: committed | aspirational`. This adds 10 seconds of work per KR and removes the most common KR-shape failures wholesale.

[observable state]\n                2. [observable state]\n                3. [observable state]\n                (3–5 KRs total)\nAffected repos: [list]\nAppetite:       ~[N] issues\n```\n\nThe skill produces one criterion; the governance model requires 3–5 KRs. This is the most load-bearing gap and probably explains most of the weak outputs.\n\n### 2.2 Other concrete weaknesses (7)\n\n1. **No \"verifiable by system inspection\" enforcement.** The skill allows \"measure by team feedback\" or sentiment signals; the governance model wants checks against the system itself.\n2. **No baseline → target delta requirement.** \"Latency < 100ms\" passes; \"latency moves from 500ms to 100ms\" is tighter but not required.\n3. **No OKR vocabulary in the template.** \"Goal\" and \"Success criterion\" are not mapped to \"Objective\" and \"Key Result.\" A fresh LLM following SKILL.md doesn't know it should produce OKR-shaped output.\n4. **No project-type differentiation.** Same template for platform work, product features, research, equity tooling.\n5. **\"Ready\" is underspecified.** Step 6 says \"Does this capture the initiative correctly?\" with no rubric for judging KR quality.\n6. **No fitness-function framing.** The governance model says KRs should be \"binary pass/fail, fitness function firing, or measurable delta\" — the skill template doesn't teach or require this form.\n7. **Common rationalisations table is good but incomplete.** It anticipates \"improve X\" vagueness and solution-naming, but not single-KR-instead-of-3-5, not Kano must-be confusion, not Tetlock-style pre-registration for research-type work.\n\n### 2.3 What the skill IS good at\n\n- Forces problem framing rather than solution framing\n- Strong gate at Step 6 (user-confirmation before project creation)\n- Enforces appetite in issue counts rather than time\n- Catches \"improve X\" vagueness in red-flags\n- Produces a structured Linear project description\n\n---\n\n## Section 3 — OKR canon (compressed)\n\n### 3.1 Practitioners\n\n**Andy Grove, *High Output Management* (1983).** Original OKR formulation at Intel. Reframed Drucker's MBOs by separating direction (Objective, qualitative) from milestones (KRs, measurable). \"Did I do that or did I not do it? Yes? No? Simple. No judgements in it.\" Introduced numerical KR grading (0.0–1.0) with ~0.7 as the target for stretch — full attainment means the KR was not ambitious enough.\n\n**John Doerr, *Measure What Matters* (2018).** Brought OKRs from Intel to Google.\n- **Committed vs aspirational OKRs.** Committed must hit 1.0 (operational commitments). Aspirational expected at 0.6–0.7. Conflating these is the most common org-level failure mode.\n- **CFRs — Conversations, Feedback, Recognition.** Without continuous cadence, KRs calcify. Weekly review, not quarterly.\n\n**Christina Wodtke, *Radical Focus* (2016, 2nd ed. 2021).** The most operationally useful guide for small teams.\n- \"OKRs are a commitment to focus. Focus is the thing OKRs buy you.\"\n- KRs must be hard, time-bound, and **trackable weekly**.\n- **One Objective, three KRs** is her default (against Lamorte's 3–5). For small teams, more than 3 isn't focus.\n- OKRs are a **scoreboard, not a to-do list**. A KR that reads like a task is a category error.\n- **Friday confidence rating**: each KR rated 1–10 weekly. The conversation about why confidence dropped is the value.\n\n**Ben Lamorte, *The OKRs Field Book* (2022).**\n- 3–5 KRs per Objective; strong preference for 3.\n- **Value vs activity.** \"Publish 5 blog posts\" is activity. \"10% increase in organic signups from blog traffic\" is value.\n- Objectives are qualitative; KRs are quantitative.\n\n**Felipe Castro — input vs output vs outcome.**\n- **Input KRs**: what you'll do. Usually wrong.\n- **Output KRs**: what you'll produce. Sometimes appropriate, easily becomes feature-factory.\n- **Outcome KRs**: what changes in the world. Default level for OKRs.\n- Rule: pick the highest level you can directly measure within the cycle.\n\n**SMART goals — Doran (1981).** Predates OKRs by two decades. SMART describes the quality of any KR. **OKRs ⊃ SMART**: every well-formed KR is SMART; not every SMART goal is an OKR.\n\n**Philip Tetlock, *Superforecasting* (2015) and *Expert Political Judgment* (2005).** For research and forecasting work specifically. Calibration matters more than accuracy on any single call; pre-registration of predictions is what makes the discipline honest. The Brier-score view: forecasts should be dated, immutable, and graded against outcomes.\n\n### 3.2 Counter-perspectives\n\n- **Marty Cagan (SVPG).** OKRs in feature-factory teams become outputs in disguise: \"ship Feature X\" dressed up as a KR. Fix: KRs must be customer or business outcomes, never team activity.\n- **Itamar Gilad (GIST — Goals/Ideas/Steps/Tasks).** OKRs need a discovery layer beneath them: Ideas (hypothesised ways to move the goal, ranked by ICE confidence), Steps (validation experiments), Tasks (implementation). For a solo author, this prevents OKRs from collapsing into a quarterly to-do list.\n- **Will Larson (*An Elegant Puzzle*, *Staff Engineer*).** Sceptical that full OKR machinery is worth its weight for very small teams; prefers strategy docs that name a small number of bets and what evidence would disprove each. Worth taking seriously for a single-author portfolio.\n\n### 3.3 Common OKR failure modes\n\n| Failure | What it looks like | Antidote |\n|---|---|---|\n| Sandbagging | Trivial targets to guarantee 100% | Doerr's committed/aspirational split; aspirational expected at 0.7 |\n| Output disguised as outcome | \"Ship X\" instead of \"X drives Y behaviour change\" | Cagan's test — could you hit it without anything changing for the user? |\n| Activity KR | \"Hold 5 interviews,\" \"run 3 experiments\" | Name what you'll **know** afterwards |\n| Too many KRs | More than 5 = checklist, not focus | Wodtke's 3-KR default |\n| Stale KRs | Set once, never revisited | Weekly confidence rating |\n| Cross-team KR with no owner | \"We will…\" with no named owner | One named owner per KR (even if it's always you) |\n| Vanity metrics | MAU, page views, downloads | Pair with retention or correctness |\n| Performance-review misuse | OKR attainment tied to bonus | Decouple OKRs from comp (or self-judgement for solo) |\n\n---\n\n## Section 4 — Project-type playbooks\n\nFor each of the 6 observed types: Objective shape, default KR mix, worked example, anti-patterns specific to the type, verification rubric.\n\n---\n\n### Type 1 — Methodology skill pack\n\n*Example: `pde-skills`. Markdown-encoded decision rules invoked by humans or agents.*\n\n**Objective shape.** \"For [agent or human invoker], make [decision moment] happen correctly without prompting.\" About **invocation quality at the right moment**, not about authoring more skills.\n\n**Default KR mix.** Outcome-weighted, with invocation-rate KRs as leading indicator and decision-quality KRs as lagging.\n\n- **Invocation-rate KR** (leading): does the skill fire at the right moment? Eg \"plan-review fires before approval in ≥80% of sampled plan-approval moments.\" Testable against transcript history.\n- **Decision-quality KR** (lagging): when it fires, does the outcome improve? Eg \"in ≥4 of 5 sampled invocations, plan-review surfaced an issue that would otherwise have shipped.\"\n- **Adoption KR** (output, sparingly): \"skill is installed in N repos.\" Only if cross-repo adoption is the actual goal.\n\n**Worked example — `/initiative-shape` itself:**\n```\nGoal:           For Anton, make initiatives properly shaped before they enter\n                a cycle — so cycle planning has goal+KR-bearing initiatives\n                rather than repo-aliased backlogs.\nKey results:    1. [committed] initiative-shape fires (or is offered) in\n                   ≥80% of \"new initiative\" moments across the next 4 cycles\n                   — measured by sampling transcript history (rtk discover)\n                2. [committed] in ≥4 of the last 5 created initiatives, all\n                   4 fields (goal/KRs/repos/appetite) populated and pass the\n                   verification rubric in Section 5\n                3. [aspirational] zero Linear projects created in the next 4\n                   cycles that are repo-aliased rather than outcome-named\n                4. [aspirational] at end of next 2 cycles, ≥3 of 4 retro\n                   conversations cite a KR (not \"did we ship X\")\nAffected repos: pde-skills\nAppetite:       ~8 issues\nWindow:         next 4 cycles (~16 working days)\nKill condition: if KR2 not hit within 2 cycles of skill update, the new\n                template is wrong, not the operator\n```\n\n**Anti-patterns for this type.**\n- \"Author 5 new skills this cycle\" — output, not outcome\n- \"Update SKILL.md for X\" — task, not KR; belongs on the issue\n- \"Improve skill quality\" — unmeasurable; Wodtke's weekly-trackable test fails\n- \"100% test coverage of skill verification rubrics\" — vanity coverage; what matters is whether the rubric catches failures, not whether every line ran\n\n**Verification rubric.** A KR is good if a fresh agent in the next session could grade it by: (a) named data source (transcript history, Linear project list, sampled invocations); (b) named sample method; (c) recorded baseline; (d) numeric target; (e) gradable 0.0–1.0.\n\n---\n\n### Type 2 — Personal product (single-user, running)\n\n*Example: `nestl`. Complete deployed software solving a recurring problem for one user.*\n\n**Objective shape.** \"Make [recurring job] happen reliably without manual intervention for [user, ie me].\" Job-gets-done; **growth and MAU are inapplicable**.\n\n**Default KR mix.** Heavily correctness and operability KRs. No adoption KRs.\n\n- **Correctness KR**: \"zero false negatives in 30 days\" / \"every new matching listing reaches me within 1 hour.\"\n- **No-silent-failure KR**: \"every job failure produces a visible alert within 10 minutes; zero silent failures in 30 days.\"\n- **Availability KR**: \"≥99% successful scheduled-run completion across 30 days\" — SLO scaled to a personal tool.\n- **Maintenance burden KR**: \"zero manual interventions (restart, redeploy) required for 30 days\" — when the project has been needy.\n\n**Worked example — `nestl` parent (currently empty description):**\n```\nGoal:           For Anton, make Dutch-housing-listing monitoring reliable\n                enough that I find out about every matching listing within\n                1 hour without checking the app.\nKey results:    1. [committed] zero missed listings (false negatives) in\n                   a 30-day window, measured by weekly spot-check of source\n                   sites against nestl's seen-listings table\n                2. [committed] every job failure produces a visible push\n                   notification within 10 minutes; zero silent failures in\n                   30 days, measured by job-failure log vs notification log\n                3. [committed] ≥99% of scheduled job runs complete\n                   successfully across 30 days, measured by Solid Queue\n                   run history\n                4. [aspirational] zero manual interventions (restart,\n                   redeploy, fix) required over 30 days\nAffected repos: nestl\nAppetite:       ~6 issues\nWindow:         30-day observation post-instrumentation\nKill condition: if the underlying housing need disappears, archive the app\n```\n\n**Anti-patterns for this type.**\n- \"Add user registration\" — wrong product type; this is single-user\n- \"Improve UI\" — unmeasurable and probably not the bottleneck\n- \"Increase MAU\" — vanity metric; user is one person\n- \"Ship Rails 8 upgrade\" — KTLO issue, not an outcome KR\n\n**Verification rubric.** Every KR points at something the running system emits (logs, alerts, telemetry) or can be spot-checked against source of truth (the housing site itself). If the KR isn't backed by an inspectable signal, the first issue in the initiative is to add the instrumentation — not to ship the feature.\n\n---\n\n### Type 3 — Utility skill pack\n\n*Example: `agent-skills` (`resell-au`, `garage-sale`, `codex-primary-runtime`). Flat collection of callable skills solving discrete tasks.*\n\n**Objective shape.** \"For [me, when doing recurring task X], make the skill produce a correct, immediately-usable artefact.\" **Per-invocation utility**, not adoption breadth.\n\n**Default KR mix.** Output-quality KRs (does the artefact work first-shot?) and coverage KRs.\n\n- **First-shot correctness KR**: \"in last 5 invocations of resell-au, generated listing required ≤1 edit before posting.\"\n- **Coverage KR**: \"for ≥X of last Y use cases, the skill handled the task end-to-end without dropping to manual.\"\n- **Time-to-result KR** (sparingly): only if speed is the bottleneck; otherwise vanity.\n\n**Worked example — `resell-au`:**\n```\nGoal:           For Anton selling stuff in Australia, make resell-au produce\n                a ready-to-post Facebook Marketplace listing that I post\n                without editing — for any item I throw at it.\nKey results:    1. [committed] in next 10 invocations, ≥8 listings posted\n                   without text edits (acceptable edit: photo selection only)\n                2. [aspirational] in next 10 invocations, ≥9 prices within\n                   ±10% of what the item actually sold for (or got serious\n                   offers within 7 days)\n                3. [committed] zero invocations where the skill fails to\n                   handle the category and I have to write the listing by hand\n                4. [aspirational] for ≥3 batch-pricing runs, the skill\n                   handles a folder of 5+ items end-to-end with no per-item\n                   intervention\nAffected repos: agent-skills\nAppetite:       ~7 issues\nWindow:         next 10 listings (rolling)\nKill condition: if KR3 fails twice in a quarter, the category-detection is\n                wrong, not the operator\n```\n\n**Anti-patterns for this type.**\n- \"Add more skills to the pack\" — output, not outcome\n- \"Improve skill descriptions\" — Type-1 invocation-rate KR, not utility KR\n- \"Run resell-au 20 times this cycle\" — activity; 20 with bad outputs is failure\n- \"Generate prettier listings\" — aesthetic, not testable\n\n**Verification rubric.** Every KR has a use-record attached — an artefact log of recent invocations with outcomes (posted / edited / failed / sold-price). The skill **produces evidence for its own KRs by being used**.\n\n---\n\n### Type 4 — Research / thesis-driven\n\n*Example: `em-os` (paused).*\n\n**Objective shape.** \"Make [thesis or concept] understood and adopted by [audience].\" Or earlier-stage: \"Make [open research question] resolved or definitively bounded.\" **Knowledge KRs, not output KRs.**\n\n**Default KR mix.** Knowledge KRs + artefact-clarity KRs. Adoption KRs valid but later-stage.\n\n- **Knowledge KR**: \"we can answer [open questions 1, 2, 3] with sourced evidence and stated confidence.\" Borrows Gilad's Confidence Meter — going from \"opinion\" (0.1) to \"data\" (≥0.5) is the move.\n- **Position KR**: \"thesis has a 1-page statement that 3 informed readers can summarise back accurately without reading the long version.\"\n- **Survives-critique KR**: \"thesis has been challenged by N domain reviewers and either updated or successfully defended on each point.\"\n- **Adoption KR** (later-stage): \"thesis is cited or referenced by ≥N external sources\" — only if external adoption is actually the goal.\n\n**Worked example — `em-os` (when it resumes):**\n```\nGoal:           For people building agentic systems, make the em-os thesis\n                on [core claim] clear, defensible, and adoptable.\nKey results:    1. [committed] one-page thesis statement exists that 3\n                   informed readers can summarise back accurately without\n                   reading the long form\n                2. [committed] the 5 most-likely critiques are pre-rebutted\n                   in writing, each citing ≥1 named counter-argument\n                3. [aspirational] ≥3 demo artefacts exist that instantiate\n                   the thesis and show concretely what it implies (not\n                   just describes)\n                4. [aspirational] at least 1 external reader has engaged\n                   substantively (debate, citation, application) — not\n                   just acknowledged\nAffected repos: em-os\nAppetite:       ~9 issues\nWindow:         this cycle + 2 observation cycles\nKill condition: if KR2 produces a critique that decisively falsifies the\n                thesis, archive the project as a learning artefact\n```\n\n**Anti-patterns for this type.**\n- \"Publish 5 blog posts about em-os\" — activity, not knowledge change\n- \"Write the full long-form thesis\" — output, not adoption; clear short-form usually beats sprawling long-form\n- \"Get to 10,000 readers\" — vanity unless reach is genuinely the objective; usually goal is depth of one reader, not breadth\n- \"Iterate the thesis until it's perfect\" — no exit condition; Wodtke's weekly-trackable test fails\n\n**Verification rubric.** A research KR is good if it names what would **change** about the world's (or the author's) state of knowledge. \"Wrote 3 essays\" doesn't change knowledge state; \"5 critiques pre-rebutted with sourced counters\" does. Can a fresh agent six months from now look at the artefacts and tell whether the KR was hit?\n\n*Note: em-os is the only project of this type observed. The playbook is high-fidelity but low-replication. Revisit when em-os resumes or a second thesis project appears.*\n\n---\n\n### Type 5 — Equity research tooling\n\n*Example: `stock-screen`, `stock-signal`, `stock-model`, `stock-timing`, `stock-portfolio`.*\n\nThe OKR bar is most different from product work here. The discipline borrows directly from Tetlock: **forecasting accuracy and calibration are what matter**, not throughput of analyses.\n\n**Objective shape.** \"Make [forecast / decision] calibrated, pre-registered, and reviewed.\" Success is *forecasting accuracy improves cycle over cycle*, not *more reports generated*.\n\n**Default KR mix.** Calibration KRs + pre-registration KRs + decision-quality KRs + Brier-style accuracy KRs.\n\n- **Calibration KR** (lagging, most important): \"for ≥5 of 7 covered tickers, the 12-month price range from `/stock-model` bracketed the actual price 12 months later.\"\n- **Pre-registration KR**: \"every BUY/WATCH/AVOID call from `/stock-signal` was logged to a dated file at the moment of call; zero post-hoc rationalisation.\"\n- **Hit-rate KR**: \"of BUY calls made ≥12 months ago, ≥X% are now above entry by ≥Y%; of AVOID calls, ≥Z% are flat or down.\"\n- **Decision-quality KR**: \"for each of last N decisions, a written postmortem compares model output, action taken, and outcome.\"\n- **Process KR** (input, sparingly): \"every covered ticker has updated `/stock-screen` and `/stock-signal` cached within last 30 days.\" Acceptable only if process freshness is a precondition for calibration AND doesn't displace lagging KRs.\n\n**Worked example — equity portfolio overall:**\n```\nGoal:           For Anton, make the 7-ticker equity-research portfolio\n                produce forecasts and decisions whose calibration improves\n                cycle over cycle — not just more reports.\nKey results:    1. [aspirational] for ≥5 of 7 covered tickers, the 12-month\n                   price band from the most recent /stock-model run\n                   bracketed the actual price at the 12-month mark\n                2. [committed] every BUY/WATCH/AVOID call from /stock-signal\n                   is logged to a dated, immutable reports/ file at the\n                   moment of call; zero retroactive edits in 90 days\n                3. [aspirational] of BUY calls made ≥12 months ago, ≥60%\n                   above entry by ≥10%; of AVOID calls, ≥70% flat or down\n                4. [committed] a calibration-postmortem exists for ≥3\n                   closed positions this cycle — comparing model output,\n                   decision, outcome, and what the model would have needed\n                   to change to be right\nAffected repos: stock-review\nAppetite:       ~10 issues\nWindow:         12-month rolling for KR1, KR3; 90-day for KR2; per-cycle for KR4\nKill condition: if KR1 doesn't move toward target across 4 consecutive\n                cycles, the model is structurally wrong (not just unlucky)\n```\n\n**Anti-patterns for this type.**\n- \"Run /stock-screen on 20 new tickers\" — throughput vanity; more tickers = more attention diluted\n- \"Update /stock-model with new financial data\" — KTLO masquerading as outcome\n- \"BUY calls outperform the S&P\" — un-pre-registered, easy to cherry-pick. KR2 above is what makes this honest.\n- \"Improve model accuracy\" — unmeasurable without calibration target and time window\n\n**Verification rubric.** Every KR points at the structured outputs the equity skills already produce (`reports/TICKER_YYYYMMDD.json`). Data source = cached report file, dated and immutable. A KR is good if a fresh agent could grade it 12 months later by reading the cached reports — **without any narration from the original analyst**. This is the Tetlock standard.\n\n---\n\n### Type 6 — Production / customer-facing (anticipated)\n\n*Not yet present in this portfolio. Included for completeness so the skill can recognise the type when it appears.*\n\n**Objective shape.** \"For [customer segment], make [valuable behaviour] happen more, retain longer, or convert at higher rate.\" Standard product-OKR territory.\n\n**Default KR mix.** Activation + retention + conversion + paired quality. Strong preference for **retention** as the lagging indicator most predictive of product-market-fit; explicit Cagan-style outcome framing.\n\n- **Activation KR**: \"≥X% of new users complete [activation event] within first session.\"\n- **Retention KR** (north star): \"Day-30 retention ≥X%, up from baseline Y%.\"\n- **Conversion KR**: \"[funnel step] converts at ≥X%, up from baseline Y%.\"\n- **Quality KR** (paired to prevent vanity gains): \"NPS ≥X among activated users\" or \"p95 latency ≤Xms.\"\n\n**Anti-patterns for this type.**\n- \"Ship feature X to 100% of users\" — Cagan's exact critique: output, not outcome\n- \"Increase MAU\" — vanity unless paired with retention\n- \"Improve user satisfaction\" — unmeasurable without an instrument\n- \"Get to product-market-fit\" — Objective, not KR; needs leading indicator underneath it\n\n**Verification rubric.** Every KR has a baseline number from production telemetry, a target, and a window. The telemetry **exists before the cycle begins**. If baseline isn't measurable today, the first issue is to instrument — not to ship the feature.\n\n---\n\n## Section 5 — Cross-cutting playbook rules\n\nUniversal across all 6 types. These should be the verification rubric in the updated `/initiative-shape` skill.\n\n1. **Every KR is inspectable in the system, not felt by the team.** A fresh agent in next session must be able to grade by looking at an artefact (file, log, telemetry, cached report, Linear state). \"We feel like we made progress\" is not a KR.\n2. **Every KR specifies baseline AND target.** \"Reduce X\" without naming current X is unscoreable. If baseline isn't known, the first issue in the initiative is to measure it.\n3. **Every KR has a time window.** \"In the next 30 days,\" \"by end of cycle,\" \"across next 4 cycles.\" Without a window, a KR cannot be closed.\n4. **Distinguish what we'll KNOW vs what we'll SHIP vs what will HAPPEN.** Research projects KNOW; build projects SHIP; outcome-bearing projects HAVE THINGS HAPPEN. Mixing these is the most common KR-shape error in solo work.\n5. **The Objective is qualitative; the KRs do the measuring.** If the Objective sentence has numbers in it, Objective has collapsed into KRs and the directional layer is lost (Lamorte).\n6. **3 KRs is the default; 5 is the cap.** More than 5 = no focus (Wodtke). Fewer than 3 risks collapsing to a single arbitrary threshold.\n7. **Define what \"killing\" means for this initiative.** When do you stop? A KR-bearing initiative without a kill condition becomes a zombie.\n8. **Pre-register the KRs.** Write them down, dated, before the work begins (Wodtke step 1; Tetlock for research-type work). Retroactively adjusting KRs to match outcomes is the loudest possible signal of self-deception — and in a solo single-author portfolio, no one else will catch it.\n9. **One owner per KR — even if owner is always you.** Naming \"owner: Anton\" makes it explicit that there is no diffusion of responsibility.\n10. **Weekly (or per-cycle) confidence rating, even solo.** Wodtke's Friday-confidence-score translated to solo work: a `confidence: 0–10` field on each KR, updated at cycle close. Confidence dropping without an event explaining it = the KR is probably wrong.\n\n---\n\n## Section 6 — Cadence and mapping recommendations\n\n### 6.1 Per-initiative OKRs (recommended; confirmed)\n\nEach Linear initiative carries its own OKR. Time window = initiative lifecycle (often current cycle + observation cycle). No second quarterly layer.\n\nRationale:\n- Single-author portfolio; the canonical reason for quarterly OKRs (cross-team alignment) doesn't apply\n- 3–4 working-day cycles with 5–15 issue appetites are already tight planning units\n- Quarterly OKRs would add a layer of bookkeeping for marginal value\n- Upgrade path exists: if portfolio grows beyond solo, add a quarterly layer on top without re-shaping initiatives\n\n### 6.2 Committed vs aspirational labelling per KR\n\nRather than tagging the whole OKR, label individual KRs:\n- **Committed** KRs must hit 1.0 (operability, no-silent-failure, pre-registration, baseline tracking)\n- **Aspirational** KRs land at 0.6–0.7 (outcome KRs, behaviour change, forecast calibration)\n\nA mixed OKR is normal: 1–2 committed + 1–2 aspirational is the common shape.\n\n### 6.3 Appetite is scope cap, not stretch signal\n\nThe user's existing \"appetite\" answers \"how much will we spend,\" not \"how hard are we trying.\" Keep appetite as is; layer commit/aspirational on individual KRs.\n\n### 6.4 KR count default: 3\n\nWodtke's 3-KR default fits small-appetite work better than Lamorte's 3–5 range. For 5–8 issue (\"small\") initiatives, 2 KRs may be appropriate — but only if the second KR is genuinely orthogonal to the first (eg one correctness + one operability) rather than a softer restatement of the first.\n\n### 6.5 Recommended extended four-field template\n\nThe four field structure stays compatible with `linear-workflow.md`. The discipline goes into how the four fields are filled:\n\n```\nGoal:           For [who], we want to [solve problem / achieve outcome].\n\nKey results:    1. [committed|aspirational] [observable state]\n                   baseline: [current value/state]\n                   target:   [target value/state]\n                   window:   [time frame]\n                   source:   [where the evidence will live]\n                2.
