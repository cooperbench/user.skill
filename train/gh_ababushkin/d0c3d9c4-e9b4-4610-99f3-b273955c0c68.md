what is GARP?

> AGENT

**GARP = Growth At a Reasonable Price.** It's a stock-picking style — a middle ground between pure value (cheap) and pure growth (fast-growing). The idea: buy companies that are growing meaningfully but aren't priced so richly that you're overpaying for the growth. Popularised by Peter Lynch at Fidelity in the 80s. The signature metric is **PEG = P/E ÷ earnings growth rate**: - PEG ≤ 1 → cheap relative to growth (Lynch's classic "buy" zone) - PEG 1–2 → reasonable - PEG > 2 → you're paying a premium for the growth - PEG > 3 → expensive on a GARP lens In this pack, `/stock-signal` applies a GARP screen using PEG and P/S (Price-to-Sales) thresholds. That's where the `model_ready=NO` verdict comes from on AMZN (PEG 3.82), NFLX (PEG 4.74), etc. — they fail the "reasonable price" half of GARP. The catch — and the reason this is the bug you've hit — is that **PEG breaks down for mature mega-caps**. A $2T incumbent like AMZN or GOOG can't grow earnings 20%/yr from that base; their growth rate is structurally 8–12%, so almost any normal P/E gives them PEG 3+. PEG also breaks for high-quality compounders the market deliberately pays a premium for (ASML's monopoly, NFLX's pricing power) — the market is correctly pricing durability that PEG can't see. So a strict PEG gate systematically rejects exactly the kind of names COVERAGE.md was built to cover.
