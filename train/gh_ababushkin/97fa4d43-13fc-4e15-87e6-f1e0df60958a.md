1	# imagedoc
2	
3	Local, offline command-line tools for preparing identity-document images:
4	
5	- **`convert.py`** — batch-converts JPG images to PDFs.
6	- **`check.py`** — checks a photo against Australian passport and visa photo specs, and can auto-fix framing.
7	
8	Everything runs on your machine; no image is ever uploaded. The Australian Passport Office warns against online photo services for exactly this reason — sending your face to a third party is an identity-fraud risk. `check.py` is **advisory**: it catches the common, mechanically-checkable failures before you print or upload, but the Passport Office and Home Affairs make the final call.
9	
10	## Document requirements
11	
12	`check.py` validates against two profiles. Pick one with `--profile`, or check both at once (the default). The figures below are verified against the official Australian government guidance (last checked May 2026):
13	
14	- **Passport** — [Australian Passport Office, *Passport photo guidelines*](https://www.passports.gov.au/passport-photo-guidelines)
15	- **Visa / citizenship** — [Department of Home Affairs, *Photo requirements for citizenship applications*](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/citizenship/photo-requirements-for-citizenship-applications), the same photo standard Home Affairs applies to ImmiAccount uploads
16	
17	Both standards describe the *same physical photo*; they differ only in how it is submitted — a glossy print for passports, a digital file for online lodgement.
18	
19	| Requirement | Official rule | Profile |
20	|---|---|---|
21	| Photo size | 35–40 mm wide × 45–50 mm high (aspect ratio 0.70–0.89) | both |
22	| Face height (chin to crown) | 32–36 mm | both |
23	| Background | plain white or light (L* ≥ 75, i.e. no darker than light grey), contrasting with the face | both |
24	| Recency, expression, etc. | < 6 months old, colour, neutral expression, no glasses (medical exception aside), no retouching, even lighting | both |
25	| File format | JPEG | `visa` |
26	| File size | 70 KB – 3.5 MB | `visa` |
27	| Resolution | preferred 1200 × 1600 px | `visa` |
28	
29	The printed-passport spec is stated only in millimetres and on paper terms (dye-sublimation, heavy-weight glossy, ≥ 200 gsm) — there is **no official DPI or minimum-pixel rule**. So `check.py` adds its own pixel floors to judge a digital file, and these are tool choices, not government numbers:
30	
31	- **`passport`** — accepts 413 × 531 px (35 × 45 mm at 300 DPI) as the minimum; warns below 827 × 1063 px (600 DPI) since that prints sharper on glossy paper.
32	- **`visa`** — fails below 354 × 472 px (the smallest 3:4 frame it trusts) and treats 1200 × 1600 px as the preferred size.
33	
34	One deliberate deviation from the letter of the spec: both profiles accept PNG as well as JPEG. Home Affairs asks for JPEG only; PNG is accepted but triggers a warning for the `visa` profile since the ImmiAccount portal may reject it.
35	
36	Beyond the table, both profiles check image quality and face geometry: in-focus (not blurry), correctly exposed, a uniform background, exactly one frontal face, the face horizontally centred, and both eyes visible.
37	
38	**What it does not check.** It does not verify the photo is recent, unedited, or a biometric match — those aren't derivable from pixels, and the government runs its own match. It never alters image content: a wrong background colour, blur, or bad exposure is reported as a FAIL with advice to re-shoot, never silently "fixed."
39	
40	> **Note on head height.** The head-height check measures the detected face box (brow to chin), which reads lower than the full chin-to-crown head height the specs define. A raw, uncropped portrait is expected to fall short here; the band is the target *after* cropping.
41	
42	## Usage
43	
44	### Converting JPGs to PDFs
45	
46	1. Place your `.jpg` / `.jpeg` images into the `input/` folder.
47	2. Run the script:
48	   ```
49	   python3 convert.py
50	   ```
51	3. Find the converted PDFs in the `output/` folder.
52	
53	### Checking a photo
54	
55	```
56	python3 check.py <image> [--profile passport|visa|both] [--fix] [-o out.jpg]
57	```
58	
59	- `--profile` — which spec to check against; defaults to `both`.
60	- `--fix` (or `-o`) — crop, scale, and re-encode the photo so its framing, dimensions, and file size meet the chosen profile, then re-check the result. Fixing is geometric only — it never retouches the image or changes the background.
61	- `-o` — where to write the fixed image (implies `--fix`).
62	
63	The command prints a per-rule PASS/FAIL report and exits non-zero if any check fails, so it can gate a script.
64	
65	## Installation
66	
67	The toolchain is pinned for reproducibility:
68	
69	- **Python version** — `.mise.toml` pins 3.14.4, managed by [mise](https://mise.jdx.dev).
70	- **Dependencies** — `requirements.txt` pins exact versions: Pillow (PDF conversion), plus NumPy and OpenCV (the photo checker).
71	
72	One-time setup:
73	
74	```
75	mise install                              # installs the pinned Python (3.14.4)
76	python -m venv .venv
77	.venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
78	```
79	
80	After that, run the scripts directly — they add `.venv` to the path themselves, so no manual activation is needed.
81	
82	Not using mise? Install Python 3.14.x yourself, then run the `venv` and `pip install` steps above.
83
