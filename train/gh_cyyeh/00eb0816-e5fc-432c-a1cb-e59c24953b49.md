This is a real cross-model prompt engineering challenge. Here's what's happening and how to fix it:

## Why GPT-5.2 Fails Where Sonnet Succeeds

Claude models are strong at following complex procedural instructions in natural language. GPT models tend to need **more explicit structure, redundancy, and concrete examples** to reliably follow multi-step behavioral constraints like interleaving.

Your current charting workflow instructions are clear enough for Claude but rely on the model inferring the correct behavior from numbered steps alone. GPT models often "understand" the rule (you see it in thinking) but still default to the more natural pattern of batching tool calls together.

## Practical Strategies

Here are the most effective techniques, ranked by impact:

### 1. Add a concrete example (highest impact)

Few-shot examples are the most reliable cross-model technique. Models that struggle with abstract rules will still follow a demonstrated pattern.

### 2. Make the anti-pattern explicitly visible

Instead of just "Do NOT render all charts first", show the wrong pattern so the model can pattern-match against it.

### 3. Use structured sections (XML tags)

Both Claude and GPT parse XML-tagged sections well. They create stronger boundaries than plain-text headers.

### 4. Repeat the critical constraint at multiple touchpoints

GPT models benefit from encountering the same rule in different contexts within the prompt.
