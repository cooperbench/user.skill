**Claim:** "TSFMs are designed to be used with a frozen backbone and a small task head: the backbone is shared and the head specializes per task."

**Evidence — strongly supported for MOMENT, more nuanced for Moirai/Chronos:**

1. **MOMENT (ICML 2024)** — DIRECT support. The official source code defines `PretrainHead`, `ClassificationHead`, and `ForecastingHead` as separate modules attached to the shared T5 encoder backbone. The forecasting tutorial states "we will explore how to use MOMENT to tackle the time series forecasting in **linearly probed setting**." MOMENT loads in 4 modes (`reconstruction`/`embedding`/`forecasting`/`classification`), each with its own head atop the same backbone.

2. **Third-party corroboration** — multiple independent papers state this is *the* standard adaptation protocol:
   - TIC-FM (arXiv:2602.00620): "The zero-shot evaluation of time series foundation models (TSFMs) for classification **typically uses a frozen encoder followed by a task-specific classifier**."
   - TS-Memory (arXiv:2602.11550): "lightweight memory adapter that augments **frozen TSFMs**."
   - AdaPTS (arXiv:2502.10235): explicitly diagrams the FM as frozen (snowflake) + trainable adapter (flame).
   - FMTK (arXiv:2512.01038): "TSFMs ... serve as fixed backbones ... task specialization often requires integrating ... task-specific decoders ... and ... adapters."

3. **Caveat — Chronos**: The default Chronos usage pattern is **zero-shot**, and when fine-tuned the default mode is **LoRA**, not a frozen-backbone-plus-new-head adapter. Chronos-2 paper explicitly emphasizes "**inference-only forecasting systems that produce accurate predictions without task-specific training**." So saying TSFMs are "designed" with a frozen-backbone + small task head overstates Chronos's design intent.

4. **Caveat — Moirai**: Pretrained encoder + output head that maps to mixture-distribution parameters. Standard usage is zero-shot or full fine-tuning, not specifically the frozen-encoder + adapter protocol.

**Recommendation for the abstract.** The strongest defensible phrasing is to say this is a *standard adaptation pattern* (well-documented in independent surveys) rather than a *design intent of all TSFMs*. Suggested rewrite:

> Time series foundation models (TSFMs) such as MOMENT ship with a shared frozen backbone and a small task-specific head, and the dominant downstream adaptation protocol — across MOMENT, Moirai, Chronos, and TimesFM — keeps the backbone frozen and trains only a lightweight adapter or head per task.

This is verifiable from the cited literature (TIC-FM, TS-Memory, AdaPTS, FMTK), avoids overclaiming for Chronos, and still establishes that any pathology in the head is a **built-in concern of the standard usage pattern**, which is what your motivation needs.

## Verification — synthesis after broader sweep

The claim "TSFMs are designed to be used with a frozen backbone and a small task head" is partially true but **gets sharper when split by architecture family**. Five new findings change the picture:

**1. The TSFM landscape splits into two design families** (TSFM.ai taxonomy + Chronos paper + TimesFM paper + Lag-Llama paper):

- **Encoder-only / encoder-decoder TSFMs** (MOMENT, Moirai 1.1-R, Chronos, Chronos-2): explicitly designed with a shared encoder backbone "**and project the final representations through a task-specific head (forecasting, anomaly detection, classification, imputation)**" (direct quote, TSFM.ai). MOMENT ships with `PretrainHead` / `ClassificationHead` / `ForecastingHead` modules in `momentfm/models/moment.py`. Your claim is well-supported here.
- **Decoder-only TSFMs** (TimesFM, Lag-Llama): designed for autoregressive generation, default usage is **zero-shot**, no separate task head per se. TimesFM 2.5 added LoRA-based fine-tuning in Apr 2026 but does *not* match the "frozen + small head" pattern. Your claim is **inaccurate** for this family.

**2. "Small" is a reviewer trap** (TRACE paper, arXiv:2503.16991, direct quote):
> "linear probing—freezing the backbone and tuning only the forecasting head—is efficient but suboptimal: it fails to exploit the rich temporal representations encoded in intermediate layers. This limitation is especially severe in long-horizon forecasting, where **the forecasting head itself can contain millions of parameters (e.g., > 35M for a 720-step horizon in MOMENT)**."

So the MOMENT forecasting head is *not* "small" in the engineering sense. A reviewer who reads this can immediately challenge "small task head." Drop "small" or use "task-specific" / "lightweight relative to the backbone."

**3. Frozen-backbone-plus-head is the *adaptation* protocol, not the only design intent.** Independent confirmation from multiple 2024–2026 papers:
- TIC-FM (arXiv:2602.00620): "typically uses a frozen encoder followed by a task-specific classifier."
- AdapTS (Lee et al. 2025, arXiv:2502.12920): "Foundation models (FMs) typically remain fixed during deployment."
- Beyond LoRA (Gupta et al. 2024, arXiv:2409.11302): the standard PEFT story for Chronos.
- TS-Memory (arXiv:2602.11550), AdaPTS (arXiv:2502.10235), FMTK (arXiv:2512.01038), MixFT (arXiv:2603.02840): all confirm frozen-backbone + adapter as a standard adaptation pattern.

**4. Chronos's primary mode is *zero-shot* with the same generation head**, not a swapped task head. Chronos is fine-tuned via **LoRA**, not by training a new head on a frozen backbone (Chronos-2 paper, AutoGluon docs). Even the original Chronos paper frames the contribution as making forecasting "as is" without per-task training.
