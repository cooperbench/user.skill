1108:While the MI decomposition in Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} is algebraically a chain-rule identity, it has deeper information-theoretic content when connected to rate-distortion theory and phase transitions.
1111:Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. In rate-distortion theory~\citep{cover2006elements}, this compression has rate $R_\text{strip} = I(X; M, \Sigma \mid S)$ and distortion (for routing) $D_\text{route} = H(E \mid S) - H(E \mid X) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S)$, which is exactly Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i). Under Gaussianity, the signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D})$ is proportional to the rate-distortion slope at the normalization operating point: it measures how much routing capacity is consumed by the statistics that normalization strips. Specifically, if the joint $(M, \Sigma, S)$ is multivariate Gaussian, then $I(M, \Sigma; E) \propto \log(1 + R(\mathcal{D}))$ (by the Gaussian mutual information formula), making $R(\mathcal{D})$ a \emph{monotone surrogate for the bits lost to normalization}. The validated $\rho = -0.88$ correlation (Figure~\ref{fig:signal_ratio}) is thus not a statistical coincidence but a consequence of this rate-distortion structure.

551	\textbf{Why RevIN destroys routing: formal analysis.} The experiments above converge on a single mechanism: instance normalization strips the per-window statistics that the router uses to tell input regimes apart. To pin this down, we ask what information the router actually has access to before and after normalization. Each input window $\mathbf{x}_i$ decomposes into a mean $M_i = \mu(\mathbf{x}_i)$, a scale $\Sigma_i = \sigma(\mathbf{x}_i)$, and a shape $\mathbf{S}_i = (\mathbf{x}_i - M_i)/\Sigma_i$. RevIN maps $\mathbf{x}_i \mapsto \mathbf{S}_i$ and discards $(M_i, \Sigma_i)$, so the question reduces to how much routing information lives in the discarded part. The mutual-information chain rule gives a clean answer.

553	\begin{observation}[Routing Information Loss Under Instance Normalization]
554	\label{thm:mi_decomp}
555	Let $X$ be a random input window, $E \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ the expert assignment produced by any deterministic router, and $S = (X - M)/\Sigma$ the RevIN-normalized shape.
556	\begin{enumerate}
557	    \item[\textup{(i)}] \textbf{Exact decomposition.} $I(X; E) - I(S; E) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S) \geq 0$.
558	    \item[\textup{(ii)}] \textbf{Quantitative bound.} Without any independence assumption,
559	    \[
560	        I(X; E) - I(S; E) \;\geq\; I(M, \Sigma;\, E) \;-\; I(M, \Sigma;\, S),
561	    \]
562	    where $\varepsilon := I(M, \Sigma;\, S) \geq 0$ captures any residual ``leakage'' of the stripped statistics through the shape. Equality holds iff $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S$ given $E$.
563	\end{enumerate}
564	\end{observation}

We therefore use the \emph{signal ratio} $R(\mathcal{D}) = [\mathrm{Var}(M) + \mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)] / \overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)}$ as a tractable monotone proxy for the variance contribution of the stripped statistics. 1066	\textbf{Proof of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}} (Routing Information Loss Under RevIN, revised form).
1067	
1068	\begin{proof}
1069	Throughout, $X$ denotes a raw window, $(M,\Sigma)$ its per-window mean/scale, $S = (X-M)/\Sigma$ its RevIN-normalized shape, and $E$ a deterministic (possibly randomized) router assignment. The map $(X) \mapsto (S, M, \Sigma)$ is invertible, so $I(X; Y) = I(S, M, \Sigma; Y)$ for any $Y$, and in particular $I(X; E) = I(S, M, \Sigma; E)$.
1070	
1071	\emph{Part (i): Exact decomposition.} By the chain rule of mutual information applied to $(S, M, \Sigma; E)$,
1072	\begin{align*}
1073	I(X; E) \;=\; I(S, M, \Sigma;\, E) \;=\; I(S;\, E) + I(M, \Sigma;\, E \mid S).
1074	\end{align*}
1075	Rearranging, $I(X; E) - I(S; E) = I(M, \Sigma;\, E \mid S) \geq 0$. This identity holds \emph{without any independence assumption} between $(M,\Sigma)$ and $S$.
1076	
1077	\emph{Part (ii): Unconditional quantitative bound.} We bound the conditional mutual information $I(M,\Sigma; E \mid S)$ from below in terms of the \emph{unconditional} quantities $I(M,\Sigma; E)$ and $I(M,\Sigma; S)$. Applying the chain rule two different ways to $I(M,\Sigma; E, S)$,
1078	\begin{align*}
1079	I(M,\Sigma;\, E, S) &= I(M,\Sigma;\, S) + I(M,\Sigma;\, E \mid S) \qquad\text{(expand $S$ first)} \\
1080	                    &= I(M,\Sigma;\, E) + I(M,\Sigma;\, S \mid E) \qquad\text{(expand $E$ first).}
1081	\end{align*}
1082	Equating and solving for $I(M,\Sigma; E \mid S)$,
1083	\[
1084	I(M,\Sigma;\, E \mid S) \;=\; I(M,\Sigma;\, E) \;-\; I(M,\Sigma;\, S) \;+\; I(M,\Sigma;\, S \mid E).
1085	\]
1086	Since $I(M,\Sigma; S \mid E) \geq 0$ (mutual information is non-negative), we obtain
1087	\[
1088	I(X; E) - I(S; E) \;=\; I(M,\Sigma;\, E \mid S) \;\geq\; I(M,\Sigma;\, E) \;-\; I(M,\Sigma;\, S),
1089	\]
1090	where the residual $\varepsilon := I(M,\Sigma; S) \geq 0$ is the \emph{leakage} of location-scale statistics into the shape. Equality (tightness) holds iff $I(M,\Sigma; S \mid E) = 0$, i.e.\ iff $(M,\Sigma)$ and $S$ are conditionally independent given $E$. In particular, unconditional independence $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S$ implies $\varepsilon = 0$ and recovers the classical $I(X;E) - I(S;E) \geq I(M,\Sigma; E)$ bound as a corollary.
1091	
1092	\emph{Part (iii): Full-information corner.} Suppose the router depends entirely on the stripped statistics: $E \perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid (M, \Sigma)$, which means $p(E \mid S, M, \Sigma) = p(E \mid M, \Sigma)$. Then the Markov chain $S \to (M,\Sigma) \to E$ holds, and by the DPI $I(S; E) \leq I(M,\Sigma; E)$ but also $I(S; E) = 0$ whenever the Markov chain's first step $S \to (M,\Sigma)$ conveys no information (which holds when $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S$). In the full-information corner case $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S$ and $E$ determined by $(M,\Sigma)$, we have $I(S;E) = 0$ and $I(X;E) = I(M,\Sigma;E) = H(E) - H(E \mid M,\Sigma) = H(E)$. Hence $I(X;E) - I(S;E) = H(E)$: RevIN destroys the entire routing distribution.
1093	
1094	\emph{Part (iv): Empirical leakage and signal ratio.} We measure the leakage $\varepsilon = I(M,\Sigma; S)$ via two independent methods.
1095	
1096	\emph{Gaussian reference.} Since $(M,\Sigma)$ is 2-dimensional, there are at most 2 canonical correlations $\rho_1, \rho_2$ between $(M,\Sigma)$ and the shape $S$. For jointly Gaussian variables, $I(M,\Sigma; S) = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^2 \log(1 - \rho_i^2)$. This yields $\varepsilon_\text{Gauss}$ ranging from $0.09$ nats (Weather, $\rho_\text{max}{=}0.35$) to $1.26$ nats (Exchange, $\rho_\text{max}{=}0.89$). The leakage is \emph{not} uniformly small: datasets with strong trend-seasonality coupling (Exchange, ETTh2) have substantial shape-level encoding of location-scale statistics.
1097	
1098	\emph{Distribution-free cross-check.} Because canonical correlations capture only linear dependence, we cross-validate with the KSG $k$-nearest-neighbor MI estimator~\citep{kraskov2004ksg}, which requires no distributional assumptions. We project $S$ onto its 2 canonical variates with respect to $(M,\Sigma)$ and estimate the $2$-vs-$2$ MI with $k{=}5$ neighbors on $N{\geq}888$ samples per dataset. The KSG estimates agree with the Gaussian reference within $\pm 0.26$ nats across all 9 datasets, confirming that nonlinear dependence does not substantially inflate the leakage beyond the CCA-captured linear component.
1099	
1100	\emph{Tightness condition.} The bound~(ii) is informative when $I(M,\Sigma; E) \gg \varepsilon$. Since $I(M,\Sigma; E)$ increases with the location-scale variance, the signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D}) = [\mathrm{Var}(M) + \mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)] / \overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)}$ is a monotone proxy for the tightness of the bound. This explains the $\rho{=}{-}0.88$ correlation: high-$R$ datasets (Exchange, $R{=}2.17$) have $I(M,\Sigma; E) \gg \varepsilon$, producing large gains ($-68.5\%$); low-$R$ datasets (Traffic, $R{=}0.14$) have $I(M,\Sigma; E) \approx \varepsilon$, producing no gain ($+2.9\%$).
1101	\end{proof}
1102	
1103	\textbf{Remark on the classical form.} The earlier version assumed $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S$ outright. The revised form~(ii) keeps $\varepsilon$ explicit and Part~(iv) measures it via both parametric (CCA) and non-parametric (KSG) methods, eliminating the independence assumption. Part~(v) bypasses the bound entirely by directly measuring $H(E \mid S)$, confirming the gap $I(M,\Sigma; S \mid E)$ averages $0.075$ nats (Table~\ref{tab:mi_tightness}). The agreement between Gaussian reference and distribution-free KSG estimates ($\pm 0.26$ nats) confirms the CCA-based values are reliable despite non-Gaussianity.

1105	\subsection{Theoretical Depth of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}}
1106	\label{app:prop2_depth}
1107	
1108	While the MI decomposition in Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} is algebraically a chain-rule identity, it has deeper information-theoretic content when connected to rate-distortion theory and phase transitions.
1109	
1110	\textbf{Rate-distortion interpretation of $R(\mathcal{D})$.}
1111	Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. In rate-distortion theory~\citep{cover2006elements}, this compression has rate $R_\text{strip} = I(X; M, \Sigma \mid S)$ and distortion (for routing) $D_\text{route} = H(E \mid S) - H(E \mid X) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S)$, which is exactly Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i). Under Gaussianity, the signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D})$ is proportional to the rate-distortion slope at the normalization operating point: it measures how much routing capacity is consumed by the statistics that normalization strips. Specifically, if the joint $(M, \Sigma, S)$ is multivariate Gaussian, then $I(M, \Sigma; E) \propto \log(1 + R(\mathcal{D}))$ (by the Gaussian mutual information formula), making $R(\mathcal{D})$ a \emph{monotone surrogate for the bits lost to normalization}. The validated $\rho = -0.88$ correlation (Figure~\ref{fig:signal_ratio}) is thus not a statistical coincidence but a consequence of this rate-distortion structure.
1112	
1113	\begin{proposition}[Phase Transition in Routing Quality]
1114	\label{prop:phase_transition}
1115	Consider a softmax router on the interpolated input $\mathbf{x}_\alpha = (1{-}\alpha)\,\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw} + \alpha\,\mathrm{RevIN}(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw})$. Under the Gaussian model with approximately orthogonal $(M,\Sigma)$ and $S$ (leakage $\varepsilon$ small), the effective routing signal power is:
1116	\[
1117	    P_\mathrm{route}(\alpha) \;=\; (1{-}\alpha)^2 \cdot [\mathrm{Var}(M) + \mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)] \;+\; \alpha^2 \cdot \overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)} \;+\; 2\alpha(1{-}\alpha)\,\mathrm{Cov}_\mathrm{cross}
1118	\]
1119	Routing discriminability crosses the router's noise floor at a critical normalization threshold:
1120	\[
1121	    \alpha^* \;\approx\; \frac{R(\mathcal{D})}{R(\mathcal{D}) + 1}
1122	\]
1123	For $\alpha < \alpha^*$, routing quality degrades continuously; for $\alpha \to 1$, routing collapses discontinuously due to the softmax's exponential sensitivity to signal-to-noise ratio.
1124	\end{proposition}
1125	
1126	\emph{Validation.} For the median $R(\mathcal{D}) = 0.67$ across our benchmarks, Proposition~\ref{prop:phase_transition} predicts $\alpha^* \approx 0.40$. The dose-response experiment (Figure~\ref{fig:dose_response}; 150 runs) confirms this: MSE degrades by $+5.4\%$ for $\alpha \leq 0.75$ but jumps to $+283\%$ at $\alpha = 1.0$. The sharpness of the transition arises from the softmax: when $P_\mathrm{route}(\alpha) / P_\mathrm{noise}$ drops below ${\sim}1$, routing weights converge to uniform regardless of expert relevance.

1165	\textbf{Information bottleneck connection.}
1166	The RevIN-encoder pipeline implements a form of the information bottleneck~\citep{tishby2000ib}: it compresses $X$ into a representation $H = f_\theta(S)$ that is optimized for downstream prediction while discarding ``irrelevant'' variability (the per-window location-scale statistics). The routing MI loss from Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i) is the price paid for this compression \emph{at the routing decision point}. This connects to a broader principle: any architecture that bottlenecks information for one objective (prediction) can inadvertently destroy information needed for another (routing). The vision cross-modality control (Appendix~\ref{app:vision_moe}) confirms this is not RevIN-specific: InstanceNorm1d on ViT patches creates the same bottleneck, while InstanceNorm2d on ResNet spatial features does not, because spatial features are not the routing signal for classification.

1168	\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}} (Gradient Co-Adaptation Drives Entropy Collapse).
1169	
1170	\begin{proof}
1171	Consider a single input $\mathbf{x}$ with backbone $\mathbf{h} = A\mathbf{x}$, router $p = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{h})$, and loss $\ell = p\,\ell_1(\mathbf{h}) + (1-p)\,\ell_2(\mathbf{h})$.
1172	
1173	\emph{Part (i): Unfrozen backbone.} The gradient with respect to $A$ is:
1174	\begin{equation*}
1175	\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial A} = p\,\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\mathbf{x}^\top + (1{-}p)\,\frac{\partial\ell_2}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\mathbf{x}^\top + (\ell_1 - \ell_2)\,p(1{-}p)\,\mathbf{w}\mathbf{x}^\top
1176	\end{equation*}
1177	Under gradient descent $\dot{A} = -\eta\,\partial\ell/\partial A$, the induced change in expert~1's loss is:
1178	\begin{multline*}
1179	\dot{\ell}_1\big|_A = -\eta\|\mathbf{x}\|^2\!\Bigg[p\left\|\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right\|^2 + (1{-}p)\left(\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right)^{\!\top}\frac{\partial\ell_2}{\partial\mathbf{h}} \\
1180	 + (\ell_1{-}\ell_2)\,p(1{-}p)\left(\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right)^{\!\top}\mathbf{w}\Bigg]
1181	\end{multline*}
1182	The first term (self-gradient) always decreases $\ell_1$. The second (cross-gradient) is negligible when expert gradients are approximately orthogonal, a condition that holds empirically once experts specialize to distinct input clusters. The third (loss-coupling) term is small early in training when $|\ell_1 - \ell_2| \ll \|\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h}\|$. Retaining only the dominant first term:
1183	\begin{equation*}
1184	\dot{\ell}_1\big|_A \approx -\eta\,p\left\|\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right\|^2\|\mathbf{x}\|^2
1185	\end{equation*}
1186	and analogously $\dot{\ell}_2\big|_A \approx -\eta\,(1{-}p)\left\|\frac{\partial\ell_2}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right\|^2\|\mathbf{x}\|^2$. When $p > 1{-}p$ (expert~1 dominates), $\ell_1$ decreases faster than $\ell_2$. The third term in $\partial\ell/\partial A$ further reinforces this: when $\ell_1 < \ell_2$, the factor $(\ell_1 - \ell_2) < 0$ drives $\dot{p} > 0$, pushing $p$ toward~1. Together these create the self-reinforcing loop: dominant expert gets more gradient $\to$ its loss drops faster $\to$ router increases its weight $\to$ it becomes more dominant.
1187	
1188	\emph{Part (ii): Frozen backbone.} When $A$ is fixed, $\dot{A} = 0$. The only dynamics are through $\mathbf{w}$:
1189	\begin{equation*}
1190	\dot{\mathbf{w}} = -\eta\,\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\mathbf{w}} = -\eta\,(\ell_1 - \ell_2)\,p(1{-}p)\,\mathbf{h}
1191	\end{equation*}
1192	This update adjusts routing weights based on current expert performance, but does \emph{not} reshape $\mathbf{h}$ to favor one expert. Without the $A$-mediated feedback loop, there is no systematic bias toward entropy collapse: the router converges to a stable allocation reflecting the static geometry of the fixed representation space.
1193	\end{proof}

427	{\looseness=-1 \textbf{The Frozen Paradox.} Frozen RR-MoA beats \emph{full fine-tuning} (all 8 blocks unfrozen, best of 5 heads $\times$ 2 LRs) by $12$--$79\%$ on every dataset (Table~\ref{tab:baselines}, Figure~\ref{fig:frozen_paradox}). First, \emph{within MoE adapters}, unfreezing triggers gradient co-adaptation (Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}): the dominant expert reshapes the shared backbone, collapsing routing, and freezing prevents this loop and preserves expert diversity. 1110:\textbf{Rate-distortion interpretation of $R(\mathcal{D})$.}
1111:Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. 1111:Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. Specifically, if the joint $(M, \Sigma, S)$ is multivariate Gaussian, then $I(M, \Sigma; E) \propto \log(1 + R(\mathcal{D}))$ (by the Gaussian mutual information formula), making $R(\mathcal{D})$ a \emph{monotone surrogate for the bits lost to normalization}. 367	\subsection{Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters (RR-MoA)}
368	\label{sec:rrmoa}
369	
370	An \emph{adapter} $g_\phi: \mathbb{R}^{B \times T \times d} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^{B \times H}$ maps a pretrained backbone's hidden states to forecasts, with $n_g \leq 500{,}000$ trainable parameters. The goal of per-window routing is to select, for each input window, the subset of expert adapters best suited to that window's characteristics, so that a quiet baseline and a volatile spike are handled by different adapter topologies rather than forced through the same head. As shown in the introduction, this fails when the router reads normalized hidden states, because RevIN removes the per-window statistics that distinguish input regimes. \textbf{RR-MoA} avoids this by splitting the information into two streams: the frozen TSFM produces deep embeddings $\mathbf{H}$ for the experts, while a separate lightweight gate reads the \textit{raw, pre-normalization} input $\mathbf{X}_\text{raw}$ to decide which experts to activate:

## 1. Observation~1 (MI decomposition) — sound, two minor cleanups

### Part (i): exact decomposition $I(X;E) - I(S;E) = I(M,\Sigma; E\mid S)$

**Correct.** The argument relies on the bijection $X \leftrightarrow (S, M, \Sigma)$ and the chain rule. The bijection requires $\Sigma > 0$ (a degenerate window with constant value gives $\Sigma=0$ and $S$ undefined); this should be noted as a measure-zero event excluded by convention. Otherwise tight.

### Part (ii): bound $I(X;E) - I(S;E) \geq I(M,\Sigma; E) - \varepsilon$

**Correct.** The two-way chain-rule expansion of $I(M,\Sigma; E, S)$ is a standard derivation. The "equality iff $(M,\Sigma)\perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid E$" qualifier matches the proof.

### Part (iii): full-information corner — redundant step

**Issue.** The proof says
> "by the DPI $I(S; E) \leq I(M,\Sigma; E)$ but also $I(S; E) = 0$ whenever the Markov chain's first step $S \to (M,\Sigma)$ conveys no information"

The first DPI bound is correct but never used; the actual deduction uses DPI on the *same* chain $S \to (M,\Sigma) \to E$ to get $I(S;E) \leq I(S;(M,\Sigma)) = 0$. Rewrite as a single DPI step. Also, the directional Markov-chain phrasing is asymmetric whereas $E\perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid (M,\Sigma)$ is symmetric — clarify by stating the joint factorization $p(E,S \mid M,\Sigma) = p(E\mid M,\Sigma)\,p(S\mid M,\Sigma)$ and applying DPI to whichever direction is convenient.

### Part (iv): CCA / KSG estimation of leakage $\varepsilon$

**Caveat that should be acknowledged.** The KSG cross-check projects $S$ onto its **two canonical variates with respect to $(M,\Sigma)$**, then estimates a 2-vs-2 MI. By the data-processing inequality, $I(\text{proj}(S);(M,\Sigma)) \leq I(S;(M,\Sigma))$. The CCA projection preserves all *linear* dependence but discards purely *nonlinear* dependence between $(M,\Sigma)$ and $S$. So KSG agreeing with the Gaussian formula within $\pm 0.26$ nats does not bound nonlinear leakage in directions orthogonal to the canonical-variate subspace — it bounds it within that subspace. The honest statement: "KSG confirms that linear-leakage is well-estimated by the Gaussian formula"; you cannot upgrade this to "nonlinear dependence is small" without an unrestricted KSG run on the full $S$ vector (computationally hard at $\dim S{=}512$, but bounding by random projections or partial KSG would be cleaner).

### "Part (v)" reference that doesn't exist

The remark at line 1103 says

But the proof has only Parts (i)-(iv). The "Part (v)" content lives in App.~`app:mi_tightness` (Table~\ref{tab:mi_tightness}). Either rename in the remark to "Appendix~\ref{app:mi_tightness}" or add a Part (v) that points at the empirical measurement. Right now this is a dangling cross-reference.

> "if the joint $(M, \Sigma, S)$ is multivariate Gaussian, then $I(M, \Sigma; E) \propto \log(1 + R(\mathcal{D}))$ (by the Gaussian mutual information formula)"

## 3. Proposition~2 (Phase Transition) — the formula $\alpha^* = R/(R+1)$ is asserted without proof

This is the weakest mathematical part of the paper. Three concrete gaps.

**Gap A: "Effective routing signal power" undefined.** The expression
$$P_\text{route}(\alpha) = (1-\alpha)^2[\text{Var}(M)+\text{Var}(\Sigma)] + \alpha^2 \overline{\text{Var}(S)} + 2\alpha(1-\alpha)\,\text{Cov}_\text{cross}$$

is presented as if it were derived, but the underlying generative model is not specified. To make this rigorous, you need: (i) a specific assumption like $X = M + \Sigma S$ with $M \perp\!\!\!\perp \Sigma \perp\!\!\!\perp S$ Gaussian; (ii) a specific notion of "routing signal" = the variance of the projection of $\mathbf{x}_\alpha$ onto a discriminative direction. As written, $P_\text{route}$ is a variance of $\mathbf{x}_\alpha$ but it's not clear which part of that variance is "routing signal" vs "noise."

**Gap B: $\alpha^*$ is not derived from $P_\text{route}$.** Setting $(1-\alpha)^2[\text{Var}(M)+\text{Var}(\Sigma)] = \alpha^2\overline{\text{Var}(S)}$ (squared comparison) gives $\alpha^* = \sqrt{R}/(\sqrt{R}+1)$, *not* $R/(R+1)$. Setting the *linear* (un-squared) variance contributions equal gives $\alpha^* = R/(R+1)$. The proof claims the latter without saying which comparison is being used or why. For $R = 0.67$ (the median), the two formulas predict $\alpha^* \approx 0.45$ vs $\alpha^* \approx 0.40$ — the empirical data is too coarse (5 grid points) to distinguish, so either formula is "validated" equally. Pick one and derive it.

**Gap C: "Router's noise floor" never defined.** The proposition says "Routing discriminability crosses the router's noise floor at $\alpha^*$." Without a definition of "noise floor," this is operational language, not a mathematical claim. A clean version: define the routing decision boundary's signal-to-noise ratio as the ratio of between-cluster expert-distinguishing variance to within-cluster variance, then derive when this ratio crosses 1.

**Gap D: The "phase transition" is at $\alpha = 1$, not $\alpha^*$.** The dose-response data (Figure~\ref{fig:dose_response}) shows mild degradation up to $\alpha = 0.75$ ($+5.4\%$) and a discontinuity at $\alpha = 1.0$ ($+283\%$). Proposition 2 says "for $\alpha < \alpha^*$ ... continuous; for $\alpha \to 1$ ... discontinuously." So $\alpha^* = 0.40$ is the *onset of mild degradation*, while the actual phase transition (sharp jump) is at $\alpha = 1$. The proposition conflates two thresholds. Recommend renaming: $\alpha^*$ is a "soft onset," and the discontinuity at $\alpha = 1$ is the actual phase transition (driven by the softmax exponential, not by SNR crossing).

## 4. Proposition~1 (Gradient Co-Adaptation) — informal; two specific gaps

### Gap E: dimensional inconsistency

The proof says
> "the loss-coupling term is small early in training when $|\ell_1 - \ell_2| \ll \|\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h}\|$"

These have different units: a loss difference vs a gradient norm. The actual condition for the third term (in the gradient $\partial\ell/\partial A$) to be small relative to the first is

$$|\ell_1 - \ell_2|\cdot|\langle\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h},\,\mathbf{w}\rangle|(1-p) \ll \|\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h}\|^2$$

which depends on the alignment of $\mathbf{w}$ with $\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h}$. Restate dimensionally.

### Gap F: frozen-backbone non-collapse not actually established

The proof of Part (ii) shows $\dot\mathbf{w} = -\eta(\ell_1 - \ell_2)\,p(1-p)\,\mathbf{h}$ when $A$ is frozen, then concludes "without the $A$-mediated feedback loop, there is no systematic bias toward entropy collapse." The actual reason RR-MoA doesn't collapse empirically is that experts cover *different* input regimes (so $(\ell_1 - \ell_2)$ flips sign across windows), and this requires diversity assumptions on the expert pool that the proposition doesn't state. As written, Part (ii) only shows that the *co-adaptation* mechanism is broken, not that collapse can't happen. 1105	\subsection{Theoretical Depth of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}}
1106	\label{app:prop2_depth}

1108	While the MI decomposition in Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} is algebraically a chain-rule identity, it has deeper information-theoretic content when connected to rate-distortion theory and phase transitions.

1110	\textbf{Rate-distortion interpretation of $R(\mathcal{D})$.}
1111	Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. In rate-distortion theory~\citep{cover2006elements}, this compression has rate $R_\text{strip} = I(X; M, \Sigma \mid S)$ and distortion (for routing) $D_\text{route} = H(E \mid S) - H(E \mid X) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S)$, which is exactly Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i). Under Gaussianity, the signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D})$ is proportional to the rate-distortion slope at the normalization operating point: it measures how much routing capacity is consumed by the statistics that normalization strips. Specifically, under a soft-routing channel approximation in which $(M,\Sigma)$ is jointly Gaussian and $E$ is generated by a noisy linear projection of $(M,\Sigma)$ (the Gaussian-equivalent of a softmax router with bounded curvature), the routing MI scales monotonically as $I(M,\Sigma;E) \asymp \log(1+R(\mathcal{D}))$. Note that $E$ is discrete, so this is a heuristic SNR scaling rather than the literal Gaussian-MI identity; the constant absorbs the routing-policy curvature. This makes $R(\mathcal{D})$ a \emph{monotone surrogate for the bits lost to normalization}, and the validated $\rho = -0.88$ correlation (Figure~\ref{fig:signal_ratio}) is empirical evidence \emph{for} this scaling rather than a direct corollary of the Gaussian-MI formula.

1113	\begin{proposition}[Heuristic SNR Onset and Softmax Discontinuity]
1114	\label{prop:phase_transition}
1115	Consider a linear-readout router on the interpolated input $\mathbf{x}_\alpha = (1{-}\alpha)\,\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw} + \alpha\,\mathrm{RevIN}(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw})$, under the generative model $X = M + \Sigma\,S$ with $M\perp\!\!\!\perp\Sigma\perp\!\!\!\perp S$ jointly Gaussian (so leakage $\varepsilon{\to}0$). Treating the location-scale component as routing \emph{signal} and the shape component as routing-irrelevant \emph{noise}, the per-component variance contributions to the router's input scale as $V_\mathrm{sig}(\alpha) = (1{-}\alpha)\,[\mathrm{Var}(M){+}\mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)]$ and $V_\mathrm{noise}(\alpha) = \alpha\,\overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)} + V_0$, where $V_0$ absorbs intrinsic router-readout noise. Two regimes emerge:
1116	\begin{enumerate}
1117	    \item[\textup{(a)}] \textbf{Heuristic SNR onset.} Setting $V_\mathrm{sig}(\alpha) = V_\mathrm{noise}(\alpha)$ in the limit $V_0 \to 0$ gives
1118	    \[
1119	        \alpha^* \;\approx\; \frac{R(\mathcal{D})}{R(\mathcal{D}) + 1}.
1120	    \]
1121	    For $\alpha < \alpha^*$, routing quality degrades continuously as the signal/noise ratio drops; this $\alpha^*$ marks the soft onset of degradation, not a discontinuity.
1122	    \item[\textup{(b)}] \textbf{Softmax discontinuity at $\alpha{=}1$.} At $\alpha{=}1$, $V_\mathrm{sig}(1){=}0$ and routing logits are a deterministic function of the shape $S$ alone. The softmax saturates: weights converge to a single distribution regardless of input, and entropy decouples from MSE. This discontinuity is a consequence of $V_\mathrm{sig}$ vanishing rather than of crossing $\alpha^*$.
1123	\end{enumerate}
1124	\end{proposition}
1125	
1126	\emph{Remark on the variance comparison.} Comparing the \emph{linear} variance contributions $V_\mathrm{sig}$ and $V_\mathrm{noise}$ (rather than the squared coefficients in $\|\mathbf{x}_\alpha\|^2$) corresponds to comparing each component's marginal contribution to the router's discriminative variance under the orthogonality assumption $M\perp\!\!\!\perp\Sigma\perp\!\!\!\perp S$. Comparing squared coefficients instead would yield $\alpha^* = \sqrt{R}/(\sqrt{R}+1)$; for the median $R{=}0.67$, the two formulas predict $\alpha^* \approx 0.40$ vs $0.45$, and the dose-response grid (5 points) is too coarse to discriminate. We label Proposition~\ref{prop:phase_transition} as a \emph{heuristic} prediction and report the formula consistent with the linear comparison.
1127	
1128	\emph{Validation.} For the median $R(\mathcal{D}) = 0.67$, Proposition~\ref{prop:phase_transition}(a) predicts a soft onset $\alpha^* \approx 0.40$. The dose-response experiment (Figure~\ref{fig:dose_response}; 150 runs) is consistent: MSE degrades smoothly by $+5.4\%$ for $\alpha \leq 0.75$ (covering the predicted onset region), and jumps to $+283\%$ at $\alpha = 1.0$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:phase_transition}(b), softmax saturation). The two thresholds are distinct: $\alpha^*$ marks where SNR-driven degradation \emph{begins}; the actual phase transition is at $\alpha = 1$, driven by $V_\mathrm{sig}$ vanishing rather than by SNR crossing.

1170	\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}} (Gradient Co-Adaptation Drives Entropy Collapse).
1171	
1172	\begin{proof}
1173	Consider a single input $\mathbf{x}$ with backbone $\mathbf{h} = A\mathbf{x}$, router $p = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{h})$, and loss $\ell = p\,\ell_1(\mathbf{h}) + (1-p)\,\ell_2(\mathbf{h})$.
1174	
1175	\emph{Part (i): Unfrozen backbone.} The gradient with respect to $A$ is:
1176	\begin{equation*}
1177	\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial A} = p\,\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\mathbf{x}^\top + (1{-}p)\,\frac{\partial\ell_2}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\mathbf{x}^\top + (\ell_1 - \ell_2)\,p(1{-}p)\,\mathbf{w}\mathbf{x}^\top
1178	\end{equation*}
1179	Under gradient descent $\dot{A} = -\eta\,\partial\ell/\partial A$, the induced change in expert~1's loss is:
1180	\begin{multline*}
1181	\dot{\ell}_1\big|_A = -\eta\|\mathbf{x}\|^2\!\Bigg[p\left\|\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right\|^2 + (1{-}p)\left(\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right)^{\!\top}\frac{\partial\ell_2}{\partial\mathbf{h}} \\
1182	 + (\ell_1{-}\ell_2)\,p(1{-}p)\left(\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right)^{\!\top}\mathbf{w}\Bigg]
1183	\end{multline*}
1184	The first term (self-gradient) always decreases $\ell_1$. The second (cross-gradient) is negligible when expert gradients are approximately orthogonal, a condition that holds empirically once experts specialize to distinct input clusters. The third (loss-coupling) term is small whenever its magnitude $|\ell_1 - \ell_2|\cdot|\langle \partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h},\mathbf{w}\rangle|(1{-}p)$ is dominated by the first term's $p\|\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h}\|^2$, i.e., when the dimensionless ratio
1185	$\rho_\mathrm{coup} := \frac{|\ell_1{-}\ell_2|\cdot|\langle \partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h},\,\mathbf{w}\rangle|\cdot(1{-}p)}{p\,\|\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h}\|^2}$
1186	is small. This holds early in training when expert losses are comparable ($|\ell_1{-}\ell_2|$ small relative to the gradient-norm scale set by $\|\partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h}\|^2 / |\langle \partial\ell_1/\partial\mathbf{h},\mathbf{w}\rangle|$) and when $\mathbf{w}$ is not strongly aligned with the expert gradient. Retaining only the dominant first term:
1187	\begin{equation*}
1188	\dot{\ell}_1\big|_A \approx -\eta\,p\left\|\frac{\partial\ell_1}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right\|^2\|\mathbf{x}\|^2
1189	\end{equation*}
1190	and analogously $\dot{\ell}_2\big|_A \approx -\eta\,(1{-}p)\left\|\frac{\partial\ell_2}{\partial\mathbf{h}}\right\|^2\|\mathbf{x}\|^2$. When $p > 1{-}p$ (expert~1 dominates), $\ell_1$ decreases faster than $\ell_2$. The third term in $\partial\ell/\partial A$ further reinforces this: when $\ell_1 < \ell_2$, the factor $(\ell_1 - \ell_2) < 0$ drives $\dot{p} > 0$, pushing $p$ toward~1. Together these create the self-reinforcing loop: dominant expert gets more gradient $\to$ its loss drops faster $\to$ router increases its weight $\to$ it becomes more dominant.
1191	
1192	\emph{Part (ii): Frozen backbone.} When $A$ is fixed, $\dot{A} = 0$. The only dynamics are through $\mathbf{w}$:
1193	\begin{equation*}
1194	\dot{\mathbf{w}} = -\eta\,\frac{\partial\ell}{\partial\mathbf{w}} = -\eta\,(\ell_1 - \ell_2)\,p(1{-}p)\,\mathbf{h}
1195	\end{equation*}
1196	This update adjusts routing weights based on current expert performance, but does \emph{not} reshape $\mathbf{h}$ to favor one expert: the $A$-mediated feedback loop that drove the runaway in Part~(i) is absent. Note that this argument removes the co-adaptation mechanism but does not by itself preclude collapse from $\mathbf{w}$-only dynamics: if expert~1 were uniformly better than expert~2 across all inputs, $\dot{\mathbf{w}}$ would still drive $p \to 1$. Empirical non-collapse therefore additionally requires that $(\ell_1 - \ell_2)$ flips sign across input regimes, i.e.\ that experts specialize to different windows. The RR-MoA pool is constructed with topologically distinct heads (mean/last/max/attention/Conv1d-pool) precisely to satisfy this; the resulting per-window expert specialization is empirically validated in Figure~\ref{fig:routing_viz} and Table~\ref{tab:rrmoa} (entropy stays in $[1.0, 1.57]$ across 54/54 configurations).
1197	\end{proof}

1199	\textbf{Corollary (Raw routing decouples the router from $A$).}
1200	\label{cor:raw_routing}
1201	\emph{If the router reads the raw input $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw}$ rather than the hidden state $\mathbf{h} = A\mathbf{x}$, then $\partial p / \partial A \equiv 0$, the loss-coupling term in $\partial\ell/\partial A$ vanishes identically, and the co-adaptation feedback loop of Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}(i) is broken \emph{by construction} regardless of whether $A$ is frozen.}
1202	
1203	\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary]
1204	With router input $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw}$ (independent of $A$), $p = \sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top G_\psi(\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw}))$ is a function of $\mathbf{x}_\mathrm{raw}$ and $(\mathbf{w}, \psi)$ only. Hence $\partial p / \partial A = 0$, and the third term $(\ell_1 - \ell_2)\,p(1{-}p)\,\mathbf{w}\mathbf{x}^\top$ in $\partial\ell/\partial A$ disappears: changes in $A$ no longer feed back into the routing decision through the gradient. The dominant-expert runaway in Part~(i) required this term; without it, even when $A$ is unfrozen, the router's update is decoupled from the backbone's update, eliminating the co-adaptation mechanism.
1205	\end{proof}
1206	
1207	This corollary identifies raw routing as the \emph{minimal causal fix}: it intervenes on the precise term in $\partial\ell/\partial A$ that drives the collapse loop, leaving all other components of the model untouched. The empirical validation is in Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory} (RR-MoA's gradient norms remain balanced across experts even when blocks are unfrozen) and Table~\ref{tab:rrmoa} (frozen-vs-unfrozen RR-MoA both maintain healthy entropy, in contrast to AdaMix where unfreezing collapses entropy to $0.000$).
