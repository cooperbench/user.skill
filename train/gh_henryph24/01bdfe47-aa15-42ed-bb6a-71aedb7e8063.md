## NeurIPS 2026 citation style: **author–year (natbib defaults)**, numbered-style is NOT the default.

**What the style file does** (`neurips_2026.sty:112–115`):
```latex
\if@natbib
  \RequirePackage{natbib}
\fi
```
It loads `natbib` with no options, which gives natbib's defaults: **`authoryear`** citation style with **round parentheses**. There is no `[numbers]` option applied, so IEEE-style numeric `[1]` citations are *not* the default.

|---|---|
| `\citep{vaswani2017attention}` | `(Vaswani et al., 2017)` |
| `\citet{vaswani2017attention}` | `Vaswani et al. (2017)` |
| `\citeauthor{...}` | `Vaswani et al.` |
| `\citeyear{...}` | `2017` |

\PassOptionsToPackage{numbers}{natbib}

### 1. The Optimization Cure: Router-Detached Gradient Flow (RDGF)
**Targets:** The "Frozen Paradox" (leaving backbone expressivity on the table).

*   **The Vulnerability:** The paper proves that unfreezing causes routing collapse due to gradient co-adaptation (Proposition 1), where the backbone reshapes itself to serve the marginally dominant expert. The authors "solve" this by strictly freezing the backbone. A harsh reviewer might view freezing as a workaround rather than a cure.
*   **The Algorithmic Tweak:** Mathematically cure the positive feedback loop in the backward pass. Implement a custom PyTorch autograd function (`register_hook` on the hidden states $H$). During backpropagation, **strip the router weighting $p_k$ from the gradient flowing back into the TSFM backbone**, replacing it with a uniform, unweighted average of the expert gradients:
    $$\nabla_H \mathcal{L}_{\text{backbone}} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{k=1}^K \nabla_H \mathcal{L}_k$$
*   **Why it elevates the paper:** This forces the backbone to learn a general-purpose representation that serves all experts equally, rather than co-adapting to the dominant one. If RDGF allows the backbone to be safely unfrozen *without* entropy dropping to 0.000, and it pushes the MSE past the frozen RR-MoA, the authors have engineered a true mathematical cure for MoE optimization dynamics.

### 2. The Systems Breakthrough: "Dynamic Bypass" Conditional Compute
**Targets:** Appendix Z.1 (The "Dead Weight TSFM" revelation) and Inference Efficiency.

*   **The Vulnerability:** Appendix Z.1 admits that on linear datasets (Electricity, Traffic), a pure ensemble of Raw MLPs matches the 35M-parameter TSFM. This invites the critique: *"Why run a massive foundation model on datasets where an MLP suffices?"*
*   **The Architectural Tweak:** Turn this vulnerability into a State-of-the-Art **Edge-AI feature**. Create a heterogeneous MoE pool containing 2 "Raw-Only" experts (simple linear projections on $X_{raw}$) and 3 "Deep" experts (operating on TSFM hidden states). 
    *   Because the RR-MoA router evaluates the raw signal *before* the TSFM executes, it can act as a **gatekeeper**. 
    *   If the router selects *only* the Raw-Only experts for a given window, **the 35M-parameter TSFM forward pass is dynamically skipped for that window.**
*   **Why it elevates the paper:** This perfectly bridges the "Simple vs. Complex" forecasting debate. The authors can claim: *"RR-MoA dynamically reduces foundation model inference FLOPs by up to 80% on stationary datasets, reserving the heavy TSFM only for volatile temporal excursions."* 

### 3. The Theoretical Mic-Drop: The Sufficient Statistic Router (SSR)
**Targets:** Bridging the gap between the math (Proposition 2) and the code.

*   **The Vulnerability:** Proposition 2 formally proves that the exact routing information destroyed by RevIN is the leakage residual $I(M, \Sigma ; E | S)$. Yet, the paper's actual router uses a 1.1K-parameter `Conv1d + AdaptivePool` black box on the entire sequence $X_{raw}$. 
*   **The Architectural Tweak:** Prove Proposition 2 empirically by replacing the Conv1D router with a microscopic **Sufficient Statistic Router (SSR)**. Extract only the per-window mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$) of the raw input, and route based entirely on those two scalars:
    $$ \text{Gate Logits} = \text{Linear}(\text{ReLU}(\text{Linear}([\mu(X_{raw}), \sigma(X_{raw})]))) $$
*   **Why it elevates the paper:** If a 10-parameter SSR matches the routing entropy and MSE gains of the Conv1D router, it is a devastatingly elegant empirical proof of their theorem. It proves that deep feature extraction for routing is a mirage—the physical statistics are truly all the router needs. ACs highly reward architectures that are physically instantiated from the paper's own proofs.

### 4. The Fusion Upgrade: Orthogonal Dual-Stream (ODS)
**Targets:** The ad-hoc nature of the Dual-Stream fix (Section 4.2).

*   **The Vulnerability:** To close the gap with DLinear, the authors use a learned scalar ($\alpha_k$) to linearly blend the Raw prediction and the TSFM prediction. Because both branches optimize the exact same MSE loss, the TSFM branch acts as a parameter sink, often lazily learning the same linear trends as the Raw branch.
*   **The Algorithmic Tweak:** Force the foundation model to *only* learn what the raw signal cannot express via **Residual Boosting**.
    *   Train the Raw-MLP branch to predict the target $Y$.
    *   Force the TSFM adapter branch to predict the *residual error* of the raw prediction: $\mathcal{L}_{TSFM} = \text{MSE}(\hat{Y}_{TSFM}, \; Y - \hat{Y}_{Raw})$.
