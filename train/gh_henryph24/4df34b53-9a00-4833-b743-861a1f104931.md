## The one edit that deserves deeper analysis: L1 (Honest TSFM disclosure)

This is the only edit a reviewer might read as weakening the paper. Let me argue both sides:

**Surface case for "this hurts the paper":**
- The new paragraph explicitly says: *"the pure raw-MLP MoE is essentially indistinguishable from Dual-Stream on average (+0.4% mean delta)"* and *"beats Dual-Stream outright on ETTm2 (−16.6%) and Electricity (−1.5%)"*.
- A skim-reader might walk away thinking "the 35M-parameter TSFM is useless."
- This dilutes the headline story that "RR-MoA on a TSFM closes the DLinear gap."

**Stronger case for "this strengthens the paper":**

1. **The ablation would have been demanded in review.** Reviewer #2 in the recent audit pass explicitly raised this exact attack ("Dual-Stream Trojan Horse — is the TSFM dead weight?"). Without the disclosure, a reviewer who runs the ablation themselves (or asks for it in review) gets the same finding and writes a much harsher version of the same paragraph, framing it as "the authors hid this." With the disclosure, the same finding becomes a demonstration of scientific honesty.

2. **The central scientific claim is preserved exactly.** The paper's core contribution is the **diagnosis** of normalization-induced routing collapse (Proposition 2 + the AdaMix collapse evidence). This contribution is **independent** of whether the TSFM is the best backbone for forecasting. The disclosure paragraph explicitly says so: *"This does not weaken the central diagnosis of the paper (normalization-induced routing collapse, which is independent of which backbone is used)."*

3. **The finding is actually a more interesting pattern than "TSFM helps universally."** "TSFM contributes complementary signal on datasets with complex non-linear dynamics, but is essentially neutral on datasets where DLinear is already near-optimal" is a dataset-conditional result that gives the field useful guidance. It's more publishable than "we always win."

4. **Pre-emption converts an attack vector into a strength.** The reviewer audit framework I've been using throughout this session draws a distinction between **"attack vectors a reviewer will find and use against you"** vs **"limitations you proactively disclose and address."** The same observation has wildly different review impact depending on which category it falls in:
   - Reviewer finds it: "the authors didn't run the obvious ablation; this paper is sloppy" → score drop
   - Authors disclose it: "the authors honestly characterized the contribution scope; their core diagnosis survives the ablation" → score stable or up

5. **The deployment motivation in §1 already justifies the TSFM independently of forecasting MSE.** The "Why frozen at all?" paragraph (which I cleaned up earlier this session) gives three deployment arguments for TSFMs over DLinear that have nothing to do with whether the TSFM contributes complementary forecasting signal: cross-task reuse, zero-shot tenant onboarding, and elimination of $O(N)$ per-tenant training pipelines. The disclosure doesn't undermine these.

**Net for L1**: it converts a 2-point review hit (sloppy paper that hides the ablation) into a 0.5-point review gain (honest paper that addresses the obvious counter-attack). On balance, **clearly net positive for solidity, with a small surface cost on perceived "marketing"**.
