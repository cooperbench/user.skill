58-Pioneering time series foundation models (TSFMs) such as MOMENT and TimesFM pair a frozen backbone with a task-specific head. Because downstream series mix heterogeneous regimes, the natural upgrade is a mixture-of-experts (MoE) head: a router over architecturally distinct experts, one per window. However, this upgrade fails on instance-normalized backbones: RevIN and similar layers strip the per-window mean and variance the router needs, a failure we call \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}. Whenever joint training makes even one backbone transformer block trainable, routing entropy falls to $0.000$, meaning that every input is sent to a single expert and the rest are never activated. A $720$-run sweep across standard MoE rescue mechanisms (load balancing, z-loss, entropy regularization, ReLU routing, expert-choice) recovers at most $10.9\%$ MSE ($2.7\times$ worse than our causal intervention), because the failure is in the router's input, not its optimization. We formalize this via a mutual-information decomposition; the resulting signal-ratio statistic, computable before training, predicts dataset vulnerability (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.88$, $p{<}0.002$) and flags boundary cases. The diagnosis prescribes a minimal causal intervention: \emph{Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters} (RR-MoA) routes on the raw, pre-normalization input. RR-MoA sets a new SOTA for adapter-based TSFM transfer under a strictly frozen backbone: $54/54$ wins on the primary backbone ($26$--$79\%$ MSE improvement over the best fixed adapter), strictly beats LoRA, TRACE, AdaMix, and full fine-tuning at $p{<}0.001$, and generalizes across four further backbones and an imputation task. The failure is specific to instance normalization: backbones such as Moirai that use LayerNorm instead of RevIN do not collapse (\S\ref{sec:cross_backbone}). 228:\textbf{TSFM Adaptation.} The standard recipe attaches a lightweight module to a frozen TSFM and tunes it on downstream data: LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora}, bottleneck adapters~\citep{houlsby2019adapters}, or a linear probe~\citep{goswami2024moment}. 232-\textbf{MoE routing collapse and rescue.} Existing diagnoses of routing collapse cluster, broadly, into three threads. \emph{Optimization-side}: load-balancing losses~\citep{fedus2022switch}, z-loss~\citep{zoph2022stmoe}, and AdaMix's hidden-state router~\citep{wang2022adamix} target training dynamics. \emph{Representation-geometry}: \citet{chi2022representation} attribute collapse to token clustering around expert centroids that emerges \emph{during} routing learning and propose hyperspherical routing scores. \emph{Architectural / paradigm}: \citet{zhou2022expertchoice} invert the token--expert paradigm via Expert-Choice routing, \citet{wang2025remoe} replace top-$k$ with ReLU gating, and \citet{hua2025inputaware} decouple the router from the task objective so that routing decisions reflect input structure rather than task gradients. Whenever joint training makes even one backbone transformer block trainable, routing entropy falls to $0.000$, meaning that every input is sent to a single expert and the rest are never activated. A 720-run sweep across standard MoE rescue mechanisms (load balancing, z-loss, entropy regularization, ReLU routing, expert-choice) recovers at most $10.9\%$ MSE ($2.7\times$ worse than our causal intervention), because the failure is in the router's input, not its optimization. We formalize this via a mutual-information decomposition; the resulting signal-ratio statistic, computable before training, predicts dataset vulnerability (Spearman $\rho = -0.88$, $p < 0.002$) and flags boundary cases. The diagnosis prescribes a minimal causal intervention: *Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters* (RR-MoA) routes on the raw, pre-normalization input. RR-MoA sets a new SOTA for adapter-based TSFM transfer under a strictly frozen backbone: 54/54 wins on the primary backbone (26–79% MSE improvement over the best fixed adapter), strictly beats LoRA, TRACE, AdaMix, and full fine-tuning at $p < 0.001$, and generalizes across four further backbones and an imputation task. Eight causal controls including a vision-modality replication isolate the cause to instance normalization, and predict a *Frozen Paradox*: frozen RR-MoA beats full fine-tuning by 12–79%, dominated by the MoE pool's architectural advantage with a smaller within-architecture freeze effect ($\leq 13\%$) attributable to gradient co-adaptation (Proposition 1).
