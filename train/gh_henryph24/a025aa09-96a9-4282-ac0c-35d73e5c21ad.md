228	\section{Related Work}
229	
230	\textbf{TSFM Adaptation.} The standard recipe attaches a lightweight module to a frozen TSFM and tunes it on downstream data: LoRA~\citep{hu2022lora}, bottleneck adapters~\citep{houlsby2019adapters}, or a linear probe~\citep{goswami2024moment}. Recent variations explore multi-scale finetuning~\citep{qiao2025msft}, importance-based LoRA selection~\citep{li2025trace}, input-representation adaptation~\citep{benechehab2025adapts}, structured pruning~\citep{zhao2025prune}, in-context tuning~\citep{faw2025icf}, and PEFT alternatives for Chronos~\citep{gupta2024beyondlora}; complementary work computes transferability scores for backbone selection (TEMPLATE,~\citealp{zhang2025template}) or provides a unified evaluation suite for general forecasting models (GIFT-Eval,~\citealp{woo2024gifteval}). These works largely vary the finetuning \textit{strategy} (where to inject parameters, what to prune, what to prompt, which transferability score to use) while leaving the per-input adapter \textit{topology} fixed; AdaPTS~\citep{benechehab2025adapts} proposes several adapter variants and reports the best per dataset/horizon, treating adapter choice as a per-task hyperparameter rather than routing per input window. We instead ask whether the adapter topology should be chosen per input window via routing over heterogeneous experts; the closest precedent in spirit is mixture-of-adapters in the LLM setting (e.g., AdaMix~\citep{wang2022adamix}; AdaMix mixes copies of one adapter type, whereas RR-MoA uses $K$ topologically distinct expert architectures), whereas, in the TSFM literature we surveyed, we did not find prior work that uses a per-window mixture over distinct adapter topologies.
231	
232	\textbf{MoE in Foundation Models.} Mixture-of-experts routing~\citep{fedus2022switch} has been used \emph{within} TSFM backbones to scale capacity, with experts placed inside the backbone architecture: Moirai-MoE~\citep{liu2025moiraimoe} routes individual tokens to data-driven experts that automatically specialize across diverse time series patterns, and Time-MoE~\citep{shi2024timemoe} uses sparse activation to reach billions of parameters. RR-MoA applies MoE on an orthogonal axis, at the \emph{adapter level} on top of a frozen backbone. The two axes operate on different parts of the model: stacking RR-MoA on Moirai-MoE further improves accuracy on the datasets we tested (\S\ref{sec:cross_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:moirai_moe}).
233	
234	\textbf{MoE routing collapse and rescue.} Existing diagnoses of routing collapse cluster, broadly, into three threads. \emph{Optimization-side}: load-balancing losses~\citep{fedus2022switch}, z-loss~\citep{zoph2022stmoe}, and AdaMix's hidden-state router~\citep{wang2022adamix} target training dynamics; \citet{guo2025expertspec} further argue that the standard load-balance loss itself causes expert overlap and propose orthogonality and variance regularizers. \emph{Representation-geometry}: \citet{chi2022representation} attribute collapse to token clustering around expert centroids that emerges \emph{during} routing learning and propose hyperspherical routing scores; \citet{wu2024mhmoe} split each token into sub-tokens to raise expert activation. \emph{Architectural / paradigm}: \citet{zhou2022expertchoice} invert the token--expert paradigm via Expert-Choice routing, \citet{wang2025remoe} replace top-$k$ with ReLU gating, and \citet{hua2025inputaware} decouple the router from the task objective so that routing decisions reflect input structure rather than task gradients. Our setting differs from all three: the routing signal is structurally absent from the router's input \emph{by design of an upstream architectural normalizer (RevIN)}, before training dynamics, geometry, or routing-paradigm choices can act. Sweeping five rescue families spanning the optimization and routing-paradigm threads (load balancing, z-loss, Expert-Choice, ReLU gating, and entropy regularization) across twelve configurations on MOMENT$+$RevIN, none recover MSE to within $2.7{\times}$ of RR-MoA (Table~\ref{tab:rescue}). Concurrent work in NLP/vision restructures the routing decision (AoE~\citep{lv2025aoe} and Routing-Free MoE~\citep{liu2026routingfree} eliminate the external router; ERMoE~\citep{cheng2025ermoe} replaces softmax+load-balance with eigenbasis-score routing) reaches related conclusions about restructuring the routing decision; in our setting (\S\ref{sec:main_results}), self-gated experts on raw input match RR-MoA, while the same architecture on hidden states collapses. \citet{wang2026myth} argues MoE specialization reflects hidden-state geometry; we extend this upstream by identifying what shapes that geometry on TSFMs.
235	
236	{\looseness=-1 \textbf{Normalization in Time Series.} RevIN~\citep{kim2021revin} mitigates distribution shift; DishTS~\citep{fan2023dishts} and Non-stationary Transformers~\citep{liu2022nonstationary} partially recover stripped statistics; FAN~\citep{ye2024fan} extends instance normalization beyond $(\mu,\sigma)$ to dominant Fourier components. Concurrently, \citet{berthelier2026revin} independently show that several RevIN components are redundant or detrimental, and \citet{zou2025ibnorm} argue that variance-centric normalization (BatchNorm, LayerNorm, RMSNorm) is suboptimal for representation learning because two representations with identical mean and variance can encode different amounts of task-relevant information. We identify a specific, mechanistic consequence of this principle that has not been characterized in the MoE literature: when the stripped statistics carry the \emph{routing} signal in a downstream MoE adapter, instance normalization causes the router to collapse to a single expert under unfreezing, a failure mode distinct from the optimization-induced collapse studied in standard MoE rescue literature. Section~\ref{sec:method} formalizes this diagnosis and presents the minimal architectural fix it implies.}

57	\begin{abstract}
58	Pioneering time series foundation models (TSFMs) such as MOMENT, TimesFM, and TTM pair a frozen backbone with a task-specific head. Because downstream series mix heterogeneous regimes, the natural upgrade is a mixture-of-experts (MoE) head: a router over architecturally distinct experts, one per window.
