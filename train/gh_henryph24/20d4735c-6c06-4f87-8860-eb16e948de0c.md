4	61:\section{Introduction}
5	66:Standard TSFM adapters~\citep{goswami2024moment, das2024timesfm, nie2023patchtst, hu2022lora} pool hidden states and project them to the forecast horizon through the same head for every window (Figure~\ref{fig:problem_overview}a). Real time series mix heterogeneous regimes (quiet baselines, seasonal excursions, abrupt shape changes), and instance normalization handles only the scale-and-offset component of that heterogeneity --- residual \emph{shape} heterogeneity (attention patterns, temporal structure) persists in the hidden states and is what motivates a per-window mixture of expert adapters: a pool of topologically distinct heads with a learned router that assigns each window to the most suitable one. Keeping the backbone frozen while training only the adapter is also what makes deployment practical: one TSFM in GPU memory can serve thousands of tenants through small ($\sim$1.7\,MB) per-task adapters swapped in microseconds, an arrangement that per-dataset models like DLinear~\citep{zeng2023dlinear} cannot match (Appendices~\ref{app:deployment},~\ref{app:benchmark}).
6	68:This natural mixture-of-experts approach fails on TSFMs that use instance normalization once any backbone layers are unfrozen (Figure~\ref{fig:problem_overview}b). When the standard MoE recipe~\citep{wang2022adamix, fedus2022switch}, routing on hidden states with load balancing, is applied to MOMENT with RevIN, routing entropy drops to zero in most unfrozen configurations (Table~\ref{tab:adamix}). The router learns to send every window to the same expert, and the remaining experts are never used. The mechanism is what we call \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}: RevIN~\citep{kim2021revin} strips the per-window mean and variance that the router needs to distinguish input regimes, leaving it with homogenized hidden states. Even with the backbone frozen, hidden-state routing still underperforms; with the backbone unfrozen, the dominant expert receives larger gradients which reshape the backbone to serve it even more, closing a co-adaptation feedback loop within tens of training steps (Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory}). The failure is specific to instance normalization: backbones such as Moirai that use LayerNorm instead of RevIN do not collapse (\S\ref{sec:cross_backbone}). Standard MoE rescue mechanisms (load balancing, entropy regularization, z-loss, ReLU routing, expert-choice) also do not help (Table~\ref{tab:rescue}), because the problem lies in the router's \emph{input}, not its optimization.
7	70:Because the diagnosis pinpoints normalization as the cause, the fix principle is direct. \emph{Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters} (RR-MoA) routes on the raw, pre-normalization input $\mathbf{X}_\text{raw}$ while keeping the backbone strictly frozen; only the router and five canonical expert heads are trainable. The same principle predicts a \emph{Frozen Paradox}: unfreezing triggers co-adaptation collapse (Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}), so frozen adapters should beat full fine-tuning, which we confirm empirically.
8	226:\section{Related Work}
9	230:\textbf{MoE in Foundation Models.} Mixture-of-experts routing~\citep{fedus2022switch} has been used \emph{within} TSFM backbones to scale capacity, with experts placed inside the backbone architecture: Moirai-MoE~\citep{liu2025moiraimoe} routes frequency bands across internal experts, and Time-MoE~\citep{shi2024timemoe} uses sparse activation to reach billions of parameters. RR-MoA applies MoE on an orthogonal axis, at the \emph{adapter level} on top of a frozen backbone. The two axes are complementary rather than competing: stacking RR-MoA on Moirai-MoE further improves accuracy (\S\ref{sec:cross_backbone}, Table~\ref{tab:moirai_moe}).
10	232:\textbf{MoE routing collapse and rescue.} Routing collapse has been framed two ways. \emph{Optimization-side}: load-balancing losses~\citep{fedus2022switch}, z-loss~\citep{zoph2022stmoe}, Expert-Choice routing~\citep{zhou2022expertchoice}, ReLU gating~\citep{zhang2025remoe}, and AdaMix's hidden-state router~\citep{wang2022adamix} all attack training dynamics. \emph{Representation-side}: \citet{chi2022representation} attribute collapse to post-training token clustering around expert centroids and propose hyperspherical routing scores, and \citet{hua2025inputaware} explicitly decouple routing decisions from task optimization to better capture input structure. Our setting differs from both: the routing signal is structurally absent from the router's input \emph{by design of an upstream architectural normalizer (RevIN)}, before any training dynamics or post-training feature clustering can act. Sweeping the five optimization-side families across eleven configurations on MOMENT$+$RevIN, none recover MSE to within $2.7{\times}$ of RR-MoA (Table~\ref{tab:rescue}). Concurrent work in NLP/vision toward router-free MoEs (AoE~\citep{lv2025aoe}, ERMoE~\citep{cheng2025ermoe}, Routing-Free MoE~\citep{liu2026routingfree}) supports the broader view that the router's input matters: in our setting (\S\ref{sec:main_results}), self-gated experts on raw input match RR-MoA, while the same architecture on hidden states collapses. \citet{wang2026myth} proves MoE specialization reflects hidden-state geometry; we extend this upstream by identifying what shapes that geometry on TSFMs.

44	1105:\subsection{Theoretical Depth of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}}
45	1111:Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. In rate-distortion theory~\citep{cover2006elements}, this compression has rate $R_\text{strip} = I(X; M, \Sigma \mid S)$ and distortion (for routing) $D_\text{route} = H(E \mid S) - H(E \mid X) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S)$, which is exactly Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i). Under Gaussianity, the signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D})$ is proportional to the rate-distortion slope at the normalization operating point: it measures how much routing capacity is consumed by the statistics that normalization strips. Specifically, if the joint $(M, \Sigma, S)$ is multivariate Gaussian, then $I(M, \Sigma; E) \propto \log(1 + R(\mathcal{D}))$ (by the Gaussian mutual information formula), making $R(\mathcal{D})$ a \emph{monotone surrogate for the bits lost to normalization}. The validated $\rho = -0.88$ correlation (Figure~\ref{fig:signal_ratio}) is thus not a statistical coincidence but a consequence of this rate-distortion structure.

57	\begin{abstract}
58	Time series foundation models (TSFMs) such as MOMENT, Moirai, and Chronos ship with a shared, frozen backbone and a task-specific head. Real series mix heterogeneous regimes — quiet baselines, seasonal excursions, abrupt shape changes — whose shape structure survives instance normalization, and different regimes are best served by different aggregation topologies; a single head must commit to one. The mixture-of-experts (MoE) upgrade replaces the head with a router and a pool of architecturally distinct experts, one selected per input window. We identify and characterize a failure mode of this upgrade on every TSFM that uses instance normalization: once any backbone layer is unfrozen, the router converges to a one-hot assignment, routing entropy collapses to $0.000$, and only one expert ever receives a gradient update. We name this \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}. Its mechanism is direct: RevIN, the instance-normalization layer built into MOMENT and inherited by any TSFM that adopts the same recipe, strips the per-window mean and variance the router needs. A $720$-run sweep across five standard MoE rescue mechanisms (load balancing, z-loss, entropy regularization, ReLU routing, expert-choice) converges to the same collapsed state, recovering at most $10.9\%$ MSE. This is still $2.7\times$ worse than the causal fix below, because the failure is in the router's input, not its optimization. We formalize the mechanism via a mutual-information decomposition that yields a signal-ratio statistic, computable before training. The statistic predicts which datasets are vulnerable to collapse (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.88$, $p{<}0.002$) and correctly flags boundary datasets where raw routing should not help. The diagnosis prescribes a one-line \emph{causal fix}: \emph{Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters} (RR-MoA) routes on the raw, pre-normalization input. RR-MoA wins $54/54$ dataset/freeze-level cells on the primary backbone ($26$--$79\%$ MSE improvement over the best fixed adapter) and generalizes across four further backbones and an imputation task. Eight causal controls including a vision-modality replication isolate the cause to instance normalization, and the diagnosis predicts a verified \emph{Frozen Paradox}: frozen adapters beat full fine-tuning by $12$--$79\%$. Applying the same raw-signal principle at the expert level (Residual-IA\textsuperscript{+}) closes the remaining gap to per-dataset supervised baselines.

61	\section{Introduction}
62	\label{sec:intro}
63	
64	Time series foundation models (TSFMs) such as MOMENT~\citep{goswami2024moment}, TimesFM~\citep{das2024timesfm}, Chronos~\citep{ansari2024chronos}, Timer-XL~\citep{liu2025timerxl}, and Moirai~\citep{woo2024moirai} now provide powerful pretrained representations for time series, with several recent variants pushing toward billion-parameter scales~\citep{lee2024units, rasul2024lagllama, shi2024timemoe, ekambaram2024ttm, liu2026timers1}. Using one of these backbones on a downstream task still requires attaching a lightweight adapter head that maps hidden states to predictions, and the design of that adapter, more than the backbone choice, determines deployment utility.
65	
66	Standard TSFM adapters~\citep{goswami2024moment, das2024timesfm, nie2023patchtst, hu2022lora} pool hidden states and project them to the forecast horizon through the same head for every window (Figure~\ref{fig:problem_overview}a). Real time series mix heterogeneous regimes (quiet baselines, seasonal excursions, abrupt shape changes), and instance normalization handles only the scale-and-offset component of that heterogeneity --- residual \emph{shape} heterogeneity (attention patterns, temporal structure) persists in the hidden states and is what motivates a per-window mixture of expert adapters: a pool of topologically distinct heads with a learned router that assigns each window to the most suitable one. Keeping the backbone frozen while training only the adapter is also what makes deployment practical: one TSFM in GPU memory can serve thousands of tenants through small ($\sim$1.7\,MB) per-task adapters swapped in microseconds, an arrangement that per-dataset models like DLinear~\citep{zeng2023dlinear} cannot match (Appendices~\ref{app:deployment},~\ref{app:benchmark}).
67	
68	This natural mixture-of-experts approach fails on TSFMs that use instance normalization once any backbone layers are unfrozen (Figure~\ref{fig:problem_overview}b). When the standard MoE recipe~\citep{wang2022adamix, fedus2022switch}, routing on hidden states with load balancing, is applied to MOMENT with RevIN, routing entropy drops to zero in most unfrozen configurations (Table~\ref{tab:adamix}). The router learns to send every window to the same expert, and the remaining experts are never used. The mechanism is what we call \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}: RevIN~\citep{kim2021revin} strips the per-window mean and variance that the router needs to distinguish input regimes, leaving it with homogenized hidden states. Even with the backbone frozen, hidden-state routing still underperforms; with the backbone unfrozen, the dominant expert receives larger gradients which reshape the backbone to serve it even more, closing a co-adaptation feedback loop within tens of training steps (Figure~\ref{fig:trajectory}). The failure is specific to instance normalization: backbones such as Moirai that use LayerNorm instead of RevIN do not collapse (\S\ref{sec:cross_backbone}). Standard MoE rescue mechanisms (load balancing, entropy regularization, z-loss, ReLU routing, expert-choice) also do not help (Table~\ref{tab:rescue}), because the problem lies in the router's \emph{input}, not its optimization.
69	
70	Because the diagnosis pinpoints normalization as the cause, the fix principle is direct. \emph{Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters} (RR-MoA) routes on the raw, pre-normalization input $\mathbf{X}_\text{raw}$ while keeping the backbone strictly frozen; only the router and five canonical expert heads are trainable. The same principle predicts a \emph{Frozen Paradox}: unfreezing triggers co-adaptation collapse (Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}), so frozen adapters should beat full fine-tuning, which we confirm empirically.
71	
72	\paragraph{Contributions.}
73	\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em, itemsep=0.15em, topsep=0.2em]
74	\item We identify \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}, a failure mode of MoE adapters on TSFMs that is distinct from the optimization-induced collapse studied in standard MoE-rescue literature, and isolate it through eight causal controls that converge on normalization as the cause.
75	\item We formalize the mechanism through a mutual-information decomposition (Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}) that yields a signal-ratio predictor of which datasets benefit from raw routing, including boundary cases where they do not, and validate the same principle across three normalization families, five backbones, and a vision modality.

1003	\section{Deployment-Regime Motivation}
1004	\label{app:deployment}
1005	
1006	The frozen-backbone paradigm is motivated by three deployment regimes where full fine-tuning is infeasible:
1007	
1008	\textbf{(i) Multi-tenant cloud serving.} A single TSFM sits in GPU VRAM and serves thousands of tenants via lightweight, hot-swappable per-tenant adapters in host RAM. Per-tenant DLinear requires collecting, storing, and periodically retraining on each tenant's historical data ($O(N)$ training pipelines for $N$ tenants), whereas the frozen backbone is loaded once and adapter weights (${\sim}50$--$500$K parameters, ${\sim}0.2$--$2$\,MB) are swapped from host RAM in microseconds. At $N{=}1{,}000$ tenants, the adapter approach eliminates $1{,}000$ independent training jobs and enables instant onboarding of new tenants via zero-shot transfer, capabilities absent from per-tenant supervised baselines.
1009	
1010	\textbf{(ii) On-device and edge deployment.} One backbone is shipped with the device image; per-site adapters are updated over low-bandwidth links. Collecting each site's training data centrally is often impossible for privacy or latency reasons.
1011	
1012	\textbf{(iii) Cross-task reuse.} The same frozen backbone drives forecasting, imputation, and classification via different expert pools. We demonstrate the first two tasks explicitly in the main text.

656:\paragraph{Implication.} Routing collapse on instance-normalized backbones illustrates a broader pattern: when a foundation model's input pipeline destroys signal a downstream router needs, optimization-side rescues (load balancing, z-loss, ReLU routing) cannot recover what is structurally absent: the fix is more readily approached by restoring the missing input than by retuning the optimizer. The same mechanism appears in a vision control (Appendix~\ref{app:vision_moe}); the signal-ratio predictor $R(\mathcal{D})$ predicts which datasets benefit from raw routing (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.88$, $p{<}0.002$, with a held-out boundary case correctly flagged as low-benefit), and generalization to normalization families we did not test is bounded by the assumptions of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}.
658:\paragraph{Limitations.} Three boundaries scope our claims. \emph{(i)~Normalization specificity.} Our diagnosis applies to backbones with instance-level normalization (RevIN, BatchNorm, GroupNorm); backbones without it (Chronos, Timer-XL) show no collapse and no RR-MoA gain, as Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} predicts. Extensions to attention-based or learned normalizers are not characterized. \emph{(ii)~Signal-ratio boundary cases.} The signal-ratio predictor $R(\mathcal{D})$ is a single monotone proxy for variance contribution; on Solar ($R{=}0.06$) it correctly flags low benefit but is not sharp. Datasets with strong shape-statistics coupling fall outside the tight regime of the bound. \emph{(iii)~Forecasting and imputation only.} We evaluate forecasting and imputation on LTSF benchmarks. Anomaly detection, classification, and irregularly-sampled tasks are deferred to future work.
1063:\section{Proposition Proofs}
1066:\textbf{Proof of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}} (Routing Information Loss Under RevIN, revised form).
1096:\emph{Gaussian reference.} Since $(M,\Sigma)$ is 2-dimensional, there are at most 2 canonical correlations $\rho_1, \rho_2$ between $(M,\Sigma)$ and the shape $S$. For jointly Gaussian variables, $I(M,\Sigma; S) = -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^2 \log(1 - \rho_i^2)$. This yields $\varepsilon_\text{Gauss}$ ranging from $0.09$ nats (Weather, $\rho_\text{max}{=}0.35$) to $1.26$ nats (Exchange, $\rho_\text{max}{=}0.89$). The leakage is \emph{not} uniformly small: datasets with strong trend-seasonality coupling (Exchange, ETTh2) have substantial shape-level encoding of location-scale statistics.
1100:\emph{Tightness condition.} The bound~(ii) is informative when $I(M,\Sigma; E) \gg \varepsilon$. Since $I(M,\Sigma; E)$ increases with the location-scale variance, the signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D}) = [\mathrm{Var}(M) + \mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)] / \overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)}$ is a monotone proxy for the tightness of the bound. This explains the $\rho{=}{-}0.88$ correlation: high-$R$ datasets (Exchange, $R{=}2.17$) have $I(M,\Sigma; E) \gg \varepsilon$, producing large gains ($-68.5\%$); low-$R$ datasets (Traffic, $R{=}0.14$) have $I(M,\Sigma; E) \approx \varepsilon$, producing no gain ($+2.9\%$).
1105:\subsection{Theoretical Depth of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}}
1108:While the MI decomposition in Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} is algebraically a chain-rule identity, it has deeper information-theoretic content when connected to rate-distortion theory and phase transitions.
1111:Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. In rate-distortion theory~\citep{cover2006elements}, this compression has rate $R_\text{strip} = I(X; M, \Sigma \mid S)$ and distortion (for routing) $D_\text{route} = H(E \mid S) - H(E \mid X) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S)$, which is exactly Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i). Under Gaussianity, the signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D})$ is proportional to the rate-distortion slope at the normalization operating point: it measures how much routing capacity is consumed by the statistics that normalization strips. Specifically, if the joint $(M, \Sigma, S)$ is multivariate Gaussian, then $I(M, \Sigma; E) \propto \log(1 + R(\mathcal{D}))$ (by the Gaussian mutual information formula), making $R(\mathcal{D})$ a \emph{monotone surrogate for the bits lost to normalization}. The validated $\rho = -0.88$ correlation (Figure~\ref{fig:signal_ratio}) is thus not a statistical coincidence but a consequence of this rate-distortion structure.
1126:\emph{Validation.} For the median $R(\mathcal{D}) = 0.67$ across our benchmarks, Proposition~\ref{prop:phase_transition} predicts $\alpha^* \approx 0.40$. The dose-response experiment (Figure~\ref{fig:dose_response}; 150 runs) confirms this: MSE degrades by $+5.4\%$ for $\alpha \leq 0.75$ but jumps to $+283\%$ at $\alpha = 1.0$. The sharpness of the transition arises from the softmax: when $P_\mathrm{route}(\alpha) / P_\mathrm{noise}$ drops below ${\sim}1$, routing weights converge to uniform regardless of expert relevance.
1166:The RevIN-encoder pipeline implements a form of the information bottleneck~\citep{tishby2000ib}: it compresses $X$ into a representation $H = f_\theta(S)$ that is optimized for downstream prediction while discarding ``irrelevant'' variability (the per-window location-scale statistics). The routing MI loss from Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i) is the price paid for this compression \emph{at the routing decision point}. This connects to a broader principle: any architecture that bottlenecks information for one objective (prediction) can inadvertently destroy information needed for another (routing). The vision cross-modality control (Appendix~\ref{app:vision_moe}) confirms this is not RevIN-specific: InstanceNorm1d on ViT patches creates the same bottleneck, while InstanceNorm2d on ResNet spatial features does not, because spatial features are not the routing signal for classification.
1168:\textbf{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}} (Gradient Co-Adaptation Drives Entropy Collapse).
1495:\textbf{What the router learns.} Per-sample routing decisions correlate with amplitude and volatility, the very statistics RevIN strips (Figure~\ref{fig:routing_viz}). On ETTh1, Expert~0 (mean-pool) serves as the generalist ($53\%$), while Expert~2 (max-pool) handles low-amplitude windows and Expert~3 (attention-pool) handles high-amplitude windows. The router reserves specialized processing for windows at the distribution tails, empirically validating the conditional independence assumption $E \perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid (M,\Sigma)$ of Part~(iii) of Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}.

1506:\textbf{Sufficient statistic router (SSR).} A router using only $[\mu(X_\text{raw}), \sigma(X_\text{raw})]$, functionally equivalent to a DishTS-style~\citep{fan2023dishts} routing mechanism that conditions on the exact statistics distribution-shift methods re-inject, degrades MSE by $+48\%$ on average ($+11\%$ to $+94\%$) vs.\ the Conv1d router across all 8 forecasting datasets. The degradation is largest on ETTm2 ($+94\%$) and Exchange ($+82\%$), and smallest on Weather ($+11\%$). While Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} correctly identifies the \emph{direction} of the information loss, effective routing requires temporal shape patterns beyond location-scale statistics.
1514:\textbf{Statistic-aware information bottleneck (SAIB).} An auxiliary loss forces the router's penultimate representation to predict the window-level $[\mu, \sigma]$, directly operationalizing Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}. With SAIB coefficients $\lambda \in \{0.1, 1.0\}$, MSE is unchanged on ETTm1 ($0.395$ vs $0.397$ baseline) and slightly degraded on Weather ($0.250$ vs $0.236$). Routing entropy increases to $1.55$ (near-maximum $\log 5 = 1.61$), indicating the auxiliary loss disperses routing weights without improving forecast accuracy. This confirms the Conv1d router already encodes $\mu, \sigma$ without explicit supervision.
1690:All three normalizers cause entropy collapse to ${\leq}0.004$, while removing normalization entirely keeps entropy stable at $0.817$. The mechanism is the same in each case: the normalizer strips per-window statistics before the encoder, the router receives homogenized hidden states, and gradient co-adaptation amplifies the initial routing bias into full collapse. The mechanism therefore holds across the three instance-level normalizers we tested (RevIN, BatchNorm1d, GroupNorm), not just RevIN. Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} predicts that collapse occurs specifically when the stripped statistics carry routing signal; a cross-modality control on ResNet-18 and ViT-B/16 (Appendix~\ref{app:vision_moe}) validates this: InstanceNorm1d on ViT patch embeddings \emph{causes} collapse ($0.000\pm0.000$), while InstanceNorm2d on ResNet spatial features does \emph{not}, exactly as the theory predicts.
1717:\textbf{ResNet-18: normalization prevents collapse.} Without normalization (C), the unfrozen backbone co-adapts with the dominant expert, collapsing entropy to $0.184\pm0.260$, confirming Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}. Adding InstanceNorm2d (A) or BatchNorm2d (D) \emph{prevents} collapse ($1.48\pm0.02$), the \emph{opposite} of time series. Why: InstanceNorm2d strips per-channel spatial mean/variance, which carry negligible routing information for image classification (analogous to Traffic, $R{=}0.14$).
1721:\textbf{Unifying principle.} The collapse mechanism (Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen}) is modality-general. Whether normalization \emph{triggers} or \emph{prevents} collapse depends on whether the stripped statistics overlap with the routing signal (Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}): ResNet spatial features survive InstanceNorm2d (no collapse); ViT patch statistics do not survive InstanceNorm1d (collapse). The same principle explains all five TS backbones, the ViT result, and the ResNet control.

The narrow converged range ($0.459$--$0.482$) means the magnitude does not strongly differentiate datasets: the signal is binary (raw $>$ norm), not graded.

1111:Instance normalization is a \emph{deterministic lossy compression}: it maps $X \mapsto S$, discarding $(M, \Sigma)$. In rate-distortion theory~\citep{cover2006elements}, this compression has rate $R_\text{strip} = I(X; M, \Sigma \mid S)$ and distortion (for routing) $D_\text{route} = H(E \mid S) - H(E \mid X) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S)$, which is exactly Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp}(i). Specifically, if the joint $(M, \Sigma, S)$ is multivariate Gaussian, then $I(M, \Sigma; E) \propto \log(1 + R(\mathcal{D}))$ (by the Gaussian mutual information formula), making $R(\mathcal{D})$ a \emph{monotone surrogate for the bits lost to normalization}. The mechanism is what we call \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}: RevIN~\citep{kim2021revin} strips the per-window mean and variance that the router needs to distinguish input regimes, leaving it with homogenized hidden states. 566:In the limit where the router's signal lives entirely in the stripped statistics ($E \perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid (M,\Sigma)$), the bound saturates and $I(X; E) - I(S; E) = H(E)$: RevIN destroys the entire routing distribution. Datasets with strong shape-statistics coupling fall outside the tight regime of the bound. \emph{(iii)~Forecasting and imputation only.} We evaluate forecasting and imputation on LTSF benchmarks. Anomaly detection, classification, and irregularly-sampled tasks are deferred to future work.
