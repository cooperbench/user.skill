558	\textbf{Why RevIN destroys routing: formal analysis.} The experiments above converge on a single mechanism: instance normalization strips the per-window statistics that the router uses to tell input regimes apart. To pin this down, we ask what information the router actually has access to before and after normalization. Each input window $\mathbf{x}_i$ decomposes into a mean $M_i = \mu(\mathbf{x}_i)$, a scale $\Sigma_i = \sigma(\mathbf{x}_i)$, and a shape $\mathbf{S}_i = (\mathbf{x}_i - M_i)/\Sigma_i$. RevIN maps $\mathbf{x}_i \mapsto \mathbf{S}_i$ and discards $(M_i, \Sigma_i)$, so the question reduces to how much routing information lives in the discarded part. The mutual-information chain rule gives a clean answer.
559	
560	\begin{observation}[Routing Information Loss Under Instance Normalization]
561	\label{thm:mi_decomp}
562	Let $X$ be a random input window, $E \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ the expert assignment produced by any deterministic router, and $S = (X - M)/\Sigma$ the RevIN-normalized shape.
563	\begin{enumerate}
564	    \item[\textup{(i)}] \textbf{Exact decomposition.} $I(X; E) - I(S; E) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S) \geq 0$.
565	    \item[\textup{(ii)}] \textbf{Quantitative bound.} Without any independence assumption,
566	    \[
567	        I(X; E) - I(S; E) \;\geq\; I(M, \Sigma;\, E) \;-\; I(M, \Sigma;\, S),
568	    \]
569	    where $\varepsilon := I(M, \Sigma;\, S) \geq 0$ captures any residual ``leakage'' of the stripped statistics through the shape. Equality holds iff $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid E$.
570	\end{enumerate}
571	\end{observation}
572	
573	In the full-information corner case where the router's signal lives entirely in the stripped statistics ($E$ is a deterministic function of $(M,\Sigma)$ and $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S$ unconditionally; see Part~(iii) of the proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), $I(X; E) - I(S; E) = H(E)$: RevIN destroys the entire routing distribution. We therefore use the \emph{signal ratio} $R(\mathcal{D}) = [\mathrm{Var}(M) + \mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)] / \overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)}$ as a tractable monotone proxy for the variance contribution of the stripped statistics. The bound is empirically tight: CCA gives $\varepsilon \in [0.09, 1.26]$ nats, cross-validated within ${\pm}0.26$ nats by KSG~\citep{kraskov2004ksg}; a held-out classifier shows normalization retains only $16$--$41\%$ of routing entropy with bound (ii) gap averaging $0.075$ nats (Appendix~\ref{app:mi_tightness}). Training $\alpha \in [0,1]$ as a learnable parameter, gradient descent converges to $\alpha < 0.5$ on all six datasets ($30/30$ runs; Appendix~\ref{app:learnable_alpha}). $R(\mathcal{D})$ predicts the raw-routing benefit at Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.88$ ($p{<}0.002$, $n{=}9$; Figure~\ref{fig:signal_ratio}); $H(E \mid S)$ alone does not ($\rho{=}{-}0.12$), since routing \emph{potential} in the stripped statistics matters, not the MI loss itself. Traffic ($R{=}0.14$, $\Delta{=}{+}2.9\%$) is correctly predicted as a boundary case; Solar ($R{=}0.06$) is the one outlier (SSR ablation $+43\%$; Appendix~\ref{app:routing_ablations}). Full proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
