> DEVELOPER

"A mutual-information decomposition formalizes the mechanism: RevIN destroys routing MI by exactly the information in the stripped location-scale statistics, and this loss is lower-bounded by a quantity we can measure" explain this in easier language for me

> AGENT

Here's what it's saying in plain terms: **What RevIN does:** Before feeding a time series window to the encoder, RevIN subtracts the mean and divides by the standard deviation. This makes all windows "look the same" in terms of their level and spread. **Why that kills routing:** The router needs to tell windows apart to send them to different experts. But the main ways windows differ from each other *are* their mean and variance — "this window is high and volatile" vs "this window is low and stable." RevIN removes exactly that. **What the MI decomposition formalizes:** It's a mathematical statement that says: > The routing information lost = the information that was in the stripped (μ, σ) It's not "RevIN removes *some* useful info." It's a precise equation: the gap between how well you *could* route (with raw input) and how well you *can* route (after normalization) equals exactly the information content of the statistics that were removed. **"Lower-bounded by a quantity we can measure":** The signal ratio R(D) = [Var(μ) + Var(σ)] / Var(S) measures how much of the total signal variation lives in those stripped statistics. High R(D) means RevIN destroys a lot of routing info. Low R(D) means the stripped stats didn't matter much anyway. That's why R(D) predicts which datasets benefit from raw routing (ρ = -0.88) — including Traffic (R = 0.14) where RR-MoA correctly doesn't help. **The analogy:** Imagine sorting mail by zip code, but someone blacks out the zip code before you see it. The MI decomposition says: "the sorting ability you lost = exactly the information in the blacked-out zip code." And you can measure how much that matters by checking what fraction of the address information was in the zip code vs the street name.

> DEVELOPER

what does it mean by Spearman rho

> AGENT

Spearman ρ (rho) is a rank correlation — it measures whether two quantities have a consistent monotonic relationship, without assuming linearity. **In this paper's context:** You have 9 datasets. Each has: - An R(D) value (computable from dataset statistics alone, no experiment needed) - A Δ% improvement from using RR-MoA Spearman ρ = -0.88 means: if you rank the 9 datasets by R(D) and rank them again by improvement, those rankings almost perfectly mirror each other. Datasets with higher R(D) consistently get bigger improvements. The negative sign is because higher R(D) → lower MSE (bigger improvement = more negative Δ%). **Why Spearman instead of Pearson?** Pearson measures linear correlation ("twice the R(D) = twice the improvement"). Spearman only asks "does the ordering hold?" — which is the right question here since R(D) is a proxy, not an exact predictor. You're claiming "R(D) tells you *which* datasets benefit more," not "R(D) predicts the exact percentage." **ρ = -0.88 with n=9 and p < 0.002** is strong evidence given only 9 data points. And the boundary cases make it convincing: Traffic (R=0.14) correctly predicted as "won't help," while Exchange (R=2.17) correctly predicted as "will help a lot."
