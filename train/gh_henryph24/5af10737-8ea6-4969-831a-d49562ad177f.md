We identify a failure mode of this upgrade under instance normalization: once any backbone layer is unfrozen, the router converges to a one-hot assignment, routing entropy collapses to $0.000$, and only one expert receives gradients. We name this \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}: instance-normalization layers (e.g., RevIN) strip the per-window mean and variance the router needs. A $720$-run sweep across standard MoE rescue mechanisms (load balancing, z-loss, entropy regularization, ReLU routing, expert-choice) recovers at most $10.9\%$ MSE — $2.7\times$ worse than our causal intervention, because the failure is in the router's input, not its optimization. We formalize this via a mutual-information decomposition; the resulting signal-ratio statistic, computable before training, predicts dataset vulnerability (Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.88$, $p{<}0.002$) and flags boundary cases. The fix is one line: \emph{Raw-Routed Mixture of Adapters} (RR-MoA) routes on the raw, pre-normalization input. RR-MoA sets a new SOTA for adapter-based TSFM transfer under a strictly frozen backbone: $54/54$ wins on the primary backbone ($26$--$79\%$ MSE improvement over the best fixed adapter), strictly beats LoRA, TRACE, AdaMix, and full fine-tuning at $p{<}0.001$, and generalizes across four further backbones and an imputation task.
