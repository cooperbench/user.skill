391	
392	\textbf{Backbones.} Five backbones spanning three normalization regimes: (i)~learnable-affine RevIN-inside-encoder MOMENT-small/large~\citep{goswami2024moment} (internal RevIN~\citep{kim2021revin}); (ii)~Moirai~\citep{woo2024moirai} and Moirai-MoE, which use RMSNorm internally with only non-learnable per-instance I/O scaling (no learnable-affine RevIN inside the encoder pipeline); (iii)~Chronos~\citep{ansari2024chronos} (T5-style, no instance normalization) and Timer-XL~\citep{liu2025timerxl} (LayerNorm only, no instance normalization) as no-instance-normalization negative controls (per-backbone normalization specifications in Appendix~\ref{app:setup}, \S\ref{sec:cross_backbone}). The collapse mechanism predicts that only regime~(i) is vulnerable: routing signal is destroyed by RevIN's learnable-affine normalization upstream of the encoder, whereas non-learnable I/O scaling preserves the (M,$\Sigma$) statistics in the hidden states the router reads. MOMENT-small is primary. Primary results use a strictly frozen backbone; freeze-level ablations follow.
393

474	{\looseness=-1 \textbf{The Frozen Paradox.} The trajectory and causal-contrast figures formalize into a sharp prediction: if normalization-driven gradient co-adaptation is the collapse mechanism, then within a fixed MoE architecture, frozen should match or beat unfrozen. Empirically, frozen RR-MoA beats \emph{full fine-tuning} (all 8 blocks unfrozen, best of 5 heads $\times$ 2 LRs) by $12$--$79\%$ on all 6 LTSF datasets (Figure~\ref{fig:frozen_paradox}; the three-dataset subset in Table~\ref{tab:baselines} is consistent). The two-factor headline gap (frozen+MoE vs.\ unfrozen+single-head) decomposes into:
475	\emph{(M1) MoE architectural advantage (dominant, $26$--$79\%$):} the best fixed adapter loses to RR-MoA \emph{at the same freeze level} (Table~\ref{tab:rrmoa}), isolating M1 from M2.
476	\emph{(M2) Within-architecture freeze effect (smaller, $\leq 13\%$, non-unidirectional):} within RR-MoA itself, frozen is best on $4/6$ datasets; light unfreezing wins by up to $13\%$ on the other two; SR-MoA independently replicates the $4/6$ pattern (Table~\ref{tab:self_routed}). Proposition~\ref{prop:frozen} attributes M2 to gradient co-adaptation: the Router-Detached Gradient Flow ablation (unfreezes backbone but cuts routing-weighted feedback) degrades MSE by $+35$--$100\%$ on three datasets (Appendix~\ref{app:routing_ablations}), and disabling RevIN inside MOMENT recovers full-FT by $54$--$61\%$ (Appendix~\ref{app:extended_ft}), pinning the mechanism on normalization rather than optimization. A $90$-config hyperparameter tune (LR $10^{-5}$--$10^{-6}$, $100$ epochs, cosine + warmup, layerwise decay, batch up to $512$) still loses by $51$--$71\%$, consistent with optimization not being the bottleneck within the sweep we ran.}

478	\begin{proposition}[Gradient Co-Adaptation Drives Entropy Collapse]
479	\label{prop:frozen}
480	Consider a $2$-expert MoE with backbone $\mathbf{h}{=}A\mathbf{x}$, router $p{=}\sigma(\mathbf{w}^\top\mathbf{h})$, loss $\ell{=}p\,\ell_1{+}(1{-}p)\,\ell_2$. Under gradient descent on $(A,\mathbf{w})$: \textup{(i)} the dominant expert's loss decreases faster ($\dot{\ell}_k \propto {-}p_k\|\nabla_\mathbf{h}\ell_k\|^2$), and $\dot{p}$ amplifies whichever expert currently has lower loss, creating self-reinforcing collapse toward $p{\in}\{0,1\}$; \textup{(ii)} freezing $A$ removes the co-adaptation feedback through the backbone (full non-collapse additionally requires $(\ell_1{-}\ell_2)$ to flip sign across input regimes, i.e., expert specialization, which is satisfied by the topologically distinct RR-MoA pool). Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
481	\end{proposition}

548	\textbf{Why RevIN destroys routing.} Each window decomposes into mean $M_i$, scale $\Sigma_i$, shape $\mathbf{S}_i = (\mathbf{x}_i - M_i)/\Sigma_i$; RevIN keeps $\mathbf{S}_i$ and discards $(M_i,\Sigma_i)$. The chain rule quantifies how much routing signal lives in the discarded part.
549	
550	\begin{observation}[Routing Information Loss Under Instance Normalization]
551	\label{thm:mi_decomp}
552	Let $X$ be a random input window, $E \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ the expert assignment produced by any deterministic router, $S = (X - M)/\Sigma$ the RevIN-normalized shape. \emph{(i) Exact decomposition:} $I(X;E) - I(S;E) = I(M,\Sigma;E\mid S) \geq 0$. \emph{(ii) Quantitative bound (no independence assumption):} $I(X;E) - I(S;E) \geq I(M,\Sigma;E) - I(M,\Sigma;S)$, with $\varepsilon := I(M,\Sigma;S) \geq 0$ capturing residual leakage of stripped statistics through the shape; equality iff $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid E$.
553	\end{observation}
554	
555	When routing signal lives entirely in the stripped statistics ($(M,\Sigma)\perp\!\!\!\perp S$; Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), $I(X;E){-}I(S;E){=}H(E)$: RevIN destroys the entire routing distribution. The signal ratio $R(\mathcal{D}) = [\mathrm{Var}(M)+\mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)]/\overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)}$ is a tractable monotone proxy; the bound is empirically tight (Appendix~\ref{app:mi_tightness}) and learnable $\alpha{<}0.5$ on $30/30$ runs (Appendix~\ref{app:learnable_alpha}). $R(\mathcal{D})$ predicts raw-routing benefit at Spearman $\rho{=}{-}0.88$ ($p{<}0.002$, $n{=}9$; Figure~\ref{fig:signal_ratio}), whereas $H(E\mid S)$ alone does not ($\rho{=}{-}0.12$); Traffic ($R{=}0.14$) is flagged a boundary, Solar ($R{=}0.06$) the one outlier (Appendix~\ref{app:routing_ablations}).

560	\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
561	\vspace{-0.4em}
562	
563	\textbf{Diagnosis and fix.} We identified \emph{normalization-induced routing collapse}: MoE adapters on instance-normalized TSFMs collapse to a single expert because the same per-window normalization handling distribution shift also strips the $(\mu,\sigma)$ statistics the router needs. Observation~\ref{thm:mi_decomp} formalizes the loss as a mutual-information gap, the signal-ratio proxy $R(\mathcal{D})$ predicts it at $\rho{=}{-}0.88$ ($p{<}0.002$), and eight causal controls (Table~\ref{tab:causal_controls}) jointly rule out optimization, geometry, and routing-paradigm explanations. Three architecturally distinct interventions confirm the diagnosis by giving the router or the experts raw pre-normalization access: RR-MoA wins $54/54$ cells ($26$--$79\%$); SR-MoA drops the external router entirely and beats RR-MoA by $13$--$42\%$; Residual-IA\textsuperscript{+} extends the fix to the expert level and closes the DLinear gap on $107/123$ cross-backbone cells. The mechanism replicates on ResNet/ViT MoE with InstanceNorm (Appendix~\ref{app:vision_moe}) and is absent from LayerNorm-only Timer-XL ($4$--$9\%$, $15$--$100{\times}$ smaller), so normalization \emph{type}, not modality or backbone, is the relevant axis.
564	
565	\textbf{Limitations and outlook.} The diagnosis is specific to per-window normalizers that strip $(\mu,\sigma)$ (RevIN, BatchNorm1d, GroupNorm, InstanceNorm); LayerNorm- and RMSNorm-only backbones need no fix. $R(\mathcal{D})$ is a monotone proxy, not a sharp threshold: it flags Traffic ($R{=}0.14$) correctly, but which intermediate $R$ benefits at which freeze level is open. Evaluation covers forecasting and imputation; classification and anomaly detection remain. The fix needs raw input at routing time, available in adapter-style finetuning but not in purely API-served models; our pool is also fixed at five hand-coded experts (learned pools are open). $R(\mathcal{D})$ itself is portable: any MoE-adapter on a normalized backbone can compute it pre-training, and surfacing this \emph{upstream architectural} cause widens a design space that downstream rescues have so far blocked.\label{page:end_main}
