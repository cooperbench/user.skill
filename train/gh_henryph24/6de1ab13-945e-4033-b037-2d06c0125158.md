493	\textbf{Three architectural instantiations of one principle.} The diagnosis says: restore raw-input access wherever normalization destroyed it. The paper proposes three variants, all confirming the same diagnosis from different angles. \emph{(i)}~\textbf{RR-MoA} (\S\ref{sec:main_results}, Figure~\ref{fig:framework}, headline contribution): external Conv1d gate reads $\mathbf{X}_\mathrm{raw}$ and selects Top-K experts; experts read hidden states $\mathbf{H}$. \emph{(ii)}~\textbf{SR-MoA} (Self-Routed MoA, Figure~\ref{fig:srmoa_arch}, architecture-agnostic validation): no external router, each expert has its own sigmoid gate on $\mathbf{X}_\mathrm{raw}$. \emph{(iii)}~\textbf{Residual-IA\textsuperscript{+}} (Appendix~\ref{app:gate_pathology}, Figure~\ref{fig:residual_ia_arch}, diagnosis-actionability proof): each expert combines a shared NLinear raw-input branch with a gated backbone residual, which closes the DLinear gap.

That a different architecture exceeds RR-MoA while sharing only the raw-input principle confirms it is the \emph{principle}, not the specific router design, that drives the gains. 521	{\looseness=-1 \textbf{Why the diagnosis matters more than the architectural label.} The MoE community has framed routing collapse as both an \emph{optimization} problem (load balancing, z-loss all attack training dynamics; effective when router input is informative but under-optimized) and a \emph{post-training representation} problem~\citep{chi2022representation,hua2025inputaware}, where token features cluster around expert centroids. Optimization-side rescue mechanisms cannot recover what is structurally absent (Table~\ref{tab:rescue}), and post-training representation fixes presuppose information the input never carried. The mechanism decomposes into two distinct gains: \emph{(Q1)} the best fixed adapter on the same RevIN-normalized hidden states still loses to RR-MoA by $26$--$79\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:rrmoa}), so residual shape heterogeneity survives normalization and rewards diverse experts; \emph{(Q2)} the signal-ratio $\rho{=}{-}0.88$ measures \emph{dispatch}-signal localization in the stripped statistics, which is why dense routing also benefits from raw access and why Traffic ($R{=}0.14$) correctly does not improve. The durable contribution is the diagnosis, not the architectural label; the choice between ``MoE with raw router'' and ``ensemble with raw input'' is dataset-conditional, we claim only that wherever instance normalization sits between input and router, the routing signal needs to be re-injected from upstream.}

558	\textbf{Why RevIN destroys routing: formal analysis.} The experiments above converge on a single mechanism: instance normalization strips the per-window statistics that the router uses to tell input regimes apart. To pin this down, we ask what information the router actually has access to before and after normalization. Each input window $\mathbf{x}_i$ decomposes into a mean $M_i = \mu(\mathbf{x}_i)$, a scale $\Sigma_i = \sigma(\mathbf{x}_i)$, and a shape $\mathbf{S}_i = (\mathbf{x}_i - M_i)/\Sigma_i$. RevIN maps $\mathbf{x}_i \mapsto \mathbf{S}_i$ and discards $(M_i, \Sigma_i)$, so the question reduces to how much routing information lives in the discarded part. The mutual-information chain rule gives a clean answer.
559	
560	\begin{observation}[Routing Information Loss Under Instance Normalization]
561	\label{thm:mi_decomp}
562	Let $X$ be a random input window, $E \in \{1,\ldots,K\}$ the expert assignment produced by any deterministic router, and $S = (X - M)/\Sigma$ the RevIN-normalized shape.
563	\begin{enumerate}
564	    \item[\textup{(i)}] \textbf{Exact decomposition.} $I(X; E) - I(S; E) = I(M, \Sigma; E \mid S) \geq 0$.
565	    \item[\textup{(ii)}] \textbf{Quantitative bound.} Without any independence assumption,
566	    \[
567	        I(X; E) - I(S; E) \;\geq\; I(M, \Sigma;\, E) \;-\; I(M, \Sigma;\, S),
568	    \]
569	    where $\varepsilon := I(M, \Sigma;\, S) \geq 0$ captures any residual ``leakage'' of the stripped statistics through the shape. Equality holds iff $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S \mid E$.
570	\end{enumerate}
571	\end{observation}
572	
573	In the full-information corner case where the router's signal lives entirely in the stripped statistics ($E$ is a deterministic function of $(M,\Sigma)$ and $(M,\Sigma) \perp\!\!\!\perp S$ unconditionally; see Part~(iii) of the proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}), $I(X; E) - I(S; E) = H(E)$: RevIN destroys the entire routing distribution. We therefore use the \emph{signal ratio} $R(\mathcal{D}) = [\mathrm{Var}(M) + \mathrm{Var}(\Sigma)] / \overline{\mathrm{Var}(S)}$ as a tractable monotone proxy for the variance contribution of the stripped statistics. The bound is empirically tight: CCA gives $\varepsilon \in [0.09, 1.26]$ nats, cross-validated within ${\pm}0.26$ nats by KSG~\citep{kraskov2004ksg}; a held-out classifier shows normalization retains only $16$--$41\%$ of routing entropy with bound (ii) gap averaging $0.075$ nats (Appendix~\ref{app:mi_tightness}). ## Frozen Paradox: causally proven? — Honest assessment

**Mostly yes for the *mechanism*, partially no for the *headline number*.** The 12–79% gap conflates two interventions and the paper itself acknowledges this on main.tex:431.

### What IS causally established

1. **Co-adaptation collapse mechanism** (Proposition 2 at main.tex:434, proof at main.tex:1176–1203):
   - Linear-MoE proof: unfreezing creates the runaway loop; freezing kills the $A$-mediated feedback term
   - **RDGF counterfactual** (main.tex:1532): unfreezes the backbone but replaces routing-weighted gradients with uniform-average gradients → MSE degrades +35–100%. This is a clean "knock-out" of the gradient-feedback term. *Caveat: only 3 datasets, no significance reported.*
   - **AdaMix trajectory** (Fig 3): unfrozen+RevIN collapses in ~50 steps; frozen control stays stable on the same code/data
   - **MOMENT vs Timer-XL contrast** (Fig 4): same code, opposite trajectories — only RevIN differs
   - **RevIN-disabled full-FT** (main.tex:2067): full-FT recovers to 0.490/0.364/0.187 (-54% to -61%), pinning RevIN as the cause of full-FT's underperformance

2. **Replication across architectures**: SR-MoA (different router) independently shows frozen-best on 4/6 (main.tex:1483)

3. **Optimization isn't a confound**: 90-config sweep (main.tex:2065) — full-FT still loses 51–71%

### What is NOT cleanly causal — and the paper admits it

The "12–79%" is **frozen-RR-MoA-Top-2 vs. unfrozen-single-head**, which bundles two effects:
- **(a) MoE architectural advantage** (5 experts + raw router): "best fixed adapter loses by 26–79% even at frozen" (tab:rrmoa). This is the dominant component of 12–79%.
- **(b) Freeze advantage within the same architecture**: main.tex:431 explicitly says *"Within RR-MoA, frozen is best on 4/6 datasets; light unfreezing wins by up to 13% on the other two."* — a much smaller and non-unidirectional effect.

So the 12–79% headline is a **two-factor contrast**, not a clean causal isolation of "freezing > unfreezing." A direct unfrozen-RR-MoA vs frozen-RR-MoA test across all 6 datasets would isolate (b); the paper has this in tab:rrmoa but the gap there is ≤13% with split direction.

### Verdict

- **"Unfreezing causes routing collapse via gradient co-adaptation under instance normalization"** → causally proven (theory + RDGF + RevIN ablation + trajectory + cross-backbone contrast).
- **"Frozen adapters beat full FT by 12–79%"** → empirically robust but the *attribution* is split between MoE-architecture and freeze-state effects. The paper's framing is honest about this duality (see main.tex:431); a skeptical reviewer could push on whether the "Paradox" branding overstates a result whose dominant driver is architecture, not freezing.

**Risk for review**: a reviewer noticing tab:rrmoa's 4/6-vs-2/6 split could argue the Paradox name is half-misleading. The current text mitigates this with "two distinct mechanisms contribute" — but that mitigation is one sentence and easy to miss.
