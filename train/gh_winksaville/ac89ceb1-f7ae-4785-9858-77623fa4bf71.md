Fascinating flip — the pinned run reverses the dev5 narrative:

| run                       | tp-pc producer min-p99 | tp2-pc producer min-p99 | winner |
|:--------------------------|-----------------------:|------------------------:|:-------|
| unpinned, -d 60           | 4,559 ns               | 3,536 ns                | tp2-pc ~22% ahead |
| `--pin 5,6,7`, -d 15      | 8,258 ns               | 8,468 ns                | tp-pc ~2.5% ahead |

**Why the flip, hypothesis:** on a 3900X, `--pin 5,6,7` straddles a CCD boundary (core 5 is CCD 0 / CCX 1; cores 6–7 are CCD 1 / CCX 2). Every channel round-trip crosses the Infinity Fabric — hence per-iter blowing up from 4.6 µs to 8.3 µs. But pinning also means the threads *stay* on their cores, so the histogram for `tp-pc` lives hot in L1/L2 continuously — `hist.record` becomes a cheap L1-hit + atomic-inc. When unpinned, the scheduler migrates threads, histogram state goes cold on each migration, and `hist.record` pays cache-miss cost. `tp2-pc`'s `Vec::push` is a streaming-write pattern that's indifferent to cache warmth (DRAM bandwidth is always available), so it wins the unpinned regime but doesn't *gain* anything in the pinned one.

In short:
- **Scope API wins when the fast-path's bucket state can't stay hot** (unpinned, migration-heavy, or very high allocation churn).
- **Fast path wins when the histogram stays L1/L2-resident** (pinned, steady thread-to-core mapping).

This is a much more interesting characterization than either dev4 ("scope slower") or dev5 ("scope faster").
