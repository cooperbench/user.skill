## Why sibling, not a mode (agreeing with you, with teeth)

- The SPSC hot path is exactly **one relaxed load + one acquire load + one release store** per side, no CAS, no per-slot state. That's what buys the 1.9 ns adjusted 1t round-trip you just pasted. Any unified type (runtime mode flag, generic over "claim strategy") puts at least a branch — realistically a per-slot sequence check — into that path.
- Separate types also keep the **atomic floor** story clean: the SPSC protocol stays load/store-only (thumbv6m works today); MPSC needs CAS, so its module gets gated (`cfg(target_has_atomic = "32")`) and only its users pay. Same precedent as the pool's Treiber free-stack — CAS entered the crate there without raising the ring's floor.
- And it gives you the A/B you want: same workload, same harness, two primitives.

## Protocol sketch (Vyukov-style, restricted to MP+SC)

- **Per-slot sequence array** between header and slots (`M × AtomicU32`). Slot at position `pos` is claimable when `seq == pos`, committed when `seq == pos + 1`, released by the consumer storing `seq = pos + M`.
- **Producers CAS-claim `producer_idx`**: load seq at `p & mask`; if ready, `compare_exchange_weak(p, p+1)`; on success the slot is exclusively yours — write payload, then `seq.store(p + 1, Release)` publishes it. Claim order ≠ commit order is exactly why the per-slot seq exists.
- **Consumer stays load/store** on its side: single consumer means its index is private — wait for `seq == c + 1` (Acquire), read, store `seq = c + M` (Release), advance. No CAS on the consume path.
- **Producers never read `consumer_idx`** — fullness falls out of the slot's seq (`seq < p` ⇒ still unreleased ⇒ full). The header keeps a consumer_idx line only for occupancy/diagnostics, if at all.
- **u32 free-running + power-of-two M** carries over unchanged; wrap math is the same.
- **Own magic + layout_version** — a region is one kind or the other; cross-attach fails toward `BadMagic`, no shared-layout entanglement.
- **Trust:** seq words are shared memory ⇒ untrusted. Positions are always masked, so a hostile peer scribbling seqs yields spurious Full/Empty or garbage-but-valid `T`s — the established degrade-never-UB posture holds. Needs its own written analysis in the design doc.

## The one genuinely new API problem: abandoned claims

In SPSC, dropping a `WriteSlot` without commit is free — `producer_idx` never moved. In MPSC, a claimed-but-never-committed index **wedges the queue**: the consumer waits at that position forever. Two candidate shapes:

1. **Closure send** — `producer.send_with::<T>(on_full, |msg: &mut T| …)`: commit happens by construction on closure return; abandonment is unrepresentable. Cost: panic-in-closure needs an answer (tombstone on unwind, or document abort).
2. **Guard + tombstone** — keep the `reserve/commit` guard mirror; `Drop` without commit stores a skip marker (e.g. a reserved seq bit) the consumer releases without delivering.

My lean: **closure send as the primary API, tombstone as the unwind path** — you need the tombstone machinery for panic safety anyway, so the guard form could even come later if wanted. Symmetry with SPSC's guard is nice but not worth a footgun.

Good question to poke at — but first, what the mean actually averages: in that harness each `step()` is one **complete round-trip** (send request into ring A → worker echoes into ring B → receive reply), with `inner=1` meaning every step is timed individually. The 73.9 ns is the mean over the ~2.34 B round-trip samples (adjusted = minus the 11.61 ns timing framing). There is exactly **one message in flight at any moment** — strict ping-pong.

And on topology: `zcr-with-2t` and `zcr-mpsc-2t` are deliberately identical shapes — **both** use two rings (request + response), one producer and one consumer per ring. So there's no "two streams" concurrency in the mpsc run that the spsc run lacks; nothing overlaps in either. Whatever the 26 ns is, it's per-handoff protocol behavior, not stream parallelism.

The mechanism we pointed at in the commit body (marked as a guess there too): count the cross-core cache lines in one handoff's critical path —

- **SPSC**: the producer must *load* `consumer_idx` (fullness check) — a line the consumer writes; the consumer spins *loading* `producer_idx` — a line the producer writes. Two distinct index lines bouncing between the cores, each written by one side and polled by the other, every message.
- **MPSC**: the producer's CAS on `producer_idx` is **core-local** here (it's the only producer, and the consumer never reads that line); `consumer_idx` is never read by the producer. The only shared hot word is the slot's **seq** — one seam line instead of two index lines. (With capacity 8 and packed 4-byte seqs, all eight seq words even share a single cache line.)

Your instinct that it's "less spinning" may still be right in a narrower sense — the consumer spins on the seq word, which is also exactly the word the producer stores, whereas the SPSC consumer's spin and the producer's fullness check hit *different* lines and both must make the trip. The mpsc distribution is consistent with something interesting: 1% of round-trips complete in ~18 ns adjusted and p10 is 35.8, while with-2t never dips below ~47 — mpsc sometimes catches a much cheaper handoff, but with more variance (p90 89.9, heavier far tail).
