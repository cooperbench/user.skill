Here are my thoughts on `-R` vs `cd`:

**`-R /path/to/repo` (aka `--repository`):**
- **Pro:** Single command, no directory change side effects, cleaner and more declarative — you state *which* repo you mean right in the command.
- **Pro:** No risk of accidentally staying in the wrong directory for subsequent commands.
- **Pro:** Works well in scripts and one-liners.
- **Con:** Slightly more verbose per command, but barely.

**`cd /path && jj ...`:**
- **Pro:** Familiar shell pattern.
- **Con:** The `cd` persists across Bash tool calls in this environment, which can lead to subtle bugs (I actually hit this earlier — my `jj log` accidentally ran from `.claude` because a prior `cd` stuck).
- **Con:** Two-step means more room for error.

**Verdict:** `-R` is clearly better here. It's explicit, stateless, and avoids the directory-persistence foot-gun. Let me update the CLAUDE.md accordingly.
