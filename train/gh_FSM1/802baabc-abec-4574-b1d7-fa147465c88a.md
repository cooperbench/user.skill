> AGENT

Good thing to pin down, since the whole tamper-resistance story rests on it. **AEAD — Authenticated Encryption with Associated Data.** A cipher that does two jobs in one operation: (1) **confidentiality** — encrypts the plaintext; (2) **integrity/authenticity** — emits an authentication *tag* so any tampering with the ciphertext is detected at decryption time and the operation *fails closed* (throws) rather than returning garbage. CipherBox's cipher is **AES-256-GCM**, which is AEAD. In the docs, **"seal"** = the AEAD-encrypt op (`sealAesGcm(plaintext, key) → IV ‖ ciphertext ‖ tag`) and **"unseal"** = AEAD-decrypt-and-verify. So "the read-body is *sealed* under `readKey`" means it's AES-256-GCM-encrypted with that key, with an integrity tag. **AAD — Associated (Additional) Authenticated Data.** This is the "AD" in AEAD: extra bytes you feed the cipher that are **authenticated but not encrypted**. They don't appear in the ciphertext, but they're folded into the tag — so if the AAD presented at *unseal* time differs by even one byte from the AAD used at *seal* time, the tag check fails and decryption is rejected. AAD is **not secret** (the values are already public in the plaintext envelope); its job is to cryptographically *bind a ciphertext to a context*. **Why the design needs it — the transplant attack.** AES-GCM without AAD still guarantees the ciphertext itself wasn't modified.
