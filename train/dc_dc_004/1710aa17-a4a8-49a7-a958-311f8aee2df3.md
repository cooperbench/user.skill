OK so here's what's happening. The full picture:

**The attestation system** is an anti-gaming friction mechanism. When an agent resolves a finding, it must include `--attest` with text containing two required phrases: `"I have actually"` and `"not gaming"`.

The template is:
```
I have actually improved [WHAT YOU IMPROVED EXPLICITLY] enough to honestly 
justify a score of [SCORE], and I am not gaming the score.
```

**The problem you're seeing**: the attestation is about **justifying a score** rather than **confirming you fixed the thing**. That's weird because:

1. Scores are computed automatically from state — you don't "justify" a score, it's a calculation
2. The `[SCORE]` placeholder is meaningless — what score? The agent has no idea what number to put there
3. The framing is "I deserve this score" when it should be "I actually did this work"
4. It's convoluted enough that agents just template-fill it without thinking about what they're attesting to

The required keywords (`"I have actually"` + `"not gaming"`) are fine as friction — they force the agent to at least acknowledge it's supposed to have done real work. But the example template is pointing at the wrong thing.

Here's what I'd change — make it about confirming the fix, not justifying a score:
