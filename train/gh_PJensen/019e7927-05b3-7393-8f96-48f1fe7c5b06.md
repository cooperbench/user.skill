For aggro VFX, I’d separate three concepts because they communicate different things: 1. **This creature is hostile / alerted** 2. **This creature is targeting someone specific** 3. **This relationship matters even when neither is the player** **Creature Has Aggro** Use a compact, readable state marker on the creature itself. Good options: - **Threat ring**: a thin red/orange ring or bracket around the creature’s tile. - **Pulse**: brief red pulse when aggro is acquired, then settles into a subtle idle marker. - **Eye / exclamation glyph**: good for first acquisition, less good as a persistent state unless very small. - **Facing emphasis**: sharpen or brighten the creature’s facing indicator when it is actively pursuing. - **Animation cadence**: slight “angry” jitter or sharper bobbing, but keep it subtle to avoid visual noise. My preference: **one-shot alert burst + persistent threat ring**. The burst says “state changed”; the ring says “still dangerous.” **Aggro On Another Creature** This needs a relationship indicator, not just a status indicator. Best options: - **Target tether**: a faint line, arc, or dotted ray from aggressor to target. - **Directional chevrons**: tiny arrows near the aggressor pointing toward the target. - **Target reticle**: small mark over the target, colored by source faction/threat. - **Shared color pair**: aggressor ring and target marker use the same accent color for a short time. I’d avoid permanent full lines for every aggro relationship; they’ll clutter fast. Instead: - Show the tether **briefly when aggro changes**. - Show it **on hover/inspect**. - Show it **when the player is watching an NPC fight nearby**. - Show persistent relation only for high-importance cases: boss targeting, pet threatened, ally under attack, etc. **NPC Creature Aggro Relationship** For NPC-vs-NPC aggro, the question is: does the player need to act on this? Muted tethers are probably the strongest fit for JSHack because they communicate simulation without turning every NPC fight into UI noise. I’d treat them as **relationship VFX**, not permanent HUD state: - **Spawn condition**: when `AggroState.targetId` changes, or when an NPC first enters combat. - **Duration**: short, maybe `350-700ms`. - **Shape**: curved or slightly sagging line from aggressor to target, not a hard laser. - **Style**: dotted, smoky, or low-alpha segmented line. - **Color**: derived from aggressor faction, but desaturated and dimmed. - **Priority**: - player target: bright, hot, persistent-ish - ally/pet target: amber warning - NPC-vs-NPC: muted, short-lived - **Repeat suppression**: don’t replay every turn while the same target remains selected. For the visual language, I’d make NPC tethers feel like “intent passing through the air” rather than “attack beam.” Something like a brief ember-red or faction-tinted thread that appears, bends toward the target, then dissolves. Implementation-wise, I’d expose aggro relationship changes through the bridge/event path, then let display own the particle/line effect. Something like: ```js world.emit('aggro:targetChanged', { sourceId, targetId, previousTargetId, sourceFaction, targetFaction, targetIsPlayer, targetIsAlly, }); ``` Then display maps that into: ```js if (targetIsPlayer) showHotThreatTether(...) else if (targetIsAlly) showAmberDangerTether(...) else showMutedFactionTether(...) ``` The key is that the persistent state stays subtle, while **changes in intention get animated**. That should make NPC combat legible without making the whole dungeon look like a targeting debugger.
