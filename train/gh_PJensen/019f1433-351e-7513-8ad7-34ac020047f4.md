The expensive/restrictive part is here: [townPlacement.js](/home/pjensen/Repos/JSHack/src/rules/environment/dungeon/townPlacement.js:418). For every building it scans outward, tries rotations, and rejects any footprint tile where `occupied` is set or `!canBuildOn(...)`. `canBuildOn` only allows `NATURAL_BUILDABLE`, so a whole building fails because one authored tile lands on water, mountain, etc. Then it does a separate spawn landing check. The better shape is probably: - Keep hard constraints: no overlap with existing building footprint/margin, stay inside bounds, keep authored spawn points sane, preserve door/path reachability. - Relax terrain constraints: allow stamping over most non-structure terrain, with a hard reject only for deep/big water under the building’s structural footprint. - Before/after `stampBuilding`, carve a small perimeter pad around the accepted footprint: grass/cobble for adjacent natural/wet-edge/mud/forest tiles, maybe do not overwrite deep water unless the footprint itself was accepted. - Keep `wantsScore` as a preference, not a gate. The water/mountain/forest/flat wants are good for flavor, but they should not be the reason startup spirals into repeated placement failure. One caveat: “not in the middle of water” should be defined per authored tile class. Walls/floors/doors should not land on water; fences/farmland/yard grass could safely normalize nearby terrain. Since `stampBuilding` already overwrites tiles directly ([stampBuilding.js](/home/pjensen/Repos/JSHack/src/rules/environment/dungeon/stampBuilding.js:67)), adding a `prepareBuildingPad(...)` around the chosen footprint is very natural. So the thesis is right: stop treating town generation as perfect terrain fitting. Let buildings be a little brutal, then repair the local terrain around them. The constraints worth preserving are topology and gameplay invariants, not biome purity.
